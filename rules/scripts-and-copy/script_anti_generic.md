Every content script goes through an anti-generic pass. Full rules live in
`$WSM_HOME/content/scripts/SCRIPT-RULES.md` — read it before writing.
It layers on top of project_retention_script_framework (framework = shape, this = language).

**Banned outright:** crowd claims ("most people don't realise"), fake contrast ("it's not X, it's Y"),
hype openers ("here's the thing", "let's dive in"), rhetorical questions ("what if I told you"),
abstract filler (mindset, journey, unlock, level up, leverage, elevate), neat triplets, cliché
closers ("let that sink in"), em dashes as a crutch. Plus the standing "quick 15" ban.

**The rules:** every claim needs a concrete anchor (number, name, place, date, first-hand moment);
never invent a specific — write `[NEED: …]` instead; write for the ear; the hook is a specific
moment or claim, never a question on the page; one idea per script.

**Self-audit before showing him anything:** line by line, "could this appear in anyone else's
script?" — if yes, rewrite with a specific or cut. Then scan for every banned pattern. Do it
silently.

**Output:** script with `[VISUAL]` cues, the list of `[NEED:]` gaps he must fill, two alternative
hooks. No preamble, no explaining choices unless asked. Missing angle / audience / length / raw
material → ask up to 3 questions first.

**Why:** he rejected a brand-intro draft with "youre script just screams AI" — the tells were
epigram density, two "not X, it's Y" flips, and zero concrete detail.
