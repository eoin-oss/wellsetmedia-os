# Pacing research: talking-head seminar reels (Kyle)

Researched 2026-10-17. **Source caveat:** no credited editor for Hormozi, Gadzhi, Koe, Abdaal or DOAC has published timed specs. Most numbers below come from tool-vendor guides plus a few real editing references (Murch, a university guide, a DOAC teardown). Treat them as starting values, then tune against the editor's notes, which take priority.

## 1. Opening, 0-5s
- **Stay on the face for 1.5s, then cut away.** This comes from the editor's note. No source contradicts it. For a talking head, the face, the first words and the hook text have to carry the first frame. Start mid-sentence or mid-gesture. https://jupitrr.com/how-to/edit-talking-head-videos
- **Make the hook raise a concrete question in the first 1-3s.** https://skills.sh/postplusai/postplus-skills/visual-hook
- **Allow 2-3 visual changes in the first 5s:** face, then b-roll at about 1.5s, then back to face or a second b-roll at about 3-4s. Shots are shorter in the hook (about 1-1.5s) than in the body (2-4s). This is derived from the rules below, not measured.

## 2. Body rhythm and b-roll share
- **A visual change every 3-5s** (one vendor). https://subscribr.ai/p/editing-talking-head-videos-engaging
- **⚠ Sources disagree.** The other figure is 3-6 cutaways of 2-4s each per 60s, about 15-35% b-roll. https://jupitrr.com/how-to/add-b-roll-to-talking-head-videos (via search summary)
- **Our setting:** keep b-roll at **30-45% of runtime** and **1.5-3s per shot**. Change the visual every 3-5s by mixing b-roll with returns to the face. Never run 3 or more b-roll shots back to back without a return to the face.
- **Anchor:** dialogue in feature films averages about 6 cuts/min, action about 25 (Murch). A short-form reel sits in between at roughly 12-20 visual changes/min. https://musicbed.com/blog/filmmaking/editing/editing-secrets-from-legendary-editor-walter-murch
- **Stay on the face** for emotional lines, personal stories, the core claim and the payoff. Face time builds trust, so b-roll should punctuate the talk, not dominate it. For variety on an emotional beat, use a punch-in instead of a cutaway. https://jupitrr.com/how-to/add-b-roll-to-talking-head-videos
- **Don't over-tighten.** Cut silences longer than about 0.5s, but keep the breath pauses between ideas, or the pace feels rushed and unnatural. https://viberoll.questera.ai/learn/talking-head-video-editing-checklist/

## 3. Intentional b-roll
- **Every cutaway needs a job:** prove a claim, explain an idea, add context, make a reference recognisable, or reset attention. If a shot does none of these, stay on the speaker. https://jupitrr.com/how-to/add-b-roll-to-talking-head-videos
- **Seminar footage hierarchy (our call, based on event practice):**
  1. Audience reaction on a punchline or claim. This is social proof that the room was engaged. https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/office-hours-video-capture.md
  2. Kyle's whiteboard while he explains a concept (a literal match).
  3. A wide shot of Kyle on stage while he describes the room.
  4. Venue and outdoor footage only at transitions or "journey" lines (a metaphorical match).
- **Prefer literal matches, and match the energy.** A calm line gets a slow shot. A punchy line gets a short, high-motion shot. Organise footage into bins like "best reaction", "best b-roll" and "best crowd sound". https://www.capcut.com/create/watch-party-recap-video-ai-editing-workflow
- **Flashback-style use of the speaker's own old footage** is a documented Iman Gadzhi move. https://www.opus.pro/pt-br/blog/how-to-edit-instagram-reels-exactly-like-iman-ghadzi

## 4. Hiding jump cuts
- **Cover each jump with b-roll or a cut to a different angle.** https://subscribr.ai/p/editing-talking-head-videos-engaging
- **Punch-in:** scale a slice to **110-115%** and land it exactly on an emphasised word's onset. Time it to sentence boundaries so it doesn't break a clause. https://firecut.ai/blog/how-to-zoom-in-on-premiere-pro-a-step-by-step-guide-for-beginners/ · https://www.tella.com/skills/add-zooms
- **DOAC** masks cuts with a sound effect, animated text, or a cut to a listener's reaction. https://amemotoeditors.beehiiv.com/p/4-ways-the-diary-of-a-ceo-hooks-their-audience
- **Priority order for us:** audience reaction or b-roll first, then a 110-115% punch-in, then (rarely) a hard cut on a full stop. Never sit a raw same-frame jump on the face.

## 5. Cut timing
- **Cut just before a word or sentence starts.** Never cut mid-breath. https://citl.illinois.edu/node/70
- **J/L split edits** run a few frames up to a few seconds. There's no fixed number, so judge by ear. Use them when moving between the face and b-roll. https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/a-video-editors-guide-to-j-cuts-and-l-cuts · https://cutback.video/blog/jump-cut-vs-j-cut-vs-l-cut-whats-the-difference
- **Our setting:** cut picture to b-roll **2-4 frames before** the word it illustrates. Return to the face on a phrase boundary. Audio always leads.
- **Cut on the blink or thought boundary** (Murch). https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/when-and-where-to-make-the-cut-inspired-by-walter-murchs-in-the-blink-of-an-eye/

## 6. Captions
- **Sizing for vertical:** text at 7-10% of frame height, no more than 35-42 characters per line, and no more than 20 characters/s on screen. Keep each block up 1.5-6s, ideally 2-4s. https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-make-captions-look-professional
- **Placement for vertical:** keep the bottom 20-35% clear of platform UI, and keep text inside 80-85% of the frame width (same source).
- **⚠ Sources disagree on outline vs shadow.** That source recommends a 3-4px stroke plus an optional soft shadow. The editor says no shadows or colours, and Eoin's rule is the modern font with a thin black outline. **Go with plain white, a thin outline, no shadow and no colour pops.**
- **What reads as cheap:** inconsistent styling, text too small, sitting in UI zones, and heavy animated word-by-word captions on brand content. A clean phrase-at-a-time caption reads more premium. https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-instagram-reels-2026 (via search summary)
- **For 16:9 delivery,** the vertical safe-zone numbers don't apply directly. Use the lower third, and never put text over the face.

## 7. Intro zoom and punch
- **Gadzhi's intro zoom** pushes in on the hook line, then returns to the original frame. A slow push suits a thesis line, and it should end on a cut to b-roll. https://www.opus.pro/pt-br/blog/how-to-edit-instagram-reels-exactly-like-iman-ghadzi
- **Smooth vs hard:** smooth pushes are for thesis lines. Hard punches are for single-word emphasis. https://www.tella.com/skills/add-zooms
- **Our setting:** a slow push from 100% to about 108% across the 1.5s face open, then cut to b-roll. Use at most 1 hard punch per 10-15s. Overuse (shakes, a swoosh every 2s) reads as a cheap Hormozi knock-off. https://marketing.riverside.com/blog/hormozi-style-videos (via search summary)
