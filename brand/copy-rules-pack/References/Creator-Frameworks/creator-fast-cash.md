## What Fast Cash Plays Are

Every business owner benefits from having a few fast cash plays in their back pocket. Fast Cash Plays are limited-time, premium offers advertised to your warmest audiences: current customers, previous customers, and all engaged leads who have given you explicit permission to contact them about what you sell.

Fast Cash Plays are different from nurturing new leads about your main offer. And different from "long term" value-adds like content emails used to keep your lists warm. They are more like winning lottery tickets you cash in once a quarter. You run a specific promotion to existing customers four times a year, on top of whatever else they are paying for. Your customers will not mind, at least if you do it the way outlined here.

**Origin story:** April 2019. A bunch of Gym Launch customers got hit with unexpected tax bills (they had never made this much money before, so they never really had to pay much in taxes). They were happy with Gym Launch but did not have the cash to pay both taxes and their subscription. [CREATOR_NAME] created a "grand opening promo" that he repurposed as a high-ticket offer. He told gyms to sell 5-10 people at $5,000 a pop. The average business collected $30,000 in extra cash in a few days. The ones who priced at $5,000 raked in $50k or more. All customers renewed and returned to good standing.

**Key principle:** "Revenue is vanity. Profit is sanity. Cash is reality." As long as you have cash, you are still in business. You can make it through all kinds of mess-ups if you have cash. And you can have the best business in the world, but if you have no cash, your days are numbered.

## How Fast Cash Plays Work

Fast Cash Plays are specifically designed to make the most money, in the shortest period possible, from the people most likely to buy it. They are limited in spots (scarcity), high touch (exclusivity), and sold for lots of money (big ticket), for a limited time (urgency).

They create tremendous profit for two reasons:
1. You have already acquired the leads you advertise to, so there is no additional cost to make the sale.
2. Bigger price tags tend to have much better margins.

**The single-offer trap:** If you only have a single fixed-price offer, you cap how much any customer can spend. By making other offers available, you allow people who want to spend money on your business to actually do it. And when they do, you make a lot more money. But this only happens if you have stuff for them to buy.

**1 out of 9 Americans is a millionaire.** Most rich people do not walk around with signs on their heads. They can definitely afford to buy more stuff so long as you provide the value to back it up.

## The 10x the 10% Rule

If you get 10% of your customers to pay 10x the price, you double your revenue. Since you already spent the money to get the customer, all the extra revenue (minus delivery) drops straight to the bottom line. Sometimes you can 2x, 3x, or even 4x revenue, just by getting one customer out of ten to buy a more expensive thing.

Business owners forget how fast ultra-premium prices add up to mountains of cash. Or they are scaredy cats. Worst case: you just do not sell that many. Fine. You still make money. You are no worse off.

To have a big-spending offer, you want to get the attention of the big-spending group. And to get the attention of the big-spending group, you limit the number of buyers. Price like you mean it.

## Core Math

Despite being ~15% of annual revenue, Fast Cash Plays can be nearly half of total profit because they require zero customer acquisition cost and carry 90%+ gross margins. In the early days, Fast Cash Plays doubled [CREATOR_NAME]'s take-home income. That was before he got good at them.

## Why Fast Cash Plays Matter

Broken equipment. Leaky pipes. New tech. Cyber attacks. Legal fees. An extra pay period in a thirty-day window. You should always expect an unexpected cash drain. You do not know where it will come from, but it will come. These plays in your back pocket come in handy.

The only thing better than making extra cash when you need it is making even more when you do not.

## Fast Cash Play Outline

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Audience** | Existing customers, past customers, engaged leads (warm only) |
| **Offer type** | Unscalable, high-touch, ultra-premium value |
| **Pricing** | 10x to 50x your average cart value |
| **Spots** | Cap at 5 to 10% of your customer base |
| **Promotion window** | 7 days or less (until spots run out) |
| **Frequency** | Once per quarter (every 90 days) |
| **Sales method** | Consults (spread appointments) or automated checkout (build hype, stampede) |

**Spot sizing:** If you have 100 customers, limit to 5-10. If you have 1,000 or more, fulfillment may constrain you before the 5-10% window. Fine. It means you will figure out how to do it better by the next run. The point is to sell out fast. This makes your Fast Cash offer even more compelling the next time you run it.

**If you have a handful of customers:** You already do lots of the unscalable stuff as your typical offer. Appeal to all of them privately and personally, expecting a few to bite.

## What to Sell (Premium Value Levers)

Think of all the most valuable things you can do that are "unscalable." The only thing that should shock them as much as the price is the value that goes with it. You will work more for these customers. You will earn that money.

| Lever | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Attention** | 1-on-1 attention from you or senior persons. Many-on-1 attention (a team of people supporting one customer). With a top expert, many-on-1 is even more valuable than 1-on-1. |
| **Personalization** | Personalized, customized versions of your standard solutions |
| **Convenience** | 24/7 support, extended hours, exclusive times |
| **Status** | Special parking, perks, badges, public recognition |
| **Duration** | Longer duration than standard (6 months to a year) |
| **Speed** | First in line, priority access, fast response promises (<10 min or less) |
| **Experience** | "White Glove" service, personal cell access, concierge line, nice dinners, nice drinks, gifts |
| **Access** | In-person experiences, retreats, normally restricted areas, behind the scenes |
| **Network** | Connection with other top customers (where applicable) |
| **Secrets** | Exclusive bonuses, trade secrets, access to vetted vendors. Think "secret menu," "black book." |

**Structure:** 1 to 3 core components + 3 bonuses. Main reason: layer bonuses in one at a time during the sequence to increase the likelihood people buy with each additional bonus. Give new information to further expand the price-to-value discrepancy to push them over the edge. All bonuses are available to all buyers no matter when they buy. If someone buys early, the bonuses simply further confirm they made the right choice.

## How to Sell It

**For most businesses:** High price points deserve a one-on-one consultation. The more personal you make the sale of a personal product, the more bites you will get.

**If you have a ton of customers or a big brand:** Use automated checkout if you can sell expensive stuff without a consult.

**If you are not tech savvy but have cards on file:** Instructions to reply "I'm in" will do fine. With their permission, charge their card, book their appointment, or both. If you do not have their card on file, call them up to collect it immediately after they respond.

How you sell matters because it changes how you promote:

**Automated checkout path:** Build up tension (launch style), cause a stampede by opening the cart to everyone at once, first come first serve.

**Consultation path:** Make your thing available for purchase day one, but drive people to book appointments. This lets you spread out consults and see everyone, even with a limited sales staff (which is sometimes just you). Limited spots and first come first serve rules still apply.

## What to Charge

A lot. Take whatever your average cart value or transaction size is, and add a zero. If you want to get frisky, multiply it by five. Think 10x to 50x your current price tag.

The point is to go beyond comparison to your other products. You do these to select for the people with the purchasing power to get it.

Price it high enough to make you happy about providing the unscalable value. You can price for volume too, just scale the features and bonuses to accommodate.

| **Next day** | Back out one spot left (if applicable). Text. |

**Waitlist tip:** Sell out, then waitlist people who already tried to buy. If they had payment ready and just got there a little too late, get permission to notify them if a spot opens up, and charge them if it does.

## Bonus Layering Strategy

- Have all bonuses ready before you tell people about them
- Layer one new bonus per message to expand the price-to-value discrepancy
- Sell each bonus with explanations of good stuff they get and bad stuff it gets rid of
- The final bonus should be worth more than the entire price tag
- If someone buys early, bonuses further confirm their decision

## Selling More to Existing Customers

Selling more to your existing customers is not only smart business, it is essential to keeping customers long term. Giving clear offers with deadlines converts a higher percentage of the leads you get. So you both get more customers AND make them worth more. And all the added revenue, if executed as laid out, should disproportionately drop to the bottom line. Running even one Cash Play per year will pay for itself many years over.

On the other hand, if implemented incorrectly, you can seem like a shill, turn off good customers, and make room for better competitors. Follow the structure.

## Important Points

**Ultra-Premium Prices for Ultra-Premium Value.** If you think "I'm not giving anyone cell phone access," you missed the point. It is not about giving people access to your cell. It is about offering crazy value. Balance what your customers find crazy valuable, how many of those things you will do, and whether those things demand prices that would overflow your bank account in a few days.

**Exclude Cooled-Off Leads.** If your list has neglected leads (you have not sent them anything for six months or more), exclude them from this offer but start warming them up for the next one. Stick with existing customers and past customers in good standing.

## 5 Reasons to Run Fast Cash Offers Every 90 Days

Running them every week does not work. Running them once a year does not work either, at least if you do not hate money. Once a quarter is the sweet spot.

1. **Cleans up your pipeline.** A great offer with urgency and scarcity will get qualified-but-on-the-fence leads over the edge. More customers than you otherwise would have.
2. **Increases the value of every customer.** People who buy more often are more likely to keep buying (provided your delivery does not suck).
3. **Always something new and interesting going on.** Shakes things up. Keeps customers engaged.
4. **Time to deliver and reset.** Doing it once per quarter allows you time to deliver, then reset before running another play. Gets you into a cadence.
5. **All additional revenue drops to the bottom line.** Even if it is a smaller percentage of revenue, it may become a much larger percentage of profit. Assuming your business is break-even or better, all the additional revenue from warm leads is nearly pure profit.

If you feel you need longer than twelve weeks to cool off between promotions, run it twice a year instead. It is still free money.

### Key Messaging Principles

- Texts draw attention to the emails. Emails give the details.
- If you nailed the offer, you should sell out by day three or four.
- If you have not sold out or sold a substantial percentage by mid-campaign, add things to the offer before sending remaining emails.
- Pair texts with emails throughout.
- Book and close everyone before the deadline. This drives urgency.
- Seven days or less. Longer loses urgency. Shorter misses sales.

## The 4Rs (After Delivery)

After you deliver the Fast Cash offer, collect the 4Rs:
1. **Reviews** - get testimonials while the experience is fresh
2. **Results** - document outcomes for future proof
3. **Referrals** - ask for introductions to others who would benefit
4. **Resells** - offer the next thing, upsell, or renewal

## Fast Cash Checklist

- Pick and assemble your list (past + present customers)
- Pick the offer. Max out unscalable value. Model the examples.
- Pick a price. 10x to 50x for maximum cash. Be bold.
- Choose sales method: consults (spread appointments, open cart immediately, book calls before deadline) or automated checkout (build hype, open cart, stampede, first come first serve)
- Prep all emails and texts with matched CTAs
- Prepare all messages: announcement, recap, 2-days-out, 24-hours, cart-open, sales-update, 2-spots-left, sold-out, back-out
- Have bonuses ready before you tell people about them
- Send the sequence. Collect cash.
- Deliver the stuff.
- Collect the 4Rs: Reviews, Results, Referrals, Resells
- Cooldown your list. Get back to providing value.
- Set your next date. Repeat quarterly.
- Notify via email and text: once you sell a handful, when there are two left, when sold out, and if someone backs out after.

## Timing and Cadence Notes

- Seven-day campaign for consult-driven offers (enough time to meet everyone, short enough for urgency)
- Five-day campaign for automated checkout (tighter window, stampede effect)
- If you nailed the offer, expect to sell out by day three or four
- If you have not sold out or sold a substantial percentage mid-campaign, add more value to the offer before sending remaining emails
- Any longer than seven days and it loses urgency. Any shorter and you miss sales.
- After each Fast Cash Play, cool down your list. Return to value mode. Resume normal content and nurture.
- Set your next date immediately after completing a play so you stay on the quarterly cadence
