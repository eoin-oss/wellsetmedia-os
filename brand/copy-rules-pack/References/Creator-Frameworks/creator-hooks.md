## What is a Hook?

A hook is whatever gets the attention of your audience. It is whatever people see or hear first: the first few seconds of a video, the first line of copy, the first image their eyes land on. A good hook "sells" the person on paying attention. The goal: make them think "Oh, this is for me."

A hook has two parts:
1. **Call out** - gets the prospect to say "this is for me" (verbal or nonverbal)
2. **Condition for value** - promises a reward if they consume the content

## Why Hooks Matter

If nobody notices your ad, nobody buys your stuff. Nothing else matters. The hook is the first impression, and the first impression is the strongest impression with every human.

**What mastering hooks gets you:**
- 5x, 10x, sometimes 100x the amount of people who pay attention to your ads, content, or outreach
- More clicks on content, ads, and outbound = more revenue
- Better clicks (higher quality leads) = even more revenue
- More, better customers for the same dollars spent = lower CAC
- Unlocks new markets that mediocre hooks could not get you into profitably
- Unlocks new levels of scale as colder traffic still converts

**What bad hooks cost you:**
- Wasted ad spend on ads nobody watches
- Higher cost to acquire worse customers
- Inability to scale past a certain spend
- Fewer leads than you should get based on impressions
- Capped advertising efforts because you cannot expand

**Time allocation rule:** If you have 10 hours to advertise, spend the first 8 hours on hooks. That is how important they are.

## How Hooks Work (Cocktail Party Effect)

Scientists call it the "cocktail party effect." In a noisy room, a single sound can cut through and hold attention. Your name pierces through chaos. Good hooks harness this effect and cut through all the noise.

**Part 1 - The Call Out:** Grabs attention. Uses the cocktail party effect to single out the right audience.

**Part 2 - The Condition for Value:** Promises benefit if they consume. Can be explicit or implicit.

- **Explicit:** "If [MOMS - target] [WATCH THIS VIDEO - condition] [THEY WILL KNOW HOW TO GET THEIR KIDS TO BEHAVE - reward/value]"
- **Implicit:** A mom showing a kid behaving well in chaos, then turning to camera and saying "Look how I did it."

**Core principle:** People consume if they think the cost of consuming is less than the benefit they get from it. The hook sets up those conditions.

**Specificity balance:** Make hooks specific enough to get the right people and broad enough to get as many of them as you can.

## Verbal Hook Types

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Labels | Words your avatar identifies with | "Local business owners, I have a gift for you" |
| Yes Questions | Questions designed to get a "yes" | "Would you pay $1,000 to have the business of your dreams in 30 days?" |
| Open Questions | Questions that create curiosity | "Which would you rather be?" |
| Conditionals | Scenarios leading to a result or command | "If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong sh*t" |
| Commands | Direct commands telling the audience to act | "Read this if you're tired of being broke" |
| Statements | Declarations that provoke thought | "The smartest thing you can do today..." |
| Lists/Steps | Numbered frameworks or processes | "The 28 ways to stay poor" |
| Stories | Narratives and anecdotes | "One day I was in the back and this old lady comes in..." |
| Exclamations | Strong emotion: surprise, sadness, intensity | "Ahhhhh... This is the blueprint to becoming a millionaire" |

## Nonverbal Hooks

Hooks do not have to be just words. They can be noises or visuals.

Back to the cocktail party analogy: a dropped tray of dishes gets attention (signals an embarrassing disaster) and the cling-cling-cling of a knife against a champagne flute gets attention (signals important news). Both work, for different reasons, but in either case everyone wants to know what happens next.

**Rule:** If the platform allows, use verbal and nonverbal hooks together.

## Hook Writing Process: 70-20-10 Rule

Based on Google's innovation allocation model (Larry Page and Sergey Brin):

| Allocation | Category | What to Do |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| 70% | Core (proven) | Reuse hooks already proven in your content or other people's. More of what works. This stabilizes all advertising and guarantees a baseline of performance. |
| 20% | Emerging (promising) | Model hooks from adjacent niches or "winner-adjacent" concepts. Subscribe to outlier-tracking tools or search platforms by topic to find highest-performing content. |
| 10% | Experimental (new) | Try completely new formats, visuals, and hooks. Document wins and losses. If an experiment loses, document it to not repeat it. If it wins, it goes into the 70% bucket. |

### 70% Core - Action Steps

1. Create a new spreadsheet with tabs for each platform you advertise on
2. Columns: name, hook, views, link
3. Every time you go to make content, review your best hooks of all time
4. Best-performing posts on one platform get reused as hooks on other platforms (e.g., winning tweets become short-form video hooks)

### 20% Emerging - Action Steps

1. Subscribe to software tools that show outliers by platform (search by topic to find highest-performing content)
2. Paste screenshots of outliers into a slide deck
3. Add a few ideas of how you could repackage or reuse the formula for each outlier
4. Model the concept from other niches, not the exact hook

### 10% Experimental - Action Steps

1. Try new visual formats and hooks you think might work
2. Document every experiment result
3. Winners become mainstays in the 70% bucket
4. Losers get documented so you never repeat them

## Testing Hooks by Platform

| Platform | Hook-to-Content Ratio | Method |
|----------|-----------------------|--------|
| Paid ads | Record 10 hooks per 1 piece of ad content | Yes, 10x. The hook dictates the content, not the other way around. |
| Short content | Every winning tweet becomes a short, expanded from the hook | Finish the short by expanding on the hook. |
| Long content | Record 3 to 4 hooks per piece | Post-production picks the best one. |
| Email | Run active split tests on every campaign | Document winners in a separate file. |

## Let The Data Do The Teaching

If you are starting out with no past content, the process is even easier:
1. Make a bunch of content with no idea what will happen
2. See what happened
3. Follow 70-20-10 going forward

**No matter how bad your content is, some of it will be in the top 10% of that content.**

- Look at the hooks for your top 10% content. Make more like that.
- Look at your bottom 10%. Make less like that.
- Repeat until the end of time.
- This is how you get better without a teacher. Let the data do the teaching. Most people have no idea anyways.

Take one idea and write it in every hook type. This is how you create variety from a single concept.

| Stories | "All of a sudden, my phone starts ringing off the hook..." |

## Hook Checklist

### First Time Through
- Look at top-performing ads or content from others
- Isolate the hooks
- Write down your favorite 50
- Use all of them in ads and content

### Every Time After
- Look at top 10% performers (top 5)
- Make 70% of next batch using those top 5 hooks
- Make 20% doing permutations of those hooks (visuals and words)
- Make 10% completely different hooks (model what you did in "first time through")

### Good Hooks Contain
- Some way to catch attention (call out)
- Some implication of value if they consume it

## Quick Reference

1. **Spend 80% of your time on hooks.** If eight hours to advertise, spend the first six on hooks.
2. **70-20-10 allocation:** 70% proven hooks, 20% adjacent winners, 10% experimental.
3. **Every hook type is a rewrite opportunity.** Take one idea and write it as a label, question, conditional, command, statement, list, and story.
4. **Let the data teach.** Your top 10% content reveals what hooks work. Make more like those. Stop making the bottom 10%.
5. **Cross-pollinate platforms.** Winning tweets become short-form hooks. Winning shorts become ad hooks. Winning ad hooks become email subject lines.
6. **The hook dictates the content, not the other way around.** Once you have massive data sets of hooks, you never need to worry about what content to make.
7. **Nailing hooks has 2x-5x-10x'd many businesses overnight.** Misunderstanding them has done the opposite, also overnight.
8. **Dean Graziosi principle:** Only the opening sentences changed. The book, the cover, the pages, the offer, the price, the website all stayed the same. The hook made it a monster hit vs. a bomb.
