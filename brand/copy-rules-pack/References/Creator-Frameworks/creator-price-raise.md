## Core Principle

The easiest way to make more money is to charge more for the same stuff. Most business owners set prices by copying competitors, but most competitors are broke. So why copy them?

"There's no strategic advantage to being the second lowest price in a marketplace. But there is for being the highest." (Dan Kennedy)

Smart business owners use extra money to make more and better advertisements, constant product improvements, and pamper their customers. They use the extra money to make it worth the extra money. Everyone wins.

People have a lot of difficulty with raising prices. They are either afraid of getting angry messages, that their sales will drop, or they simply do not think they are worth that amount of money. It is "poor people" thinking. Over the long term, it becomes a race to the bottom. With you and every other poor business owner competing to have the lowest price. Avoid.

**What this playbook covers:**
1. Why raising prices is a good idea
2. Rules of raising prices
3. How to pick your price
4. How to raise your price (step by step)
5. The price raise letter that saved Trey's business from certain death

## Opening Case Study: Trey's Gym

Trey ran a gym in North Carolina. He charged $49/month for memberships because he wanted everyone to have access to fitness. He thought cheap prices would attract grateful customers. Instead, three massive problems emerged:

1. **Worst customers ever.** The cheapest customers created a customer service nightmare. They were not grateful at all.
2. **More customers, still losing money.** His savings were gone. Any unexpected cost would have ended the operation.
3. **Could not afford paid ads.** He was losing so much money each month he had to pay employees out of savings. No budget for growth.

Trey was 12 weeks from closing his gym. Zero members.

Trey's advice to raise prices was met with terror: "Man, that sounds terrifying. I really don't want to do that to my members." The response: "Trey, in 12 more weeks, you won't have a gym. Which means zero members. It's do or die."

First step: negotiate partial payments with the landlord and use that difference to cover payroll. That bought a couple of weeks. Then, create the plan to raise prices.

**The fix:** We crafted a price raise letter. Trey used it as a script for an explainer video posted in his gym community (comments turned off, questions directed to him personally). Prices tripled on all members.

**Result:** Trey lost some members, but not even close to all. His business went from bleeding $2,000/month to pocketing $4,000 in profit. Took one week to roll out.

**The bigger point:** This is not just about Trey, distressed businesses, or even OK businesses. Although most business owners wait until disaster strikes before trying to make a bunch of money fast, you do not have to. A business does not have to be on the brink of disaster to do things that skyrocket profits in less than a week. Imagine turning $4,000/month into $10,000. Or $40,000 to $100,000. Or $400,000 to $1,000,000.

## Why Raising Prices Creates a Virtuous Cycle

### When you INCREASE prices (virtuous cycle):

**Customer effects:**
- Client emotional investment INCREASES
- Perceived value of your service INCREASES
- Client results INCREASE (they try harder when they pay more)
- Client demandingness DECREASES (the people who pay you the most are the most easygoing)
- Clients get MORE service because you have more money to spend on them, raising your level of service (race to the top)

**Business effects:**
- Profit per customer INCREASES
- Perceived self-value INCREASES (you feel like you are worth more, because you charge what you are worth and are able to serve more)
- Ability to create results INCREASES (you can get the word out and actually help more people by reinvesting money into the business and marketing)
- Level of service rendered INCREASES for each customer
- Conviction in sales process INCREASES (you and your team are confident you can deliver what you promise and even guarantee it)

### When you DECREASE prices (vicious cycle):

**Customer effects:**
- Client emotional investment DECREASES
- Perceived value of your service DECREASES
- Client results DECREASE (decreased investment, decreased perceived value)
- Client demandingness INCREASES (the cheapest customers ask for the most)
- Clients get LESS service because you have less money to spend on them

**Business effects:**
- Profit per customer DECREASES
- Perceived self-value DECREASES
- Ability to create results for customers DECREASES
- Conviction in sales process DECREASES (you and your team are no longer sure you can even deliver what you promise)
- Gratitude for customers DECREASES (you feel underappreciated, like you are "giving away the farm")

## Rules for Raising Prices

People are petrified of raising prices because they fear losing some customers. But if they continue feeding this fear, it leads to losing all their customers because their business goes under. They wait until the universe forces their hand in a mad scramble to exist. This happened to Trey. Do not be like Trey.

Warren Buffett: "If you've got the power to raise prices without losing business to a competitor, you've got a very good business. And if you have to have a prayer session before raising the price by a tenth of a cent, then you've got a terrible business."

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Never grandfather existing customers** | Value depends on price. If value goes up, so should price. Never lock customers into a price if you can avoid it. You never know what you are going to want to do (or deliver) in the future. Locking in a price limits your options. Not worth it. |
| **Never sell "one-time price" for lifetime access** | Unless it truly costs zero dollars to fulfill. Every business owner who started this way ends up fixing it over time. If you sell "pay me once and I have to deliver forever," understand that forever lasts a lot longer than what they paid you once. You typically end up with upset customers when you run out of the money they paid but still have to maintain the commitment. Theoretically, if LTV on lifetime access is greater than what you normally make, it makes sense. But show me one large company with lifetime access for a one-time payment. They do not exist. Model success, not failure. |
| **It is OK to be insecure early** | You probably suck at first. But as things improve and your brand grows, so should your price. Do not resent early adopters paying early adoption prices. Do not be the entrepreneur who hates their customers. Let your product do its job and have a price to match. |
| **Raise prices at least once per year** | Buffett bought See's Candies a gazillion years ago. After buying it, he let managers run the business and control all decisions except one: price. He sent them the prices of all products every year. Over 50+ years he raised prices on average 10%/year, and in some years up to 17%. If he made it the sole decision he was in charge of, you can bet it is a good investment of your time too. |
| **Test on new customers first** | First, this gives you data to see if it actually makes you more money. Second, knowing other people have said yes without hesitation gives you confidence when you present it to the rest of your base. It also shows existing customers the marketplace has already agreed with you. |
| **Meet with people if raising 50%+** | Big jumps (usually from early pricing mistakes) require personal conversations on top of the letter. But you do that on top of all the other stuff. |
| **Pair with a launch when possible** | Every business has new products or promotions that launch. The best time to do a price raise is within 90 days of a launch. Frame it as "early adopter pricing" and draft off the momentum. |
| **Do the math first** | Know what percentage of customers you can lose and still make money before you raise. If you have already tested on new customers, then no matter what, you will make more money eventually. |

### Case Study: Shopify Competitor (100x Price Increase)

Fear not. A Shopify competitor discovered they made a grave undercharging mistake. They charged $30/month. Hundreds of their customers made over $1,000,000/year because of the $30 software. Which sounds cool, but they had another problem. The more sales customers made, the more it cost to fulfill. Their biggest customers actually lost them the most money. Two choices: fire their biggest customers or raise prices.

The CEO hopped on the phone with those customers, all 300 of them, and took prices from $30 to $3,000 on average (a 100x increase).

How many did they lose? One. If people see the value, they will stay.

## How to Pick Your New Price

The best price is the price that makes you the most money. That means understanding how price affects the number of sales AND the profit per sale. Look at how one price change cascades through conversion rate, churn, LTV, and total return.

### Price Testing Table

| Price | Clicks | Conv Rate | Sales | Churn | LTV | Total Return | Difference |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------|-------|-----|-------------|------------|
| $10 | 100 | 5% | 5 | 10% | $100 | $500 | Baseline |
| $20 | 100 | 4% | 4 | 10% | $200 | $800 | +60% |
| $100 | 100 | 2% | 2 | 33% | $300 | $600 | +20% |

### Analysis: $10 to $20

- 100% price increase causes only 20% drop in conversions (5 sales to 4 sales)
- Churn stays the same at 10%
- LTV doubles from $100 to $200
- 4 customers at $200 = $800 vs 5 customers at $100 = $500
- Net: 60% more revenue from the same 100 clicks
- At 30% margins, this TRIPLES profit
- You lose one customer to gain an extra 60% revenue. Massive difference.

### Analysis: $20 to $100

- 10x increase in price (1000%) results in a 60% decrease in conversion (from 5 per 100 to 2 per 100). Ouch.
- They go from paying that price for 10 months to paying 10x higher price for only 3 months. Double ouch.
- Customer LTV goes up 3x from original ($100 to $300), and 50% from the $20 price even with 3x churn increase
- But the increase in LTV does NOT make up for the loss in conversion
- This still makes more money than the $10 price. But not as much as the $20 price.
- The $100 price would work only if churn did not spike so severely. That is why you test more points in between.

### Key Insight

Pricing affects both conversion rate AND churn rate, which both suffer as prices go up, but not always proportionally. That is the point. You may lose some customers, but often not as much as you gain.

**The golden rule:** If you can double your price and close only 20% fewer deals with all else staying the same, do it. If you owned a business that ran 30% margins with the original model, you would triple your profit with the higher price. Hard to imagine anything else that could make that kind of impact on the bottom line.

### Next Steps on Pricing

- We know the best price of those tested is $20. But we do not know the optimal price yet.
- Next, test more prices between $20 and $100. Look at $39 to $59 range. If churn stays lowish, these may be even more profitable options.
- The easiest way to test is to try on new customers. Then, provided churn does not go up like crazy and sales do not go down like crazy, raise on the rest of your customers.
- The first and most important thing to do is test, test, test.

## The RAISE Letter Framework

Five-section framework to communicate price increases. Developed in collaboration with Patrick Campbell (Profitwell), who oversaw 100 price increases directly and analyzed over 500. The letters themselves stay pretty short. The acronym stands for:

- **R** - Remind them of the value they have gotten
- **A** - Address the price change directly
- **I** - Invest in their future
- **S** - Soften the news with a loyalty discount
- **E** - Explain away their concerns

### R: Remind Them of Value

Start by reminding them of all the stuff you do and the subsequent value they have gotten since becoming a customer. The more you can personalize, the better. Highlight:

- Number of calls they have attended
- Number of deliverables they have used
- Amount of revenue they have generated
- Increase in profits they have seen
- Specific features they use daily

Make it about them.

> "Over the past [X] months, we have added so much value to [PRODUCT]:
> - We have made you [X result]
> - [Feature A] helped you do [Y]
> - [Feature B] you use daily"

### A: Address the Price Change Directly

Rip off the bandaid. Do not waste time. The rest of the letter will soften the blow. It is usually a single sentence.

> "For us to continue to invest in making [PRODUCT] for you and the [COMPANY] team, we need to increase our prices."

Then tell them the news.

### I: Invest in Their Future

Frame the price increase in the context of the value you are going to keep providing. You do not want to look greedy. Show how them paying you more is going to get them even more of the stuff they love, or things they did not know they wanted.

"This is what we are going to do (and you are going to get) with the extra money."

**Investment categories:**
- Hiring and retaining better people
- Training the people you have
- Better equipment
- Better technology/software
- Facility upgrades
- Higher level of service

**Framing rule:** Pair each investment with "more good stuff" or "less bad stuff":
- More good stuff: more aligned with their desired outcome, faster, easier, risk-free
- Less bad stuff: less misaligned, less slow, less hard, less risky

**Examples with the framework applied:**
- "Hiring and retaining great people so that we can build out this feature everyone has been asking for / deliver a higher level of service / faster response times / extended hours"
- "Training the people we have so that we can deliver a higher level of service"
- "Equipment so that you can use this rather than paying separately / make better stronger widgets faster"
- "Technology/software to make your experience faster and more accurate"
- "Facility improvements to elevate your experience"

**Three rules for this section:**
1. Do not say things you are not going to do (that is lying)
2. Frame all investments as value for them
3. Do not decide to add expenses you did not plan on incurring. This negates the benefits of the price raise to begin with.

Tell them the stuff you already planned to do over the next 12 months. Clearly show how that investment gets them even better value.

### S: Soften with a Loyalty Reward

People are more OK with a vanishing discount than a raised price. And they mind even less when another discount appears in the future. Raise the price now and immediately offer a discount that expires 3 to 6 months from now.

Bonus points: if you can show that they are getting this credit to their account on their invoices, do it. It almost feels like you are gifting them money/credit more than you are raising prices.

> "You have been insanely loyal to [COMPANY], using us for the past [X months/years]. As of today we are raising prices on new customers, but since you have been so loyal we are keeping you on your existing plan for the next [3-6 months]. We are giving you a [$X credit] as a way of saying thank you (after which we will move you to the new price of $[X])."

Tell them they are special. New customers have to pay full price today. They get a discount now and a price raise in the future.

### E: Explain Away Concerns

Let them know you take their concerns seriously. And personally. Add a PS statement that gives them a way to voice concerns. Be ready to roll up your sleeves and respond. Not as many will reply negatively as you think.

> Thank you for letting us be a part of [COMPANY]'s mission. If you have any questions at all, let me know. All replies go directly to me.

B2C version:
> "PS: If this will materially affect your ability to buy groceries, let me know and we will work something out."

B2B version:
> "PS: If this materially impacts your business, let me know and we will work something out."

**Three types of responses you will get:**

1. **Value-seers (majority).** They may not like it. But they understand and comply because they get enough value.
2. **Actually affected.** They will write sob stories. This gives you optionality. Respond: "Don't worry, we got you. Why don't we extend your discount another 6 months, then reach out then. Is that fair?"
3. **Going to cancel anyway.** Churn spikes month 1, drops below normal month 2, returns to baseline month 3. This indicates a high likelihood you simply "pulled forward" churn. People who were going to cancel or not buy next month just decide to do it a month sooner. You only miss one month of revenue from this tiny slice.

## Real-World Example: Gym Price Increase Letter (Trey's Gym)

This is the longer format letter used by hundreds of gym owners. Covers the same RAISE tenets in more detail. Patrick Campbell's shorter template is better, but this longer version still worked fine for hundreds of gyms. "Cover the main points. Be bold. You'll survive."

**Section 1: R (Remind) + Context Setting**

Open with gratitude and transparency. Acknowledge the journey and express genuine thanks.

> "As you most likely have seen, we have grown a tremendous amount over this period of time. A letter with black and white words can never communicate how deeply grateful I am to you for being a part of this journey. You have honored us with your patronage, and I want to honor you with transparency and with a significant improvement to the level of service you have been receiving. We want to truly transform lives, not just say we are."

**Section 2: I (Invest) - What We Currently Do**

List what you currently do to demonstrate ongoing commitment:
- **Hire and Retain The Best Coaches:** "We strive to replace the traditional model of fitness, watching the clock, counting reps, lack of enthusiasm, lack of knowledge, with a friendly face that knows your name, top-notch coaching, and motivation to not only get you in the door, but keep you coming back."
- **Continued Education:** "The best coaches in the industry are the best because they invest in their education. As a result, we continuously invest in our coaches' education like [certifications/workshops]."
- **New Equipment:** "As we continue to learn about new training systems and methodologies, we continue to invest in up-to-date equipment. This applies to new equipment we recently purchased, but also replacing malfunctioning equipment."

**Section 3: I (Invest) - What Is Coming**

List what is coming down the pipeline:
- **Facility improvements:** "We are a lot like the Millennium Falcon: we ain't pretty, but we will get you there fast. While we don't provide cucumber-scented towels and marble bathrooms, you deserve working AC and showers. So our goal is to dramatically improve this facility or move to a new one whose quality matches the caliber of its members."
- **Member events:** Build community. Host events so members have something social to look forward to. "This is so you all can get to know one another like we know you, which I feel is one of the greatest benefits of owning this gym."
- **Specialty programs:** More individualized coaching to address specific needs for an even faster rate of progress than is possible in a large-group setting.
- **Accountability system (the biggest change):** Changing from "come whenever you can" to "you now have an appointment with a trainer 3x per week." Smaller class sizes. Better service. "We never want to be a 'get your sweat on' facility. We want to be a gym that prides itself on transformations, both internal and external. And we have noticed that making sessions 'whenever you want' has become 'never' for many people. And this does not sit well with me." People get results in the beginning, then slow down as novelty wears off. The accountability system fixes this.

**Section 4: A (Address)**

State the price change directly. Break it down to weekly terms to minimize sticker shock. Instead of saying the monthly increase, frame it as "$10 to $15/week increase depending on your current membership." Include a revised schedule stapled to the back with effective date clearly marked.

**Section 5: S (Soften)**

Launch a Refer-a-Friend program: one free month of service per referral who signs up. "Refer 12 friends over the next year and get an entire year absolutely free, as our way of saying thank you for helping us transform more lives."

**Section 6: E (Explain)**

Direct all questions to in-person conversations only. "Not via text, or FB Messenger, or Instagram DM, or email, or on a Facebook thread (God help us all). We want to talk IN PERSON. That way, there is zero chance of miscommunication." Turn off social media comments on announcement. No gossip mill. "We will not be allowing any social media group posts related to the business side of the gym. The group will stay focused on what it is intended to be, a forum for the community to connect and encourage one another on this path to transformation."

Sign by hand. Close with a personal PS. "I love you all, seriously."

## Execution Options

### Option 1: Go All the Way Up at Once

If you are not profitable, do it. It worked for Trey (3x price, lost some members, went from -$2K/mo to +$4K/mo profit in one week). Actually send the letter. Do not be a chicken.

### Option 2: Stair-Step Increases

Position the discount as stepping up. Some portion falls off every X months or quarters:
- "$200/mo off for the first 4 months, then $100/mo off, then $50/mo off last three months"
- This allows you to ease customers into their new price
- People handle disappearing discounts better than raised prices

### For Incoming New Customers

Get new customers up to the new price immediately. No point in delaying that. They have no context. If they give you flack because they heard it was cheaper from a friend, your sales team can say:

> "That may feel like bad news but it is actually good news. First, it is good news because we just keep making the value better. And to give you some confidence, 96% of our customers agreed. And they should know, they are already using it. Beyond that, the good news is, this is the cheapest it will ever be. So there will never be a better time to get started."

People moan, but they still buy. Just because someone wants something for less does not mean they will not buy it for more.

### If You Have a Community

Post a video version (like Trey did). Turn off comments. Do not want a complaint fest in the comments. Refer questions to you directly via the PS statement.

## Why You Should Actually Do This

Most business owners live lives of quiet desperation. They keep their heads barely above water. They try harder and harder every year, and for some reason, they make the same or less. This is a hard way to live. Oftentimes, it is because they do not understand the power of pricing. And just how much more you make with even small increases in price.

You have two options. Allow competitors to slowly grind your price down until you cannot offer any more for any less. Or take control and set the prices that maximize your goals.

## Benefits of Optimized Pricing

**When you optimize pricing right:**
- Attract better customers
- Make more profit per customer
- Invest the extra profit into making a better experience
- Invest more into more advertising to grow the business
- Unlock more expensive channels of acquisition your mispriced competitors cannot use
- Feel good about your product/service
- Keep the best talent because you can pay them the best

**When you do it wrong (or never change prices):**
- Bring in the wrong customers
- Watch profits slowly erode
- No longer reinvest in product or service
- Keep less money every month
- Feel worse and worse about what you sell
- Lose the best talent to competitors who can pay more

Revisit pricing at least annually. First, make up for inflation. Second, make up for other costs that have gone up. Then test for more.

"Raise prices one time. See that you don't die. Notice how you just make more money. Get addicted to price increases. Keep making your stuff better so that you can keep raising your prices."

**The fastest way to make more money is to charge more money for the same thing.** Even better, do it without losing any customers. When you do that, you give yourself a permanent raise, make your business more valuable, and build a war chest of cash to ward off competitors. When you do not, you eventually get beat by inflation and competitors who do. Worse yet, if you do it wrong, you can lose customers and money. But costs go up over time. Raising prices is a fact of business. You have to do it. And you want to do it right.

**Mindset shift:** This can be a death sentence to a business that does it wrong, or a miracle to one who does it right (like Trey). Do not underestimate the potency of proper pricing just because the playbook is short. These playbooks are meant to be used, not studied. Make money. Have fun.

**A 20% price increase on a 10% margin business triples profit.** The math on pricing is wildly asymmetric. Small increases in price translate to massive increases in profit because you have already absorbed your fixed costs. The incremental revenue from a price increase flows almost entirely to the bottom line.

## Price Raise Checklist

Cut or tear this out for your own reference. Follow it step by step.

1. Decide on your new price based on testing with new customers
2. Test with new customers first. If they buy and stay at rates that increase how much money you make, continue.
3. Segment out "old" customers that came before the price raise
4. Write out 1-5 bullets of current value: get as much data as you can from customers to demonstrate what you have done for them so far, or how much they use the products/services you have. Personalize to the greatest degree possible.
5. Tell them the price raise happens now
6. Write out 3 bullets of the biggest investments you will make with the profit from the increase (things you are already going to do)
7. Explain how these investments benefit them
8. Give them an expiring discount as a reward for being a loyal customer. This softens the blow.
9. Tell them you respond personally if they have any issues
10. Sign in ink if possible. If not, sign the email personally.
11. Finish with a strong PS statement telling them to reach out if this is going to ruin their lives or business
12. Do this individually (phone calls) if the price raise is 50%+ and you can manage the call volume
13. Stair-step discount: if the raise is large, drop off the discount at 6-month intervals to accomplish 2-3 mini jumps in price. People have an easier time handling disappearing discounts rather than raised prices.
14. Track churn for 3 months (expect spike month 1, drop month 2, baseline month 3)
15. For incoming new customers: raise to new price immediately. If they heard it was cheaper, use the "actually good news" script.
16. If you have a community: post a video, turn off comments, direct questions to you personally
