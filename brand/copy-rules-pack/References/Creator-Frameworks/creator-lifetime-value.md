## Core Principle

If advertising is the machine that makes a business grow, LTV is the fuel. The business owner who can make a customer more valuable than the competition wins, because they can outspend everyone to acquire the customer. The cost of getting a customer can only hit zero, but how much money you make from each customer can go infinitely high.

**Key insight:** Eyeballs and earballs only get more expensive over time. Cost per impression has gone up consistently over the last hundred years. As new platforms emerge, attention is cheaper temporarily, but the market catches up quickly. The only durable advantage is making your customers worth more to you than your competition's customers are worth to them.

**[CREATOR_NAME]'s preferred strategy:** Buy businesses that can already get customers but struggle to maximize how much those customers spend. Then fix it back to front: maximize LTV first, then pour more money into the already-working advertising machine.

## What is LTV?

Lifetime Value (LTV) is the gross profit collected over the lifespan of a customer. Total money made from a customer minus everything it costs to deliver. Also called Lifetime Gross Profit (LTGP) or Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

## How to Calculate LTV

### Step 1: Gross Profit

Revenue from a sale minus cost to deliver (NOT net profit, which includes all expenses). Gross profit is what pays the rest of your bills.

- **Product:** Sell widget for $100, costs $20 to make and ship. Gross profit = $80.
- **Service:** 10 clients/rep at $3,000/mo. Rep costs $6,000/mo.
  - Revenue per rep = 10 x $3,000 = $30,000/mo
  - Gross profit = $30,000 - $6,000 = $24,000
  - Gross margin = $24,000 / $30,000 = 80%
  - Gross profit per client = $3,000 x 80% = $2,400/mo

**Action:** Figure out your gross profit AND gross profit percentage for each thing you sell and your business overall. You may be surprised that some products you spend a lot of time on don't make you as much profit as you thought.

### Step 2: Average Transactions or Churn

Figuring out average customer transactions is always an estimate because customers come, leave, and come back. Lifetime transactions always increase as a business gets older because old customers buy more. Two methods:

- **Transaction business:** Export lifetime customer data. Sort by number of transactions. Average that column.
  - Example: Avg # of transactions = 4
- **Recurring business:** Calculate churn. Churn = customers lost / original customers for the period.
  - Last Period = 100
  - This period = 95
  - Difference = 100 - 95 = 5
  - Churn = 5 / 100 = 5%

**Critical note:** New signups do NOT affect churn calculation. You could sign up zero or 1,000 new clients during the same month. You still lost five of the original one hundred. Churn is still 5%.

### Step 3: Multiply or Divide

- **Transaction business:** Gross profit x avg transactions = LTGP
  - $80 x 4 = $320 LTGP
- **Recurring business:** Gross profit / churn = LTGP
  - $2,400 / 5% = $48,000 LTGP

## The Crazy 8: Eight Ways to Increase LTV

The traditional framework says "there are only two ways to make a customer more valuable: increase average order value, or increase the number of times they buy." That is too broad to be actionable. The Crazy 8 breaks it into smaller, implementable chunks.

| # | Method | Direction | Description |
|---|--------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Raise prices | Up | Charge more for the same thing |
| 2 | Lower costs | Up | Reduce cost to deliver the same thing |
| 3 | Upsell frequency | Up | Get them to buy more again later |
| 4 | Cross-sell | Up | Get them to buy a different thing on top |
| 5 | Upsell quantity | Up | Get them to buy more now |
| 6 | Upsell quality | Up | Get them to buy a premium version |
| 7 | Downsell quantity | Capture | Get them to buy fewer things rather than nothing |
| 8 | Downsell quality | Capture | Get them to buy lower-cost things rather than nothing |

---

### #1 Increase Prices

Charge more money for the same thing. Pricing affects gross profit more than any of the other eight methods. A 20% price increase on a 10% profit business triples profit (the extra revenue drops straight to the bottom line).

**Case study:** [CREATOR_NAME] bought one company, reviewed everything, and simply doubled the price. That one change tripled the business. Nothing else changed.

**How to find the sweet spot:**

1. **Formula:** Sales conversion rate x lifetime gross profit. The price that maximizes this product wins. The goal is not to sell the most stuff. The goal is to make the most money.
2. **Testing cadence:** Test prices every quarter. Research by Profitwell found a tight relationship between profitability and how frequently companies tested pricing.
3. **Nudge method:** Start low to make sure people actually want the thing and run water through the pipes to find leaks. Then nudge up 20% every 10 sales until conversions drop dramatically. Go back to the sweet spot.

**Break-even calculation:** Before raising price, calculate what conversion rate at the new price would be your break-even point vs. the old price. Track the actual conversion rate. If above break-even, you have a winner. Then test again until conversion rate x LTGP drops.

**If you overshoot:** Add extra bonuses for customers who bought at the too-high price. Or refund them the difference. Testing price is expensive for a few months. Not testing price is far more expensive for life.

### #2 Decrease Costs

Reduce cost to deliver the same thing. Costs can only go to zero, but the gains improve profitability and scalability.

| Tactic | How It Works | Impact |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Increase employee-to-customer ratio | Give reps 10 clients instead of 5 | Cuts costs in half |
| Offshore talent | Same skill set, lower cost of living | Can cut costs 80%+ |
| Productize delivery | Sell similar customers with similar problems. Use templates and automation. | Faster, more consistent, more profitable |
| Done-for-you to done-with-you | Dramatically increases customer-to-employee ratio | Minor price decrease, major profit increase |
| Cap usage | Limit revisions or sessions. Charge per use after the cap. | Prevents endless resource drain |
| Lifetime to annual | Stop servicing customers indefinitely off a one-time transaction | Removes infinite obligation |
| In-person to remote | Lower costs for you and the customer for travel and sourcing talent | Cost reduction on both sides |
| Cut meeting times | Delete all recurring meetings quarterly. Add back only what is needed at half the time. | Reduces waste for employees and customers |
| Buy in bulk and prepay | Lock in 10-20% discounts with vendors. Guaranteed ROI. | Immediate return on capital |

**Action:** Circle the top two you could implement within your business to decrease delivery costs.

### #3 Increase Number of Purchases

Get people to buy the same thing more times. Three methods:

#### a) Add Recurring

Add a version of your offer that recurs on a weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual basis. Even if churn is high, going from buying once to buying three times = 3x purchase frequency.

| Business Type | Recurring Add-on |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Service (plumber) | Plumbing membership for all plumbing needs. 20% of customers buy. |
| Physical product (mugs) | Funny mug of the month membership. 10% take it, stay 5 months. |
| Digital product (course) | Monthly accountability calls as ongoing service. |

#### b) Decrease Churn

Get them to stay and pay longer. Halving churn doubles LTV (Price / Churn = Lifetime revenue).

| Business Type | Churn Reduction Tactic |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Service (lawn care) | Hold local customer appreciation events via handwritten letter. People cancel less because they like you more. |
| Physical product (meat membership) | Allow customers to change quantity and delivery frequency via text. Flexibility keeps them longer. |
| Digital product (course) | Add community feature that keeps people engaged longer. |

#### c) Follow Up Regularly

Run a reactivation campaign to get leads from your list or audience to re-engage. Preferred method: run a quarterly promotion. Between promos, provide value to former customers and potential leads. This balances the give:ask ratio and keeps the business top of mind.

| Business Type | Follow-Up Method |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Service (lawn care) | Quarterly "best lawn" competition to reactivate old customers |
| Physical product (supplements) | Quarterly weight loss challenge |
| Digital product (course) | Quarterly cohort promotion with extra attention from you. Or add one new module and "launch" a new/improved version. |

**Action:** Pick one of the three above to prioritize implementing over the next quarter.

### #4 Cross-Sell Something Different

Sell a complementary product alongside the first. Add conversion rate x gross profit of the upsell to your original LTV.

**Example:** LTV = $100. 20% buy a $100 cross-sell at 100% margin. New LTV = $100 + (20% x $100) = $120.

**Rule:** The cross-sell should not dramatically change who you serve or what you do daily. It should fit seamlessly into existing infrastructure, resources, and expertise. Do not break your business to pick up extra change.

| Business | Cross-Sell |
|----------|-----------|
| Lawn care | Snow blowing in winter |
| Burgers | Fries and a soda |
| Course | Community membership on top |

**Action:** Write down a cross-sell for your business that you think people would take and that does not dramatically change who you serve or what you do every day.

### #5 Sell More (Increase Quantity)

Sell more of the same thing at once. Three variations:

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Bulk | Prepay for longer period | Client prepays for a year (12x). Or upsell from one burger to two. |
| More often | Increase service frequency | Monthly to every 3 weeks (1.33x) |
| Bigger | Increase size per unit | 1-hour service to 3-hour service (3x). Or normal burger to bigger burger. |

**Tactic:** Write down a version of each quantity upsell. Offer the quantity upsell first on sales calls, then downsell to the standard offer. Routinely see 20%+ lifts in cash collected overnight.

### #6 Sell Better (Increase Quality)

Sell a premium version at a higher price. Two paths: newer version or premium version.

**Premium service levers (17 dimensions):**

| Dimension | Standard | Premium |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| Response time | Hours/days | Minutes |
| Time availability | Come/call at specific times | Whenever you want |
| Days of the week | Mon/Wed/Fri | All days |
| Hours of the day | 9 to 5 | 24 hours |
| Session length | 15min support calls | 60min support calls |
| Location availability | This one location | All locations |
| Cancellation flexibility | Reschedule fees | Free rescheduling |
| Speed of delivery | Wait in line, next week | Priority, same day/next day |
| Service ratio | One-to-many | One-on-one or many-to-one |
| Communication method | Chat support | Text, video call support |
| Provider qualifications | New employee | You or long-time senior employee |
| Live vs. recorded | Recorded (watch later) | Live (watch it happening now) |
| In-person vs. remote | Remote | In-person where it happens |
| DIY/DWY/DFY | Do it yourself | Done with you or done for you |
| Expiration terms | Works for X time or specific times | Works forever |
| Personalization | Generic or 3-5 avatars | Made just for you |
| Guarantee | Standard or none | Premium guarantee |

**Premium physical products:** Better ingredients (sirloin vs ground chuck), stronger/lighter/longer-lasting materials (steel vs iron), newer models, updated recipes.

**Action:** Write down a quality upsell for your product or service. Offer it first on sales calls, then downsell your standard offer. Or sell the standard, then meet with the customer again to upgrade them into a higher quality experience. Routinely see 20%+ lifts in cash collected upfront and LTV overall.

### #7 Downsell Fewer (Lower Quantity)

Sell a smaller amount of the same thing to people who would otherwise buy nothing. This or nothing = this wins.

**The math on why downsells increase LTV per visitor:**

| Scenario | Visitors | Main Offer Sales | Main Revenue | Downsell Sales | Downsell Revenue | Total |
|----------|----------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| Before downsell | 4 | 2 at $5 | $10 | 0 | $0 | $10 ($2.50/visitor) |
| After downsell | 4 | 2 at $5 | $10 | 1 at $2.50 | $2.50 | $12.50 ($3.13/visitor) |

50% more per person who walks in the door.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Fewer units | Shorter commitment | 3 months instead of 12. Or one burger instead of two. |
| Less often | Reduced frequency | Every other month instead of monthly |
| Smaller | Reduced size/scope | 30 min sessions instead of 60 min. Or smaller burger. |

**Rule:** Only downsell to unqualified prospects. Never let your sales team downsell someone who qualifies for the main offer. This prevents cannibalizing your core product while capturing extra revenue. Forbid the sales team from selling a qualified person a downsell. Best of both worlds: no cannibalization, plus extra cash from downsells.

### #8 Downsell Lower Quality

Offer a less valuable version at a lower price. Reverse the premium quality levers.

**Service downsell dimensions:**

| Dimension | Standard | Downsell |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Response time | Minutes/hours | Wait in line |
| Meeting availability | Flexible | Limited slots |
| Location access | All locations | Fewer locations |
| Days per week | All days | Fewer days |
| Hours | Extended | Limited hours |
| Rescheduling | Free | Less flexible |
| Staff | Senior employees | More junior employees |
| Customer ratio | Lower ratio | Higher customer-to-employee ratio |
| Communication | Convenient methods | Less convenient methods |
| Live vs recorded | Live | More recorded, less live |
| In-person vs remote | In-person | More remote, less in-person |
| Service level | DFY or DWY | DWY or DIY |
| Personalization | High | Less personalization |
| Guarantee | Strong | No guarantee or weaker guarantee |

**Physical products downsell:** Lower quality ingredients/materials, worse or zero warranty, longer wait times.

**Same rule:** Only downsell to unqualified prospects.

## The Favorite Upsell Formula

[CREATOR_NAME]'s most successful upsell across all businesses:

> More of -- or more help with -- what they just bought... but with faster results, less risk, less effort, less hassle -- for more money.

**Case study:** This formula was used as a back-end upsell for a portfolio company. Survey showed vast majority of customers preferred this over an unrelated product the founders wanted to sell. Result: 2.2x LTV per customer, which allowed 5x advertising spend across all channels profitably. Revenue went from a few million per month to a few million per week.

**Key lesson:** The business serves the customer, not the other way around. If customers like your stuff, they want to buy more stuff like it. Forcing a product customers do not want is a mistake that costs millions of dollars and months of time.

## Quick Reference

1. **LTV is the fuel for growth.** The business that makes each customer worth the most can outspend everyone to acquire them. If you can make a customer worth more to you than to your competitor, you have a veritable monopoly of attention.
2. **Price is the highest-leverage of the 8 methods.** A 20% price increase on a 10% margin business triples profit. Test prices every quarter.
3. **The best upsell is more of what they just bought.** Faster results, less risk, less effort, less hassle, for more money. This formula 2.2x'd LTV in one portfolio company.
4. **Upsell first, downsell second.** Always present the premium option first, then standard. Only downsell unqualified prospects. Forbid sales team from downselling qualified buyers.
5. **Halving churn doubles LTV.** Price / churn = lifetime revenue. Small churn reductions create massive LTV gains.
6. **Drill the framework until fluent.** Run through the Crazy 8 for every business you encounter. The more you use the frameworks, the more "fluent" you become in monetization.
7. **Cross-sells should not break the business.** The easiest thing to add seamlessly with existing infrastructure wins. Do not dramatically change who you serve or what you do daily.
8. **Costs can only go to zero. Revenue can go infinitely high.** Prioritize revenue-side moves (pricing, upsells) over cost-cutting, but do both.
