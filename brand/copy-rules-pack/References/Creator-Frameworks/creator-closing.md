## Core Philosophy

Closing gives people the power to decide. The goal is not to force a purchase. It is to help the prospect overcome decision avoidance and make a genuine choice, yes or no. Decision avoidance is the real enemy, not objections.

Closing somebody means three things:
1. Get a good understanding of what they want in the earlier part of the sales conversation.
2. Put what they want next to the option to have you help them get there.
3. Make sure they come to a genuine decision.

Bottom line: People with enough decision-making power can decide. Seeing the right stuff you've won, a decision to make, and people on the fence end up purchasing more overall. For that reason, providing someone with decision-making power before something they do benefits them in some way.

> "The difference between help and manipulation is intention."

### Three Audience Buckets

Advertising splits audiences into three buckets:
- **YESes (Lay Downs):** Advertising already closed them. Just accept payment. Don't mess it up.
- **NOs (Unqualified):** Bad fit. Wrong person pulled in by advertising. This is an advertising problem, not a closing problem.
- **MAYBEs (On the Fence):** They value what you sell but have reservations. This is where closers earn their money.

> "The money is in the maybes."

Most people interested in what you sell fall into this bucket. The job of the salesman is to check those boxes, close the deal, and finish the job that advertising started. Closing separates winners from losers because it determines who gets the massive number of customers from bucket three.

The better your advertising, the more maybes you get. Better ads make the pie bigger, and closing gets you a bigger slice of it.

### Power: The Missing Ingredient

People on the fence struggle over a buying decision. They have too little power to influence their own decision one way or another, so they waffle. Decision avoidance becomes the sole focus of closing.

A purchase describes a black-and-white event. People either buy or not. But "no" and "I avoid deciding" are not the same thing, even though neither leads to a purchase. Some of the people who might buy, don't. And those people almost always get swept into the "no" bucket. This horrendous mistake costs businesses so much money.

You eliminate this problem by answering one question: How can you tell the difference between somebody who doesn't want your thing and somebody who avoids deciding? Closing. The answer is closing.

If you close properly: best case, they buy your thing and live happily ever after. Worst case, they don't, but you dodge the costly bullet of unqualified purchases. You should feel bad about letting anyone stay on the avoidance treadmill.

### Benefits of Closing Right vs. Wrong

**When you close the right way, you:**
- Decrease your cost of getting customers
- Improve your Return On Advertising Spend (ROAS) on all campaigns
- Get better customers who are more skeptical (and therefore more committed)
- Improve cash flow (more Paid In Fulls and fewer payment plans)
- Get better customer results because they are fully bought in
- Out-execute competitors in the same market with the same services
- Keep closers longer
- Get better paid salespeople because they make you more money

**When you close the wrong way, you:**
- Ward off good customers
- Pay more for every customer
- Make it harder to stay profitable on advertising campaigns
- Create friction to scaling
- Get customers worse results because they're not bought in
- Extend out your cash flow with extended payment terms
- Lose good salespeople because they aren't making enough

## The Onion of Blame

People avoid decisions by blaming things that look reasonable but fall apart under inspection. All objections map to three layers:

| Layer | Core Belief | Sub-Categories |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Circumstances** | "Stuff outside my control prevents me from buying" | Time, Money, Fit |
| **Other People** | "Someone else limits my ability to decide" | Authority/Permission, Trust/Skepticism |
| **Self** | "Something about me makes this wrong" | Fear, Avoidance, Preferences, Rushed Decisions |

### Circumstances
- **Time:** "I don't have time right now" / "I'm too busy"
- **Money:** "This is too expensive" / "I can't afford it"
- **Fit:** "I don't like this detail" / "Not right for me" / "I need something different than what you offer"

### Other People
- **Authority/Permission:** "I need to check with X before deciding" (partner doesn't trust them to buy)
- **Trust/Skepticism:** "I've been burned before" / "What makes you different?" / "Let me think about it"

### Self
- **Fear:** "It might work for them, but it won't work for me" / "I'm scared to repeat my past mistakes"
- **Avoidance:** "I need to think about it" / "Send me a brochure and I'll email you tomorrow"
- **Preferences:** "I want your results, but I need to get them my way"
- **Rushed Decisions:** "This is happening too fast" / "I can't make a decision right now"

People often swap between layers. Expect multiple objections. It is normal for someone to present three or four "reasons" why they can't do something before buying. Each one you resolve peels back a layer until they can make a genuine decision. When someone swaps objections, it means you're closing correctly. Just keep peeling back the layers until you've empowered the prospect to make a decision.

## STAR Qualification Framework

Before closing, qualify prospects by confirming:
- **S** = Situation (they have the problem you solve)
- **T** = Timing (they have the time to solve it)
- **A** = Authority (they can make the decision)
- **R** = Resources (they have the money to spend)

A STAR prospect who says no is a closing problem. A non-STAR prospect who says no is an advertising problem. Qualify before selling. Use common sense. Trying to sell somebody a product they can't use wastes everyone's time. And unqualified prospects simply sell themselves an unqualified problem.

The best way to avoid decision-maker obstacles: make sure they can make a decision ahead of time. Have decision makers on the call (this is in the qualification process). We often schedule re-closes if we know decision-makers won't be on the call.

## Rules of Closing

1. **When in doubt, repeat back what they said.** You get time to think about what you're gonna say next, and you get active listening bonus skills. Too few salesmen listen at all, so this is good practice in general for most.

2. **Acknowledge or agree, never disagree.** The moment you disagree, it increases the likelihood people become hostile. Instead, buy yourself time: 1) Repeat back the last thing they said. Nod your head. 2) Say "totally agree" or "totally understand" or "that's a great point" or "thank you for bringing that up" or "You're totally right, I forgot to mention..." as ways to transition into your closes. We want our closes to be reframes or other ways to think about it, never a disagreement.

3. **Before getting real, get permission.** If you need to "get real" with someone, ask for permission first. Three ways: 1) "Can I be a coach instead of a friend for a second?" 2) "Would it be totally overstepping for me to put my coach hat on for a second?" 3) "Can I tell you what I told someone in your exact position just yesterday that helped her make this decision?" Then deliver the harsh truth.

4. **Learn to stack closes.** If one close doesn't work, hit it from another angle or analogy. Sometimes people don't bite at the first one. You can stack multiple closes together. And once it gets through, just one more will take us one step closer to a sale.

5. **You don't need to memorize.** You need to understand. When you understand the logic behind the closes, you can use them in your own words to help people. Understanding the reasoning behind them will allow you to move between them smoothly.

6. **You only need two or three closes per obstacle.** I provide seven or more, but you don't need to use them all. Not every close, just the ones that fit you. You just need to know one or two that work per main obstacle that you like.

7. **Use nudges when someone is right on the edge.** Small extra pushes after the main close. They've done as full closes in here. They're useful one-liners that tipped deals over the edge. They work especially well when someone is right on the edge of making a decision.

8. **Yellows are gold.** This may seem contrary to STAR (Situation, Timing, Action, Result). But I don't think so. Mainly because 1) people lie, 2) you already have the time blocked for the consultation, so might as well try to close, and 3) practice. Train teams to be equal opportunity closers. As long as the person has the problem, we try to close. This gets you better faster. And less qualified leads make the perfect opportunity for practice. Every qualified prospect is worth maximum effort.

9. **Volume negates luck.** The best way to guarantee you'll be world-class is to be the best in the volume category. Do so many sales that it would be unreasonable that you aren't successful.

10. **Don't assume objections mean they won't buy.** Statements like "It's expensive" aren't actual objections. Sometimes they're just observations. To which you can respond, "I agree." Or, just let them process out loud. Unless they say they don't wanna buy, don't assume it. Sometimes people just process their thoughts out loud. You're just there for the show.

11. **Never change your price on the spot.** If the product has value, hold the price. Changing the price just to get a sale sends you down a sad, dark, and ethically questionable path. Nevermind getting bullied by your own customers.

12. **If you feel like you're always in a battle, look at what happens before the call.** Look at what happens before they show up and at your scripting before you ask for the sale. The better you are at marketing, the more concerns have been resolved ahead of time.

13. **Only ask for the sale when you think you've got them.** If they need more convincing, keep probing.

14. **People want to believe you.** They want to buy. You must help their brains justify the decision they already want to make.

15. **Selling happens before the ask.** Closing happens after. This guide is explicitly for "after the prospect says no."

16. **Diffuse as much as you can before asking for money.** Easier to handle problems before the ask than after.

17. **Expect and plan for no.** It is not failure, it is expected.

18. **Price shock isn't a "no."** Unless someone tells you there's a problem, assume they're just processing their thoughts out loud.

19. **Selling properly is the first step to becoming a coach.** If you want to help people, you need to learn to help them make decisions to help themselves.

20. **The person who cares most about the prospect wins the sale.** Keep the prospect, not the sale, as the priority. If you keep their long-term well being at the center of the conversation, you will approach things with the right frame. They will hear it in your tone. It also completely divorces you from needing to make a sale. This pays dividends long term. Sometimes the best sales we make are the ones we don't.

21. **Seek to understand, not to argue.** This diffuses any tough situations. Just try and understand their perspective rather than get them to agree. Ask questions, don't accuse.

22. **Selling is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust.** You can only build trust if you genuinely want to help. You build trust by doing tips 11, 12, 13. They create an environment that increases the likelihood of a sale occurring.

23. **Belief and trust are a continuum, not binaries.** You can always get better at getting people to believe and trust you. The question is how much, not whether.

24. **Closers ask hard questions because they genuinely care.** Be kind, not nice. Nice guys don't want to offend. Kind people want to genuinely help the person improve their lives. And sometimes that means calling them on their BS, in a tactful way by helping them realize it for themselves.

25. **If you are going to say something offensive, say it about someone like them or your past self, not about them directly.** You may be right, but you won't close the sale. So tell a story of yourself saying the same thing or of you having overcome the same issue. They'll connect the dots.

26. **Once the prospect says yes, shut up.** This is the rule of law. Everything else you say has the potential to disrupt what is working.

27. **Record your sales calls.** There's a reason championship salespeople and champions treat each sale like game tape. And champion people, sales on top of the closers on the team. Focus on improving one thing at a time. What did you do well? What could you improve? What are things that you suck at so much that at a time until you've corrected them. Treat it like a performance.

28. **Power is the ability to directly influence people.** If you want to be powerful, you must understand how to close. Just commit a few to memory. Try them. Watch them work. Try a few more. And in no time you'll be one of the top closers on the team. And if it's a skill, you can learn it.

> "Reminder: You sit on the same side of the table. You're not competing with them. Validate what they say before responding. We can't convince someone. We can only guide them through a series of questions and statements to convince themselves."

## Tonality and Delivery Notes

How you deliver your message matters as much as the message itself. You can have the perfect script, but unless you've got big rapport and correct tonality you will flop.

**Key delivery principles:**
- **Warmth over pressure.** Your tone should communicate genuine care, not desperation.
- **Confidence without arrogance.** You believe in what you sell because you've seen it work.
- **Humor disarms.** If you can get them to laugh at the silliness of their own objection, they'll often close themselves.
- **Pacing matters.** Slow down on the important parts. Let the silence do the heavy lifting after a strong reframe.
- **Mirror their energy.** If they're calm, be calm. If they're excited, match it.

**Role-play training:** The best way to internalize these closes is to drill them daily with your team. Have one person play the prospect throwing objections, and the other practice the closes. Switch roles. Do this every huddle until the team can handle any objection without thinking.

## All-Purpose Closes

These are the 80/20 closes. The 20% of closes that work 80% of the time. They cut through a lot of smoke and mirrors and force prospects to wake up and decide, or pick a real obstacle you can then overcome.

Use these to respond to any objection that is not yes.

### The "Main Concern" Close
**They say:** *Anything that's not yes.*

**You say:** "What's your main concern? What are you afraid of happening?"

This is probably the most used phrase to build rapport with a prospect. If someone says they need to think about it, ask what their main concerns are or what variables they are using to make the decision. This allows you to have a more productive conversation.

If they give fluff answers, use the more pointed version: "What are you afraid of happening?"

If they still don't budge, spell out the actual worst case: "So you're afraid of spending money and it not working out?" They will laugh and give you a closer answer to the truth.

### The "Reason" Close
**You say:** "The reason you are telling yourself not to do this is the reason you need to do it."

| Objection Type | Script |
|---------------|--------|
| **Money** | "How long do you want 'I can't afford this' to be on your list of problems? The fact that you don't have money is the very reason you need to do this more than anyone." |
| **Time** | "The fact that you don't have time is the very reason you need to do this." |
| **Authority** | "The fact that you're so dependent on your spouse is the very reason you need to take this decision and own it." |
| **Avoidance** | "The fact that you're not sure about the person you want to be is the very reason we're going to help you in this program." |

Whatever the reason, it is usually the biggest chain holding them back. If you're casting your power to the thing that you're holding yourself back, that is the very thing that is enslaving yourself. The very reason you're holding on is the reason you need to do it. Breaking that chain is the very thing they need to do. The power comes back to you.

### The "Hypothetical" Close
**You say:** "If this were perfect, would you do it? What's the difference between perfect and what we've got?"

If they name a small fixable detail, fix it and close. They're not going to know all the things you can deliver on the spot.

If they can't think of anything, pivot: "Right, well because it sounds like it's nothing to do with the program; it's something else. What are you afraid of happening? Let's talk about that."

This works astonishingly well.

### The "Zoom Out" Close
**You say:** "Let's zoom out for a second. You want [X]. We sell [X]. Do you think overanalyzing the details might be the reason you haven't made it happen yet? At the end of the day, you just need to decide whether you think this makes it more likely for you to hit your goal than doing this on your own. And if you've tried on your own, it doesn't just look like it's for waiting, this is just the thing you've been looking for."

### The "1 to 10" Close
**You say:** "On a scale of 1 to 10, where 1 means you're not interested at all and 10 means you're ready to start right now, where do you see yourself?"

- If 10: "Great. Let's rock and roll." Close the sale.
- If 9 or less: "What would it take to get you to a 10?" They reply. You say: "No big deal. I think we can take care of that for you. Feeling 10ish now?"

### The "Best Case Worst Case" Close (3 Variations)

**Variation 1 (With guarantee, full):**
"Let's consider the options, both are risk-free, only one is guaranteed to not get you what you want. Best case, worst case. Option one, you do the thing, you get the result, life is awesome. Option two, you don't do the thing, you don't get the result because you didn't do the thing. Option three, you do the thing, but then you don't get the result. These are the options that can happen in front of you because we have a guarantee, they're all risk-free except for one of them. Only one of them has a true guarantee of not getting you where you want, which is walking out the door. So, which risk-free option do you want, the one that's risk-free that guarantees that you're not going to get there or the one that's risk-free and has the potential to get you where you want to go?"

**Variation 2 (With guarantee, short):**
"Best case we change your life forever. Worst case you get X amount of time free and learn a bunch of stuff along the way. Either way you get closer to your goal. Ready to start?"

**Variation 3 (No guarantee alternate):**
"Let's consider the options. Option one, you take a risk and do this. You either hit the goal or you get closer to your goal no matter what because you'll learn a ton. Option two, you take no risk but you guarantee you don't get what you want, by not starting. So, which option do you feel gets you closer to your goal?"

### The "Card Not On Me" Close
When they say they forgot their wallet or card:

**These sound like:**
- "I forgot my wallet at home"
- "I don't have the card I want to use on me"

**You say:** "No problem. Do you have your phone on you? Great. Just pull up your banking app, go to statements, and you can find the account number on the top right. I'll Google the routing number while you do that."

**Pro tip (ACH):** If you don't currently take automated clearing house (ACH) payments, you should. Besides the lower costs, fewer expirations and account changes, and decreased risk of chargebacks, you can always sign up a new client, even if they don't have their wallet.

**Prevention tip (ID first):** Once you get a verbal agreement, ask first for their ID. This forces them to take their wallet out. Once they give their ID to you, use it to fill out the contract. Chat about where they're from, etc. This fills the time. Once you've copied their information down, gesture to their wallet they usually have open on the table and say "I'll trade you for the card you wanna use." This avoids many of the "forgot my wallet" stalls.

## Specific Closes by Blame Type

### What I Say When They Blame Circumstances

**Core Argument:** "I avoid buying your thing because of stuff out of my control."

**What To Do About It:** We help them understand how they choose to let circumstances decide for them. But that's good news, because it also means they have the power to change it. This shifts decision-making power from the circumstances back to them.

Like saying "I can't make the wedding because something outside of my control came up" absolves the person of responsibility. Our goal is to get them to say "I'm not going to your wedding because I'm choosing to do something more valuable to me." You want the prospect to understand there is absolutely nothing wrong with making genuine decisions in their best interest.

> **REPEATED FOR EMPHASIS:** Assume you say something like "Totally get it," "Heard," "That's a great point," or "I'm right there with you" before you transition to overcome. You sit on the same side of the table. You're not competing with them. Validate what they say before responding. We can't convince someone. We can only guide them through a series of questions and statements to convince themselves.

---

### What I Say When They Blame Time

**These sound like:**
- "I'm too busy right now"
- "I don't have the time"

**What To Do About It:** First, we help them see where their hours go. Second, we discuss how they can better use them. If our thing saves them time compared to what they do now, makes their life easier, and we support them so they get maximum value, then our thing really does make for a better use of their time. Win-win. And since we're there to help, starting while busy is actually the best time to start. It means they'll get even more value when they're not busy.

#### "Better To Start When You're Busy"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Honestly, here's some good news: the busier you are, the better time it is to start. Because life is always going to have busy seasons, and if you can't figure out how to do it during busy seasons, then this isn't going to last. And you want this to last, right? Then what better time to do it than when you need it most?"

#### "You're Gonna Get Busy Again"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Even if you did wait until it wasn't busy, you'd get busy again right? Well, then even if you did start when you had time, you'd stop again the moment you were busy. So, if you learn how to make this work when you're busy, then you'll be able to stick with it no matter what life throws at you. And you want this to be permanent right? Right..."

**(Nudge over the edge):** "And besides, this is geared to give you time back, not cost you time. The first thing we're gonna do is get you to stop doing all the stuff that isn't working."

#### "It's Not About Timing, It's About Priorities"
**You say:** "Totally understand it's a busy time right now. Can I ask you a real question? What are you doing that's more important than this right now? ... okay, well, either you're never going to have time, or something needs to become less important so this can be more important. It sounds like it's more a question of priorities than it is timing. Because let's face it, stuff is always going to come up. So either [goal] is more important than these interruptions, or it's not. And if it's not, that's totally okay. I'd rather you leave knowing you're never going to achieve [goal] rather than suffering all the time hoping it's going to happen. It's either important enough to change your schedule for, or it's not. And if it is, then let's do it."

#### "The Smartphone Close"
**You say:** "Totally understand. I honestly thought the same thing when I started my business."

Pull up their screen time on their phone. Share a personal story. "I told that to my wife. She had enough of my whining and said 'give me your phone,' and showed it. 20 hours per week on social media. She said 'I guess I found you some time,' and handed it back to me."

"Because at the end of the day, you, me, and Jeff Bezos all have the same 24 hours. Would you say that's exactly what started out, that 2 is not having enough time, it's about how we allocate it. And up to this point, you've allocated your time well to get to where you want. It's not about having time. It's about how we allocate it. That's exactly what we're going to show you how to do."

**(Extra nudge):** "And the first thing we're actually going to do is eliminate 90% of the stuff you're doing to make room for what does work, and stop doing stuff that's not working."

> **Author Note: What's a nudge?** They're useful one-liners I've used over the years. They don't count as full closes, but they come as a useful add-on that tipped deals over the edge. They work especially well when someone is right on the edge of making a decision.

#### "The When/Then Close"
**These sound like:**
- "I'll start when I have the time"

**You say:** "Totally understand. This is a classic example of a when/then issue. I used to do this all the time. Can I explain why this thought kept me stuck for almost a decade? Well, it's a false premise. When you say 'I'll do it when I have more time' it's like saying 'When I get healthy, then I'll go to the doctor' or 'When I'm rich, then I'll start saving money.' It flips the order."

### What I Say When They Blame Money

Money blame comes in four flavors:

1. **Not enough value** (have money, don't see value). They point their blame finger at the price. Here, they have the money but avoid buying because they don't see the value.
2. **Can't afford it** (see value, don't have resources). In the far rarer situation, they see the value, but it really does cost more than they've got.
3. **Competitor does it for less.** They point to other people who they claim offer a similar product for less.
4. **Haggling for discount.** They try to get a lower price.

**Key principle:** When somebody says "the price is too high," they aren't saying they don't value your thing. But they're saying they'll avoid deciding until the value exceeds the dollar. For that reason, you should immediately translate "price too high" into "value too low." You want to shift the focus away from the price to the painful stuff before buying and the awesome stuff after.

---

#### Flavor 1: Not Enough Value ("It's too much")

##### "It's Good That It's A Lot"
**You say:** "I understand that it feels like a lot of money, and that's actually why I believe this is the right step for you. Most of our biggest success stories started exactly where you are, seeing this as a big investment."

"And honestly, that's a good thing. The fact that this feels like a big investment means you're more likely to take it seriously. Just imagine: if you follow all the steps I outlined, do you have any doubt you'd succeed? Of course not. The commitment of your hard-earned money is there to hold you accountable. If this were just $1, it might be easy to overlook, just like other things you've tried in the past."

"And let's think about the worst-case scenario. You mentioned losing $XXX feels significant. That tells me you wouldn't take this lightly. The real risk isn't in investing the money; it's walking out the door right now and staying where you are, without achieving the results you want."

**(Nudge):** "If this means a lot to you, I know you will succeed. So, are you ready to take the next step and get started?"

##### "Good Things Aren't Cheap, Cheap Things Aren't Good"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Let me ask you a question. How many free PDFs have you downloaded and made no more money? We had two choices: give the bare minimum for people to be successful and control costs to make it as easy as possible on your part, OR, we could rather than starting with price, start with value, and put so much help, support, and resources to stack the chips in your favor so much it would be unreasonable not to be successful. So yes, it's not cheap, but would you rather be less likely to hit your goal? Right, us neither."

##### "Would You Even Believe Me If It Were Less?"
**You say:** "Totally hear where you're coming from. Let me ask you a question... Would you even believe it could do what I say it could do if it were one third the price? ... (They'll say no)... Then what are we talking about?? (joking tone) Let's get you going."

##### "It's Not A Lot When You Think About It"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Let me ask you a question... Hypothetically, if this got you an extra $10,000/month, would it be worth it? Right, you can do it, you believe it. Well, okay, if I show you success stories of people like you, at what point does it become more unreasonable to believe? 10, 20, 50, 200. At some point, you just have to make the jump."

"If all it does is [single small result], would it be worth it? Right, then the only thing more expensive would be not doing it."

##### "Not What You Make But What You're Gonna Make"
**You say:** "Totally understand. But I think you're right. It's not about how much it's worth. It's about how much you're gonna make and how much you make today. It's about how much you're going to make when we're done."

##### "You're Gonna Spend The Money Either Way"
**You say:** "Totally understand. And let's be honest, in the next 12 months, you're gonna spend this amount of money either way. So the money is already spent. Are you going to spend it on something where you can look back from 12 months from now and say 'that was the best decision of my life?' After all, what is money good for anyways? It only has value when it is exchanged. Your goal is to increase the skill to generate money and find meaningful things to spend it on. But what better to spend it on than something that increases your ability to generate money?"

##### "You Pay The Price Either Way"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Mind if I share a different way of thinking about it? You're gonna pay the price either way. (they nod)... With years of your life to figure it out, or with a payment today to use the time other people have already put in for you. We just pay with the currency we value least. Your time or your money."

"As long as you value your time above minimum wage, you come out way ahead buying your time back at a steep discount and dragging your future earning power into the present."

**(Nudge):** "And at the end of the day, that's why we reverse all the risk and provide you a XX guarantee or your money back. So best case, you accomplish everything we said, or, worst case, you learn tons of stuff for free. Either way, you win."

##### "Some Now or More Later"
**You say:** "Totally understand. If that's the case this is probably the best time to do it then because it's only gonna become more expensive. Doing this costs some now but not doing this will cost more. The amount of time and annuity will only increase as you put it off. So let's get it done now while it's the cheapest and fastest it's gonna be."

##### "Future Favor" Close
**You say:** "It totally is a lot of money. But that's because of the amount of time it will save you. How long have you been wanting this? As long as you can remember, right? If 10 years ago, you could have made a decision that would have made the last 10 years of your life the fittest and best, would that be worth what we're asking? Of course, right? So then let's do future you a favor and have her look back on these next 10 years really happy."

#### Flavor 2: Can't Afford It ("I can't afford it" / "It's not in my budget")

##### "Resourcefulness Not Resources"
**You say:** "So you don't have the money? In other words, the resources to start, is that right? Can I tell you something that blew my mind when I heard it?"

"Do you know the difference between a self-made millionaire when they had nothing and someone who has nothing? Nothing financially. The only difference is that it proves that if they were successful, it wasn't about the resources they had; it's about how resourceful they were."

**(Optional addition if they look like they don't get it):** "Phil Knight, in the story of Nike, was about to lose everything multiple times. He needed a tremendous amount of money and didn't have it. His people already worked for free. He went to his vendors and said, 'You need to pay my payroll for me.' He used his resourcefulness, not his resources."

"When you don't have nothing, it's the easiest excuse to give yourself because every self-made millionaire and every self-made billionaire started with nothing, which puts them in the exact same seat you are. But every self-made millionaire and every self-made billionaire started with something: resourcefulness. When you have power over your life, the question is, how much control do you want over your life? The things you can control are how resourceful you are, not the resources you have."

"So when they want to become wealthier, do you think you want to have more or less resourcefulness? Let your resourcefulness be stronger than your excuses. Let today be the first day your resourcefulness turns to resources. And you let your resources turn to resourcefulness in return."

**(Nudge):** "Let me prove it to you right now: if you needed life-saving surgery, would you find a way to pay for it? Of course you would. So the question is, is saving yourself from the life you're living worth it?"

##### "Had It Worse And Done Better"
**You say:** "Can I tell you something? My dad told me something when I was young. Before he took this job, he had to make ends meet to move out. Do you think anyone else who's been in your situation, who's achieved what you want, has had it worse? (nod)... Yeah. So what's the difference between them and you? They found a way. They were resourceful. And that's exactly the kind of person we want to work with, because those are the people who actually get results."

The key frame: if other people have been in a worse financial position and still found a way to invest in themselves and succeed, then resources aren't the issue. Resourcefulness is.

##### "Everyone Starts At Zero"
**You say:** "Mind if I share something that blew my mind when I heard it? All self-made millionaires have one thing in common: they all started at zero. In fact, many self-made millionaires are right now where you are right now. Which means where you are is not where you'll end up. So if they didn't have resources, they had something else: resourcefulness."

"So when they want to become wealthier, do you think you want to have more or less resourcefulness?"

You wait until they finish then say: I totally agree! Those are all awesome reasons. Couldn't have said it better myself. So now you understand why we aren't the same price, right?" Laugh and ask for the sale again.

##### "Good Fast Cheap"
You can only pick two: good, fast, or cheap. We choose to do it good and right.

##### "Cheap Or What You Need"

In other words, you can get yet another thing that doesn't hurt to buy because you and I both know it won't do anything, or you can invest in yourself and solve this problem for good. If you look at the time and value of [the results you want], don't you think this would be worth it?"

#### Flavor 4: Haggling for Discount ("Can you do it for less?")

**These sound like:**
- "Can you do it for less?"
- "Can you do it for $XXX?"

##### "The We Could Do It For More" Close
**You say:** "Uhh... we could do it for more (ha)."

OR: "We could do it for $XXX (higher price than you quoted)."

**Note:** If someone ever asks for a discount, this works insanely well. As soon as someone changes what they blame, we're getting close to a buyer in power. These objections don't come up with your product's value. They come up when your buyer needs one more obstacle to overcome: price and payment. This takes the focus only to one small obstacle that you can dismantle.

**Additional Note:** As soon as you give up on price, they'll give up on value. If someone says they'd do it for less, you give up all related objections. Then they give you real obstacles. This allows you to focus only on one small obstacle to price and payment.

##### "It's Not, Not The Money"
When they say "It's not the money, I just don't think it's worth it" or "It's not the money..."

**You say:** "If this were free, would you do it? (They say yes)... So it's not, not about the money. OK, well... I'll tell you what, if you're not gonna do it now, either way, you're gonna push out your start date as far as you like, because if you're like one, you might as well lock in the savings now. Either way, you might as well get started."

### What I Say When They Blame Other People

#### When They Blame Decision Makers

**Core Argument:** "I don't want to buy your thing because SOMEONE ELSE prevents me."

**What To Do About It:** We need to help them understand that there are no social obligations, only social consequences. They don't need permission, they need support. They are in control of their lives and as soon as they give that control away, they'll never be able to own their lives.

**Best prevention:** Have decision makers on the call ahead of time (this is in the qualification process). We often schedule re-closes if we know decision-makers won't be on the call. But if you're in a sales environment selling cheaper stuff relatively, this is the best sequence I know of.

This is a series of questions and responses. Instead of being a couple of one-liners, this is a sequence. Meaning, at any point someone can agree instead of the prospect. The prospect has their own off ramps and destinations in this sequence.

**These sound like:**
- "I have to talk to my spouse/business partner/admin/grandma/employer/etc."
- "I can't make this decision alone"

**Step 1: What if they say no?**

**You say:** "What if they don't give you permission? Or, what if your spouse says no?"

If they say: "I'd do it anyway."
**You say:** "Awesome. Then let's get you started."

If instead they say: "Then I wouldn't do it."

**Step 2: What would they object to specifically?**

**You say:** "What part specifically do you think they wouldn't approve of? The price? The program? Or me? haha."

If they give you an answer, off-ramp and handle that specific obstacle. This diverts focus to a different obstacle that you can overcome.

**Step 3: If they know their spouse would not be happy, explain why their spouse would stop them.**

"Explain... why would they stop you? I already read it with a partner, is their partner's problem fine with bringing it up? If the roles were reversed and your husband needed this and you reversed the roles, would you support him? What we're trying to do is get them to make the argument that their spouse would surely say yes and approve of this if asked."

"Remind them that they are their own person and should make decisions for themselves."

**Step 4: Ask for support, not permission.**

**You say:** "I totally get it. Can I offer a different perspective? (yes)... You're not asking for permission. You're asking for support. Because let's play it out. It's your life, not theirs. What happens is if you give that power to them from two years from now and then, that's when resentment sets in. And that's not fair to you or to them. I'm not saying you shouldn't explain the decision to them, you just need to ask for their support."

**(Nudge) Role-play:** "If your wife/husband needed this and reversed the roles, would you support her/him? If she came to you and said 'I need this, I want to make my dreams happen,' would you support her? Then there's no reason to believe he/she wouldn't support you."

**If they still say: "My partner and I always make decisions together"**

**You say:** "Totally get it. Can I offer a potentially different perspective? (yes)... I think you're looking for support, not permission. Because let's play it out. It's your life."

**If they still say no...**

**You say:** "Tell ya what... sign up, I'll tear the contract up but lock you in your spot, and second, I'll delay the payment 24-72 hours so you get time to talk to your partner. Fair enough? This way, you won't lose your spot in case they're cool with it, and you still get time to talk. Most states require a three-day guarantee anyways, so you're not changing much."

> **Pro Tip: Three-Day Guarantee.** Most states require this anyways, so you're not giving up much. Put a card down, delay payment 24-72 hours, and you still have time to chat to them. This is a downsell you can lock them in with but still have the "stop the clock" option if they can truly not decide alone.

**Go for a humorous hypothetical (EXTREME NAME = PARTNER'S NAME):** "Hear me out... if you sign up and he/she gets upset and tells you he/she doesn't want you to get in shape... like, 'Girl, I don't want you to get in shape. I want you to stay overweight. Put those sweatpants on. Eat Cheetos, wipe the Cheetos fingers on your sweatpants. And eat ice cream. I don't want you to live ten years less long. I want you to not have a heart attack. Right? If he says that to you, you have him call me.' They're cracking up normally. Okay, okay, listen. Sign up because he just wants you to be out of shape. And he does tell you that, just let me know. I'll tear this up. But if he's not going to say that, then he's asking for permission rather than forgiveness, right? (ha)"

**(Nudge):** "Sometimes it's better to ask for forgiveness than permission."

#### When They Blame Bad Experiences With Other Products

**These sound like:**
- "I've been burned before"
- "I tried something like this already and it didn't work"

##### "Don't Let It Burn You Twice"
**You say:** "Totally understand your reservations. I'd feel the same way too if the same thing happened to me. But maybe this is worth considering. If you don't do this, then you let that bad decision burn you twice. It burned you once when you made the poor investment, but it'll burn you twice if that bad decision prevents you from making a good one."

"It would be like having a bad eighth-grade boyfriend or girlfriend and being like, you know what, men/women aren't for me. All coaches suck, right? There are good coaches and bad coaches. There are crazy-ass chicks and crazy-ass dudes. There are also good ones. The only thing worse than making a bad investment is letting one bad investment stop you from making a good one. Don't let that bad decision burn you twice."

**Other People Conclusion:** Remember, in the best situation you talk to both of them at once. Or they come to you with permission-in-hand. But if you don't, this gives you the best tools. People who blame others for their decision avoidance can be a pain to deal with. But it's totally doable. How you deliver your message matters as much as the message itself. You can have the perfect script, but unless you've got big rapport and correct tonality you will flop. They're not the prisoners they make themselves out to be. So if you can get them to laugh at the silliness of blaming others, and that they really can make decisions for themselves, you'll get a genuine close.

### When They Take The Blame (Self)

**Core argument:** "I avoid buying your thing because I'm scared of making a mistake."

**What To Do About It:** They know you can solve their problem but either don't like how your product goes about it (preferences) or they get scared around new things or they've tried to solve this problem before and failed. In either case they use their own fear of getting a bad result as a way to avoid making decisions that can actually get them results.

We help them decide by explaining that a past failure should never make them miss an opportunity for future success. And really, the biggest mistake of all is not making a decision. If they avoid making a decision it guarantees they stay the same, which is NOT where they wanna be.

People hate making decisions because they hate making mistakes. So your further responsibility is to show them that avoiding the decision is the biggest mistake of all. It does the opposite of what they want, and it takes them further from where they wanna go.

Two flavors of self-blame:
1. When they blame their own preferences ("It's not for me")
2. When they blame rushed decisions ("I need to think about it" / "This is happening too fast")

---

#### When They Blame Their Own Preferences

**"I want your results, but I need to get them my way."** A prospect may value the results you offer, but avoid deciding because of what it takes to get them. Like they would, but only if they could change core parts about the product and still get the offered result. The "I want to have my cake and eat it too" argument.

**What To Do About It:** We explain that doing things their way probably led them to their current problems. And that means their preferences actually get in the way of achieving the result they want. They solve this problem either by sacrificing the result they want or the preferences that keep them from getting it. And we help them decide which is more important to them.

**These sound like:**
- "This isn't right for me"

##### "Isolate And Solve"
**You say:** "What specifically would make it for you vs. not for you?"

Always start here. Isolate the specific issue so you can solve it.

##### "We Vote With Our Dollars About The Things That We Care About"
**You say:** "Totally get it. Can I offer a different lens? (yes)... Well... we vote with our dollars about the things that we care about, right? If you show me what someone is spending their money and their time on, I can tell you what their future is. I'll tell you an interesting story."

"I had somebody who was just like you who was like, 'You know what, I don't want to do this either.' I told her this story. Can I tell it to you? Yes."

"I was with Leila and we went to Sephora, which is a makeup store. I saw these two little girls not older than 13. They were giggly and so excited. A sales clerk helped them out explaining the products to them, 'Okay, this is eyeliner, this is lipstick, this blush and so on.' The girls were so excited. Then, right before she hands it to them she says, 'Girls, you have to remember, now that you're getting older and you're becoming women, you need to start budgeting for this stuff. You're going to start buying this every month, so remember, you've got to save some money for this so you can have it.' So the young girls are thinking, 'Yes, we're becoming women now, and these are the things that women do.'"

"When you have a new identity, you have new priorities. You've had priorities that are aligned with your old identity. There's the you that goes out, the you that drinks, the you that does the blah blah blah, spends money on stuff you shouldn't do, spends time on stuff you shouldn't be spending time on. There's the you that you want to be. Right now, we can draw the line in the sand and be like, 'Do you want to keep walking down the road that you have been walking?' If you don't, then you have to step into a new identity. With that new identity comes new priorities."

**B2B Alternate:** "People who want to be rich spend money on themselves. They spend money on education. They invest in their skill sets because they want to align their identity with the identity of those who came before them, who've done the things they want to do. Just like those little girls stepping into that identity, we too must step into the identity that we want to be. Does that make sense?"

##### "Pain Of Change Is Less Than The Pain Of Staying The Same"
**You say:** "Can I share something I've learned in life? People don't change until they fail. We change when the pain of staying the same is greater than the pain of change. So what I'm hearing is that you like what's going on in your life now more than the pain of change. And if that's true, then you are ordering yourself to keep what you have. But from everything you've told me, that doesn't seem like what you want. Or is there something else you're afraid of?"

##### "You Gotta Change To Change"
**You say:** "Totally hear you. We've gotten an internal motto for successful customers. Mind if I share it with you? (nod) You gotta change to change. What you have been doing has been getting you what you've been getting, and you gotta change to change. I remember I would go through meal plans with people, and they'd be like, 'Well, can we just make that for breakfast?' or 'Can we just eat what I eat for breakfast?' 'That breakfast makes you look the way you look. That's the way your bank account looks. It makes you look the way it looks. The question is when the pain of staying the same is greater than the pain of change. Change is gonna hurt to change because the pain of staying the same is greater than the pain of change."

"If it is, then let's do it. If it's not, then things may need to get worse before it hurts enough to get better."

**(Nudge):** "Listen, we know this works, this way. And if we change the variables, we change the outcomes. You gotta change to change."

##### "Change The Variables, Change The Outcome"
**You say:** "We TOTALLY could change this thing you don't like, but... then you'd probably also get an outcome you don't like. So I wouldn't feel ethical offering that to you. Here's the main point: we know these variables, in this order, create this outcome. And if you change the variables, you change the outcome. You want this outcome, right? Then this is the best way we know how to get you there."

##### "The Mechanic Close"
When they ask a very specific question or a question that you don't know the answer to that's personal to them.

**You say:** "That's a great question. I'd need to know more about your business and know more to schedule a person to specifically help you. It would be kinda like asking the mechanic what's wrong with your car before bringing it in. The only way we'd be able to answer that is after we get to know you/your body/your business better. And anyone who does give you an answer to that question beforehand, run the other way, because there's no way they could know that."

##### "Surgeon Secretary Close"
**Alternate:** "Honestly, for me to answer that would be like a secretary at a heart surgeon's office telling you what the doctor is gonna do. It's beyond my scope and frankly, it would do you a disservice. But to get you a very detailed answer, the moment we get you in, we'll put together a thorough plan."

#### When They Blame Rushed Decisions

**These sound like:**
- "This is happening too fast"
- "Let me think about it"
- "I can't make a decision right now"
- "I'm not sure I want to do this"
- "I just need more information"
- "Can you send me an email with all this summarized?"
- "I'll call you tomorrow"

##### "What Would Make It A No?"
**You say:** "Totally understand. I don't want you to feel rushed. If you don't mind me asking, what would make this a no?"

This is especially useful for people who seem to be indecisive. Rather than getting them to say yes, try and get them to decide on what makes it a no. If they can't decide what makes it a no, or the reasons they give are weak, it's much easier to point to the idea that they're simply unsure. And that makes them just like every other great customer you've ever sold.

##### "Don't Repeat The Same Mistake That Got You Here"
**You say:** "I definitely don't want you to feel an ounce of urgency, but... do you think maybe you're in this situation because you've delayed making the decision? Do you think it would be a bigger mistake to make the same decision again?"

##### "How Much Has Not Making This Decision Cost You?"
**You say:** "Totally understand. May I share something I shared with someone yesterday that helped them reframe the decision? (nod) Great. I think the question isn't what it costs you, but how much it has cost you to not decide up to this point? Right? The cost of inaction. I think that when we think about our lives in reverse, we never regret the things that we did; we regret the things we didn't do, the opportunities that we let pass by. And think about all the years of your life you've already lived that you could've had this improvement in your life. Isn't that worth starting as soon as possible?"

##### "Another Year of Almost"
**You say:** "I hear you. Can I share something our CEO said to our company that changed my perspective on taking action? (nod) No one wants another year of 'almost'... do you? Here's what I mean... another year of almost hitting your goals, of almost getting to 10k a month, of almost hiring the team that you wanted, of almost retiring your wife or husband, or almost moving into the neighborhood that you wanted to move into, or almost being able to pay for your kids' recreational sports rather than not being able to because you can't afford it. Well, if you're tired of another year of almost, then we can't do what we're doing to get here for another year because we're going to get another year of almost. And I'm pretty sure that's not where you wanna be. So what about a 'this is my year' kinda year for a change?"

##### "It Doesn't Take Time To Make Decisions, It Takes Information"
**You say:** "Let's think as wise as I think you are. After all, you're not going to go home, sit in the rocking chair, stare at the wall and think 'hmm, am I going to do this program?' Of course not. You're gonna get in your car, and then three, four, five days from now, you're going to put on that old pair of jeans and it's not going to fit, and you're like, crap. Right there, you'll associate the pain and let's just make the decision now because the reality is that it doesn't take time to make decisions. It takes information. I'm the only source of information you got, so let's talk. I'm here to help."

##### "The Gameplan Close"
**You say:** "I totally get why you'd hesitate to make a decision. Mind if I share something that might make you feel better about it? (yes) Great... has anyone walked you through how to decide on an X program before? No? Well I can walk you through it right now so you can know you made the right decision. There are three things that we got to understand:"

"1) Do you think this will help you get you closer to your goals? Yes or no?" (They're going to say yes.)

"2) Do you have access to the amount of money to get started? Yes or no?" (They're going to say yes. If they say no, ask if they know someone who has access. Resourcefulness over resources. Because the real world is if I was giving you a Ferrari right now for five thousand dollars, you'd find the money. If your landlord needed you to come up with ten thousand dollars because you had back rent and payroll, you'd find the money. So, find the money, let's do it.)

"3) Do you think I/we can help you do this better than on your own? Yes or no?" (They're going to say yes.)

##### "This Isn't A Fast Decision"
**You say:** "Totally understand you don't want to make a fast decision. Have you considered that maybe this isn't a fast decision at all? After all, you've already made it this far. You saw an ad. Clicked. Opted in. Set time aside in your schedule. Showed up for the call. And we've been talking for X minutes. You did all this stuff because it's important to you... you don't wanna stop digging now. All the best stuff is ahead!"

"And you've already been thinking about some version of this decision years ago. You're just doing it now."

#### Guarantee Closes

The following closes only work if you offer a guarantee. They're very effective because they underpin the obvious truth that the prospect takes on greater risk by choosing not to buy than choosing to buy.

##### "Both Options Are Risk Free"
**You say:** "Super reasonable. That being said, I've got good news and I've got bad news. The bad news is, you can't really make an informed decision until you've actually tried it. Everything else is guessing. The good news is, you can try it risk free. So, I'm not even asking you to make a decision right now. I'm just actually asking you to make an informed decision, which you can only do once you try it. If after 30 days I'm not what I said, this product doesn't do what I told you, I didn't fulfill my promise, or you don't think it's going to work for you, you let me know, I'll give you the money back. No questions asked."

##### "Decadere"
**You say:** "Totally understand. Can I share something cool with you that helped me make a decision a while back? (nod) Do you know where the word 'decide' comes from? It's Latin for de-cadere which means to kill off, or cut off. Doing this is a decision. Not doing this is a decision. So the question is, which future are you cutting off? The one where you get closer to your goals or the one where you get further away?"

##### "Closer Or Further"
**You say:** "If you keep doing what you're currently doing for five years, what does life look like? Do you think making this decision is going to help you get closer or further from your goal? That's it. Because if we keep making decisions that keep getting us closer, we will get there eventually. But if we make decisions that get us further away, we'll never get there. Do you think that this is going to get you closer to your goal than what you're currently doing? Yes. What are we waiting for? We don't need to be snipers; we need to be directionally right. We do that long enough, we'll get there. Does that make sense?"

**(Nudge):** "That helps you too because we have this fear of perfectionism. 'Is this going to be the one?' Probably not, but it will get you way closer. I can tell you there's no one program/service/product that changed my life, but the decision to invest in the first one changed my life forever. Does that make sense?"

## Summary of What We've Addressed (By Blame Type)

**Time:** They can make better use of their time. And besides, the best time is now because they're always going to be busy anyway. They benefit from time saved or spent on this anyway.

**Money:** We appeal to their resourcefulness. They'll spend the money anyway, so they may as well spend it on something valuable. And when they see the value exceeds the price, they'll figure out a way. "Price too high" really means "value too low." If it appears our offer has too little value, then we do a better job making the value exceed the price.

**Price/Fit:** Remember, "price too high" really means "value too low." If it appears our offer has too little value, then we do a better job making the value beat the price.

**Authority:** Catching flak from a partner for doing something that benefits them. Seriously? This is about realizing they already have the support they need. They can own their life and their decisions.

**Bad Experiences:** The fact they were burned before means they now know what to look for and what to avoid. That makes them more qualified, not less. Don't let one bad decision burn you twice.

**Self/Preferences/Avoidance:** Avoiding decisions by blaming the self comes in two basic flavors, and we'll address both. It's closing time. People hate making decisions because they hate making mistakes. But avoiding the decision is the biggest mistake of all.

## Ethical Framework

Three assumptions moving forward:
1. The product will get them to their goal the way they want to go
2. You, and others, will support them
3. It will work for them, not just everyone else

## Transition Between Blame Layers

When someone changes what they blame, you're making progress. For example, they start with "I don't have the money" (Circumstances), then shift to "I need to talk to my spouse" (Other People), then shift to "I'm just not sure" (Self). Each shift means you've resolved the previous layer.

**What to do when they swap objections:**
1. Recognize the swap as progress, not regression
2. Acknowledge the new objection with validation ("Totally understand")
3. Apply the appropriate close for the new blame category
4. Continue until you reach a genuine decision (yes or no)

**When someone gives up unrelated objections after you address one,** they're giving you real obstacles. This allows you to focus on the one remaining thing preventing the sale.

**The flow typically goes:**
- Circumstances (outermost layer, easiest to address)
- Other People (middle layer, requires more rapport)
- Self (innermost layer, requires the most trust and care)

Each time you peel back a layer, you're getting closer to the real reason. The real reason is almost always in the "Self" layer.

## Usage Notes for AI Sales Script Generation

When generating sales scripts, closing sequences, or objection-handling frameworks:

1. **Match the close to the objection type.** Always diagnose which blame layer the prospect is operating from before selecting a close.
2. **Stack closes, don't repeat them.** If one close doesn't land, use a different one from the same category, not the same words again.
3. **Always lead with acknowledgment.** Every response to an objection starts with validation ("Totally understand," "I hear you," "That's a great point").
4. **Use the prospect's own words.** Mirror their language back to them before reframing.
5. **Include nudges for edge cases.** When a prospect is 80% there, a nudge is more effective than a full close.
6. **Adapt tone to context.** B2C fitness scripts can use the humorous hypotheticals. B2B scripts should lean on the "Mechanic Close," "Gameplan Close," and "Resourcefulness" frameworks.
7. **Never argue.** Guide through questions and statements that help them convince themselves.
8. **Preserve the ethical frame.** Every script should assume the product genuinely helps the prospect reach their goal.

## Quick Reference Principles

1. **The money is in the maybes.** Most revenue comes from bucket three. Train for it.
2. **Follow the finger of blame.** Map every objection to Circumstances, Other People, or Self. Peel layers.
3. **Acknowledge, never disagree.** Validate first, then reframe. You sit on the same side of the table.
4. **The reason they can't is the reason they must.** Their biggest excuse is usually their biggest chain.
5. **Once they say yes, shut up.** Every additional word risks undoing the sale.
6. **Closing is the last 20% where 80% of the difference is made.** Sales teams routinely double close rates with effective closing training.
7. **You only need two or three closes per obstacle.** Master the ones that feel natural to you.
8. **Expect people to say no more than once.** They did nothing wrong. It's expected. Be patient.
9. **When someone swaps objections, you're closing correctly.** Keep peeling back the layers.
10. **Record your sales calls.** Treat each sale like game tape. Focus on improving one thing at a time.
