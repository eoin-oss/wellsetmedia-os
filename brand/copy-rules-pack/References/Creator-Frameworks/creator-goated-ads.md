## Core Principle

Ads hit a ceiling not because you saturated the market, but because you saturated your ad quality. The solution is volume: 150 to 750 ad variations per week, assembled from interchangeable parts.

**The Old Spice lesson:** Old Spice ads converted people who were never Old Spice buyers. The ad was good enough to convert 70% of the market. That is an ad that was not capped. The better your ads, the bigger the audience they will convert. You scale ads by making better ads. And you only make better ads by making more of them.

**The real bottleneck:** "You haven't saturated Facebook. But you have gotten all the low hanging fruit. You've hit a wall with your ad quality, not with the market."

## Eugene Schwartz Awareness Pyramid

As you scale spend, you move from the smallest but highest-converting audiences at the bottom to progressively larger but lower-converting audiences at the top. Target your hooks to the right awareness level.

| Level | Awareness | Hook Type | What the Customer Knows | Example Hook |
|-------|-----------|-----------|------------------------|--------------|
| 1 (Bottom) | Most Aware | Offer-driven | Knows your product, only needs the deal | "50% off our best-selling XYZ for the rest of the month" |
| 2 | Product-Aware | Proof-driven | Knows what you sell but not sure it is right for them | "Discover why 10,000 people chose XYZ to solve their [problem] last month" |
| 3 | Solution-Aware | Promise-driven | Knows the result they want but does not know your product provides it | "The fastest way to [desired result] - introducing XYZ" |
| 4 | Problem-Aware | Pain-driven | Senses they have a problem but does not know a solution exists | "Tired of [specific problem]? There's a better way" |
| 5 (Top) | Completely Unaware | Curiosity-driven | Does not know they have a problem or need | "The hidden danger in your daily routine that's costing you [money/time/health]" |

### B2C Example: Weight Loss Shake (XFast)

| Level | Hook |
|-------|------|
| Most Aware | "XFast's new formula: Now with 25% more protein - Same great taste!" |
| Product-Aware | "Why 9 out of 10 XFast users reached their goal weight within 3 months" |
| Solution-Aware | "Lose 15 pounds in 30 days with our scientifically proven meal replacement system" |
| Problem-Aware | "Frustrated with crash diets that don't last? There's a sustainable way to shed pounds" |
| Unaware | "The hidden hormonal imbalance that's making 1 in 3 Americans gain weight" |

### B2B Example: Marketing Agency (Digital Boost)

| Level | Hook |
|-------|------|
| Most Aware | "DigitalBoost: Now offering social media management at 20% off for new clients" |
| Product-Aware | "See how DigitalBoost increased ROI by 150% for 5 different industries" |
| Solution-Aware | "Double your online sales in 6 months with our data-driven marketing strategies" |
| Problem-Aware | "Is your website getting sales? You might be missing these crucial elements" |
| Unaware | "The unexpected way your business is losing $1000s each month in untapped revenue" |

### Awareness Level Key Insight

If 90% of your hooks land in the "Most Aware" bucket, spread them out to capture a bigger slice. When in doubt, go broader. You still catch warm audiences while attracting some of the colder ones too.

### The Subway Caveat

A national brand like Subway can run an offer-driven hook ("$5 Foot Long") to everyone because they have already spent hundreds of millions getting everyone to know who they are. If you are a no-name competitor with a product that needs education, leading with an offer-driven hook to a national audience is the closest thing to burning money. Context matters: these are frameworks, not rigid rules.

### Pro Tip: The Widest Hooks Possible

Memes or meme-like content attract the largest percentage of your audience. If you target a narrower group than "everyone," they will have their own culture-specific memes you can advertise with. A relevant meme for a specific audience works like a moth to a flame. It will explode the number of eyeballs exposed to your ad.

## The Ad Assembly Process

Ads are not created. They are assembled from three interchangeable components. Six-pack abs are made in the kitchen, not in the gym. Ads are made in research, not in recording. 90% of the work in advertising is in the preparation to record, not in the recording itself.

### Time Allocation for Prep

| Component | Prep Time | Volume Per Session |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Hooks | 80% | 50 hooks |
| Meat | 20% | 3 to 5 scripts |
| CTAs | ~0% | 1 to 3 variations |

**Output math:** 50 hooks x 3-5 meats x 1-3 CTAs = 150 to 750 ads per week.

**Time investment:** An hour or two for hooks, maybe an hour to record 3-5 meats, five minutes for a few CTAs. The rest is prep. This is how you scale advertising.

**Defense benefit:** No one can figure out your top-performing ads when you have hundreds of variations running simultaneously. Good offense and good defense against copycats.

## Component 1: Hooks (80% of Effort)

If someone does not make it past the hook, nothing else matters. Hooks break into two categories: winners-based hooks (bread and butter, weekly production) and expansion hooks (to reach new, broader audiences).

### Where to Find Winning Hooks (Priority Order)

1. **Your own previous winning ads** - Literally past winners that you reuse. Over time you develop a stable of winners. You would be amazed how much runway one good hook can give you. (Skip if running ads for the first time.)
2. **Your own free content that performed well** - Cross-platform hooks. Find hooks that work on one platform and use them on another. These have the highest likelihood of converting. Grab from longs, shorts, tweets, emails. Look at content that performed well, chop the hook off the front, and use it in an ad that may or may not be related to the original content.
3. **Other people's ads you have saved** - Save all ads you like and write down the hooks they use. If broad enough, use them. Keep an album on your phone.
4. **Other people's top-performing free content** - Look for content with tons of views in your space. Snag the hook and test it.
5. **Platform-specific ad libraries** - Last place to look. Pros: you can see a bajillion ads from any business. Cons: hard to figure out which ads perform well enough to justify modeling. Most people do not know what they are doing. Big ad libraries become a fun place for ideas more than they guarantee winners. Look at companies you know run great paid ads. Key point: winning hooks in one industry often work in others.

### Writing Expansion Hooks to Enter New Markets

When you have "capped" your existing ad capacity and refreshed your creative, you may need to expand the audience you target with your hooks.

**The trap:** If you continue to write hooks that focus only on your offer or proof, you will convert a small slice of a larger audience very well. To grab a bigger slice, meet the audience where they are at.

**How to expand:**
- Write hooks spread across ALL awareness levels, not just Most Aware
- If 90% of your hooks land in one bucket, redistribute
- When in doubt, go a little broader
- Go through your own past winners, then write 50 or so hooks spread across awareness buckets

## Component 2: Ad Meat (20% of Effort)

The meat is the part of the ad that fulfills the hook. The creative aligns with the hook, the hook aligns with the audience's awareness level. The meat tends to educate the customer about: the offer, the product, the solution, the problem, or makes them aware of the phenomenon that is your product.

Meat rotates less often because fewer people see it. You do not "use it up" as often. 3 to 5 per weekly recording session is more than enough.

### Five Proven Ad Formats (Work Across All Industries)

These work across services, education, physical products, brick and mortar, software, and every other industry. Mix and match them.

#### Format 1: Demonstration Ads

Show the product or service in action.

| Sub-type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Live use / Reactions | Show real people using the product live |
| Unboxing | Reveal what the customer gets |
| Comparisons / Before-and-Afters | Side-by-side proof of transformation |
| High-production hero ads | Polished, fast-paced ads like Dollar Shave Club and Old Spice |
| Product demonstration | Literally show the lead magnet or product while explaining it |
| Service demonstration | Show the results of the service (e.g., a packed gym) |

#### Format 2: Testimonial Ads

Let others speak for you.

| Sub-type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| User-generated content (UGC) | Customers create the ad themselves |
| Founder direct-to-camera | You make the case personally |
| Podcast style | Clipped from a recording session, conversational feel |
| Professional testimonials | Polished customer stories |
| Raw iPhone-style testimonials | Unpolished, authentic customer recordings |
| Walk-n-talk rant style | Casual rant format, showing results at actual locations |
| Parade of proof (group) | Multiple testimonials compiled together - [CREATOR_NAME]'s favorite group testimonial style |
| Man-on-the-street interviews | Spontaneous interviews with real people |
| Influencer / celebrity collabs | Partner with recognized voices |
| Lifecycle ads | Show the customer journey over time (see Marketing Machine Playbook) |

#### Format 3: Education Ads

Teach something valuable.

| Sub-type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Whiteboard explainer | Draw and explain concepts on a whiteboard |
| How-to / tutorial | Step-by-step instructional content |
| Listicle videos | Numbered tips or points |
| High-performing organic content | Repurpose content that already worked organically |
| Explainer videos | Break down a concept or process |

#### Format 4: Story Ads

Pull people in with narrative.

| Sub-type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Storytelling / narrative | Origin stories, customer transformation stories |
| Lifestyle | Show the aspirational life (use carefully) |
| Warnings and opportunities | Alert the audience to risks or upside |
| Documentary style | Longer-form, journalistic feel |
| Skits | Short comedic or dramatic scenes |
| Brand manifestos | Statement of values and mission |
| Problem-solution | Frame a problem then present your answer |
| Humorous / comedy | Make them laugh, then sell |
| Emotional / sentimental | Pull on heartstrings |

#### Format 5: Faceless Ads

No person on camera required.

| Sub-type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Screenshot compilations | Screenshots of customer comments, texts, or DMs run as the ad itself |
| Text-only | Pure text on screen |
| Slideshows | Image sequences with text overlays |
| Animations | Motion graphics explaining a concept |
| Cartoon ads | Illustrated characters and scenarios |
| Visual effect-based ads | Eye-catching VFX to stop the scroll |

**When stuck on what format to use for the meat, refer back to this list.**

## Component 3: CTA (Minimal Effort)

If your ad got them interested, your audience will have huge motivation for a tiny time. Take advantage. Tell them exactly what to do next. S-P-E-L-L it out.

### The Five Elements of a Sound CTA

A good CTA shows or tells:

1. **What to do:** "Take advantage of this great offer by..."
2. **How to do it:** "...tapping the button on the bottom of your screen..."
3. **When to do it:** "...before it expires..."
4. **What they get for doing it:** "...and you'll get $1000 of free stuff..."
5. **What happens next (optional):** "...delivered straight to your inbox."

Element 5 is more important with lead magnets and multi-step sales processes.

### CTA Rules

- **Clear beats clever.** Always. Spell out exactly what to do: click this button, call this number, reply with "YES," go to this website, scan this QR code.
- **Show AND tell.** Do not just say the CTA. Demonstrate what it looks like to follow the next step. Show what happens when they click the button, where they fill out information, what clicking submit looks like. This seems obvious and basic but almost no one does it. It provides the ultimate congruence.
- **Add urgency, scarcity, guarantees, bonuses** where applicable. These get even more people to take action.
- **Stick with winners.** Once you find 1-2 CTAs that work, mostly stick with them.
- **Test by isolation.** Run three identical hook + meat combos to the same audience, only changing the CTA.

### CTA Example Variations for a 14-Day Free Trial

1. "Start free on the next page"
2. "Grab your 14-day free trial on the next page"
3. "Get started on the next page free"

### CTA Best Practice: Show What Happens Next

Round out your CTA by visually demonstrating the next steps. Show each screen on a digital board, swipe through them live. Walk through the actual process of clicking, filling out info, and submitting.

- **Urgency add-on:** The last 5 seconds of a top-performing ad can bring urgency. This single addition has crushed for [CREATOR_NAME]'s campaigns.

### CTA Bottom Line

A sound CTA has never broken a campaign. But NO CTA has. More people will do what you want more often if you tell them to do it. Spend the least time on CTAs, but always include one that follows the five elements.

## The Kaleidoscope Method (Scaling)

Once you find winners, double down. Reuse winning hooks to create more variations.

### The Process

1. Find your best-performing ads
2. Chop them into hook, meat, CTA
3. Run them through the kaleidoscope: 50 new hooks paired with proven meats and CTAs
4. Generate 3-5 variations on the meat
5. Test variations to find the next winner
6. Repeat weekly

### Scaling Mindset

- New customers enter your market every day. You owe it to them to serve the highest-converting ad possible.
- Do not get bored repeating the same stuff. It will be the first time they see it.
- In the beginning, you will not know which ads will hit. Make a lot of variations. These appeal to many different segments.
- Over time, a few ads will wildly outperform the rest. Double down on what works.
- Winning hooks are the key to unlocking your customer's wallet.

### Defense Benefit

No one can figure out your top-performing ads when you have hundreds of variations running simultaneously. This is good offense and good defense against copycats.

## GOATed Ads Playbook Cheat Sheet

**Step 1 - Identify awareness level.** Figure out the level of awareness you are targeting: 1) Unaware, 2) Problem Aware, 3) Solution Aware, 4) Product Aware, 5) Most Aware.

**Step 2 - Write 50 hooks.** Either divide hooks into buckets that hit each awareness level, or focus the majority broader than your current hooks. To find inspiration:
1. Look at your prior best ads
2. Look at other top ads from different industries (gold here)
3. Look at your past top short content
4. Look at others' best short content
5. Save your favorite ads to review on writing day

**Step 3 - Write 3-5 meats.** Pick from the five formats:
1. **Demonstration:** Showcasing product/service, unboxing, comparisons, before-and-afters, high-production hero ads
2. **Testimonial:** UGC, founder direct-to-camera, podcast style, professional testimonials, raw iPhone style, walk-n-talk rants, group testimonials, parade of proof, lifecycle ads, man-on-the-street, influencer collabs
3. **Education:** Explainer videos, how-to/tutorials, whiteboard explainer, listicle videos, high-performing organic content
4. **Story:** Storytelling/narrative, lifestyle, emotional, humorous, brand manifesto, problem-solution, warnings and opportunities
5. **Faceless:** Screenshots of comments/texts, text-only, slideshows, animations, cartoon ads, visual-effect-based ads

**Step 4 - Write 1-3 CTAs.** Both show and tell them what to do next:
1. What/how to do it
2. When to do it
3. What they get for doing it
4. What happens next (optional)
5. Demonstrate what it looks like visually

**Step 5 - Find a new winner.** Repeat steps 1-4. Double down on what works. Scale through volume and iteration.

## Quick Reference

1. **80/20 rule:** Spend 80% of ad prep time on hooks. If someone does not make it past the hook, nothing else matters.
2. **Volume wins:** 150 to 750 ad variations per week from assembly-line production beats 5-10 "perfect" ads per month.
3. **Awareness targeting:** Match hook type to audience awareness level. Broaden hooks to scale spend.
4. **Assemble, do not create:** Hooks, meat, and CTAs are modular. Mix and match proven components.
5. **Double down on winners:** When you find a winning hook, make 50 more like it. New customers enter the market every day.
6. **Research over recording:** 90% of ad work is prep. Six-pack abs are made in the kitchen, not in the gym.
7. **Cross-platform hooks:** Hooks that work on one platform or ad method often crush on another. These have the highest likelihood of converting.
8. **Cross-industry hooks:** Winning hooks in one industry often work in others.
9. **Memes as hooks:** Culture-specific memes for a niche work like a moth to a flame. Test them.
10. **Show the CTA:** Demonstrate the next step visually. Show every screen, every click. Almost no one does this and it works.
11. **Never skip CTA:** A sound CTA has never broken a campaign, but no CTA has broken many.
12. **Copycats cannot keep up:** Hundreds of variations running simultaneously means no one can reverse-engineer your winners.
