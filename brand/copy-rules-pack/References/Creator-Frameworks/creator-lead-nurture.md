> Source: "$100M Playbook: Lead Nurture" by [CREATOR_NAME] (Acquisition.com). Reference material for email-flows skill.

## Core Concept

Lead nurture maximizes the percentage of interested leads who actually show up to buy. It sits between advertising (generating leads) and sales (closing them). If they do not show, they cannot buy.

**Origin story:** [CREATOR_NAME] built software called ALAN (Artificial Lead Automation & Nurture) that managed 4,000+ appointments per day across portfolio companies generating 20,000+ leads per day. When COVID hit and his gym-focused software had to pivot to other businesses, he hired a data scientist (Vlad, winner of Russia's machine-learning Olympics) to analyze a million rows of appointment data. Vlad found four data points that correlated to positive appointments. Those four data points became the Four Pillars.

**Key metrics:**
- **Schedule Rate:** % of engaged leads who book an appointment
- **Show Rate:** % of scheduled leads who actually show up
- **Throughput:** Total % of engaged leads who show up (schedule rate x show rate)

**Example math:**
- Leads: 100
- Schedule Rate: 50% = 50 scheduled appointments
- Show Rate: 50% = 25 shown appointments
- Throughput: 25% (25 shows out of 100 leads)

A 20-40% increase in show rates translates directly to revenue. At 20% margins, that means 2-3x the profit of your business in year one alone.

**Why most business owners ignore this:**
1. They think they cannot do anything about it
2. They do not know what to do about it
3. They think they have more important stuff to do

Only #3 is sometimes valid. It is rare to get better returns than getting more people showing up to buy.

**The invisible loss:** The money you did not make is money you lost. Once you see show rate as one of the most important numbers in your business, everything changes. The things in this playbook make it go up fast and risk-free.

**Scope:** This covers medium-term lead nurture, maximizing 30-day show rates. The time after leads show interest but before they buy. Long-term follow-up (emails, podcasts, content) is a separate topic.

## The Four Pillars

| Pillar | Definition | Core Principle |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **Availability** | Number of open appointment slots | If leads cannot schedule, they cannot show |
| **Speed** | How fast you respond and schedule | Money loves speed |
| **Personalization** | Making communication relevant to each lead | Personalize, don't pressure |
| **Volume** | Number of reach-out attempts | Volume negates luck |

**The four pillars combined:** If you reach out to a lead the moment they express interest, make it convenient for them to talk, frame the conversation as beneficial to them, and do it as many times as you can, more people will schedule and show up to buy your thing, no matter what you sell. Some industries have higher show rates than others, but any business can become best in class within their industry.

**Foundation principle:** "You must first become consistent before you can become exceptional."

**There are no silver bullets, only golden BBs.** Getting leads to show up is not one thing. It is many things done together.

## Pillar I: Availability

**"If leads cannot schedule, they do not show."**

The biggest single predictor of show rates. Data analysis of 4,000+ appointments per day showed an almost perfect correlation between availability and throughput. On an absolute basis, businesses with the most time slots had the most schedules, shows, and purchases. There are diminishing returns, but large increases still exist at the upper quartiles of availability.

### Why Availability Matters

Availability is a combination of how many appointment slots you have and how far out leads can schedule them. If you do not have availability when leads have availability, they either:

1. **Do not schedule at all.** Nobody shows, nobody buys.
2. **Schedule at a bad time for them.** This creates three problems:
   - Huge chance of ghosting because the time is inconvenient
   - If they do show, lower chance of buying because they are managing scheduling annoyances
   - They steal a convenient time from someone who would have shown up and bought

### Is More Availability Worth The Cost?

Yes. Always. Businesses that implemented these tactics saw 20-40%, sometimes 200%+ increases in appointments where people actually show up. Those translate straight to revenue and profits.

**Reframe:** "I can't be open any more hours" really means "It's not worth it for me right now." Calculate what it actually costs vs. how much money you burn by not doing it.

### Tactics

**1. Take Appointments 7 Days Per Week**

You pay rent 7 days. You pay payroll 7 days. You pay insurance 7 days. Make money 7 days.

Going from 5 to 7 days = 40% more availability = up to 40% more sales.

Pattern: First, it sucks. Then, it works. Then you kick yourself for not doing it sooner.

**2. Take Appointments More Hours Per Day**

Be available to sell when your customers are available to buy.

Having a retail store open 9-5 M-F is like wondering where your customers are. They are at work making money.

For nationwide businesses: 6am to 6pm PST covers the highest percentage of US sales. You can always go beyond those hours.

**3. Give Leads More Flexible Appointment Times (15-Minute Slots)**

Most businesses only allow scheduling every 30 or 60 minutes because they have 30- or 60-minute appointments. That makes things convenient for the business, not for the lead.

Offer 4 scheduling options per hour (12:00, 12:15, 12:30, 12:45) instead of 1-2. Keep appointment length the same, just let people choose when it starts.

**Considerations:**
- You may get some awkward gaps between appointments. Those gaps become extremely profitable for contacting fresh leads (covered in Speed section).
- May require extra salespeople to handle extra volume. Most salespeople cost less than what you make from a 20-40% revenue increase.
- For expensive products: use 15-minute qualifying calls on a separate schedule from closing calls. The people who qualify leads and the people who close them have separate schedules. Works like a charm and makes more money.

**4. Offer Inbound, Outbound, and Self-Scheduling**

Leads can call you. You can call leads. Leads can schedule themselves online. Do all three. The more ways you give people to book an appointment, the more people will book.

### Online Scheduler Best Practices

1. **Header confirms they are in the right place.** Headline tells them next step and why. (Header = "Attention Avatar", Headline and Subheadline are self-explanatory.)
2. **Dates and times visible immediately on page load** (mobile and desktop). Time slots must be visible right away.
3. **Eliminate steps.** If leads already gave info earlier, do not ask again. Tech exists to eliminate pre-filled fields. Creates huge improvements in schedule rates. Do not underestimate this.

### Handling Too Many Bad Appointments

When you have too many unqualified appointments, add friction strategically:
- Add a video to watch before they see the scheduler
- Add a sales letter above the scheduler so they read before booking
- Add a price to the page so people know what they are in for
- Delay when the scheduler appears to make sure people are sufficiently qualified

### Thank You Page Evolution ([CREATOR_NAME] Example)

1. "Thank you, you'll be getting a text from XXX-XXX-XXXX shortly to book a time."
2. Added image of Leila with "Leila will be reaching out to you at this number."
3. "Text Leila at this number with the time that works for you to come in." (This CRUSHED.)

**Lesson:** A picture of a real person dramatically increased bookings for both men and women. Nobody cared about faceless Alex, but as soon as they saw a picture of a young in-shape woman, both men and women were more likely to book.

## Pillar II: Speed

**"Money loves speed. Wealth loves time. Poverty loves indecision."**

### Key Stats

- **391% increase** in conversions when leads contacted within 60 seconds (Velocify)
- **7x more likely** to qualify leads by calling within 1 hour vs. waiting longer. **60x more likely** than companies that waited 24 hours. (Harvard Business Review)
- **78% of customers** buy from the first company that responds (Lead Connect)
- **Average business takes 42 hours** to respond to a lead (InsideSales.com)

Like availability, the cost of getting to all leads immediately is excess capacity. You have people on your sales team or front desk who are not busy with fresh leads all the time. The alternative is people are busy and leads wait. But leads waiting leaves the door open for competitors to steal them. Or more realistically, for boredom, distraction, and disengagement to steal them.

### Three Speed Dimensions

**1. Speed to First Contact**

How long it takes to call or message leads after they express interest. Call within 5 minutes of opt-in. If you made the availability changes (especially the time slot one), your team will now have some calendar gaps. Use those gaps to immediately contact fresh leads. No wasted time.

**Key insight:** The faster you contact leads, the fewer times you have to contact them before they buy. The slower you contact leads, the more times you have to contact them before they buy. This is a rare instance where "work less to make more" is actually true.

**Litmus test:** If you are not hearing "Wow, that was fast! You guys are on top of it!" at least once per day, you are not calling your leads fast enough. Why would you not want to impress someone as a first impression and make more money?

**2. Speed to First Appointment**

How long between scheduling and having the appointment. The shorter the delay, the higher the show rate.

**Limit scheduling to 72 hours out maximum** (can get away with 5 days if you must). Same-day appointments have the highest show rates. If talking to someone live, show rate is 100%.

**Why 72 hours max:** Beyond 3 days, you get more people scheduling, but also more ghosting. A triple loss:
1. The person ghosted you
2. Enough ghosted meetings kills sales team morale
3. The ghost stole a convenient time from a person who would have bought

**Crush Scheduling Obstacles:** When leads do not want to schedule within 72hrs, ask what days they plan on using your service/product. Use that time they already planned on doing it to justify the appointment. "This appointment is the first step." If you have a service where someone has to do something, the time they planned on doing it, you can ask up front and then use that time for scheduling.

**Pull appointments forward (preferably to that day).** Five call outcomes in order of preference:
1. Sell them right now (already talking, show rate = 100%). If the same people set and close, just go for the close. If not, do a "hot handoff" to an active closer.
2. Contacted, qualified, and pulled appointment forward to today or sooner
3. Contacted, qualified, and appointment confirmed
4. Contacted and unqualified. Remove from calendar, give free useful stuff or references. Or offer an appropriate downsell.
5. No response. Remove from calendar or double-book if in-person.

**Appointment pull-forward script:**

> "Hey, this is Jim calling from XYZ on a recorded line. I saw that you just booked a time for our X on Monday. Just calling to confirm some details before your appointment. Is now a terrible time?
>
> [WHO] Great. So I see you are a [qualification 1], is that right? Awesome.
> [WHAT THEY WANT] And you're looking to [qualification 2], is that correct?
> [WHAT THEY'RE STRUGGLING WITH] And what are the things you've tried so far? Got it. What else? Got it. So this is a priority for you to fix or [bad thing] happens. Is that fair to say?
>
> Okay great. Well, two things. First, good news: I think we can help you out. Second, better news: I have an opening that canceled later today at 4pm for us to get you all squared away... unless now is a good time?"

You either go into the sale after qualifying them, move up their appointment, or worst case, you qualify them and re-confirm their time.

**If they cannot move their appointment, confirm it:**

> "No worries at all. We always like to offer more times to make it convenient. We want to cost you as little time as possible. So we're all good for two days from now at 4pm. Is there any reason you think you won't be able to make that appointment?"

Then, either text them a confirmation if you are having the appointment, or connect them in a three-way message with the closer (the hot handoff).

**Hot handoff script (if separate setter and closer):**

> "You're in luck. You're actually going to speak with one of our top experts on X. He's been in this game for a while and helped Y [businesses/people that match their description] achieve [thing they want]. We're fortunate to have him. He'll definitely be able to help you with that. Let me check his calendar really quickly. [pause] Yep I'm gonna slide you in so you can get this taken care of ASAP."
>
> "The moment we get off the call, I'm going to introduce you to him via text message. Does the XYZ number work? Great. Alright. Have an amazing day, and enjoy your time with CLOSER."

**3. Speed of Response**

How fast you get back to leads between scheduling and the appointment. This is where leads "fall through the cracks." Leads often make buying decisions before the sales call, and that makes it the competitor's sale to lose.

**Scenario 1 (Bad):** Lead texts a question. No response until next business day. They assume you are scattered and disorganized. They stop asking questions. If they do not show up for you, why bother showing up for them?

**Scenario 2 (Good):** Lead texts a question. Immediate response. They ask another question. Ding! Another response. Then the closer adds context. Both answered within a minute. "These guys are on top of it!"

**Tracking:** Every messaging service and call system provides timestamps. Use them. If show rates drop, look here first, especially for a particular rep. Their performance will also show who puts in the extra time. Encourage that behavior.

### Speed Summary

You need to reach out quickly. You need to get them in a sales meeting as soon as possible. If you do, you will contact more of them, follow up less, and sell more. When you speak to them, aim to set appointments inside 72 hours: same day, next day, day after. Or sell them right then and there. And if you have not scheduled the appointment, or they reach back out, respond fast. It shows you are serious and prevents undoing all the hard work you did to get them.

## Pillar III: Personalization

**"Personalize, don't pressure."**

Personalization means making yourself useful, relevant, and pleasant to individual leads. Figure out what they want, get that thing for them, and tell them they can have it if they show up. If the lead knows they will get value just for showing up, their chance of showing up goes up with it. Nobody wants to waste their time, so this lowers their risk and raises their opportunity cost. If they skip it, they miss out on something valuable.

### Six Personalization Tactics

**1. Use the Lead's Preferred Communication Method**

How to initiate contact: Text? Email? DM? Phone Call? The answer: all of them. Start contact via call, text, DM, and email. The works. Then continue the conversation wherever they respond first. You can switch to a richer medium later (phone, Zoom, video call). So many people only reach out one time on one platform. Big mistake.

**The urgency test:** If you needed to get ahold of your parents because of an emergency, you would call multiple times, text, DM, email, call their friends, call the front desk. These leads need your help. They said they wanted help. Reach out like you mean it.

**Bottom line:** Higher initial response rates by starting conversations in as many ways as you can, then continuing in the way they prefer.

**2. Qualify the Leads**

Use applications to score leads. Cancel appointments with poor fits. Spend more time on high-quality leads.

**The counter-intuitive insight:** A weight loss company with exceptional show rates actively canceled sales appointments. The owner fills out applications, identifies who will be a fit, and only meets with good leads. Result: way more money with less effort. He spends way more time working good leads and completely ignores the bad ones.

**3. Route Best Leads to Best Closers**

Score leads on a 1-5 or red/yellow/green system. Match highest-scoring leads with highest-performing reps.

**The timeshare proof:** Top sales rep at the #1 US timeshare company (nearly 3,000 reps) won first place five out of six years. He got a sixty-minute meeting with the CEO and told him: "You're wasting all the good leads on these junior reps. Meanwhile, I waste two-thirds of my time with people who have 500 credit scores. Just give me the best leads, and I'll make you a lot of money." The CEO tried it at one local office. The office 5x'd production. They rolled it out nationwide, and the company went from $200M to $1B/year in sales over 5 years.

**Steps:**
1. Profile best customers (actions they take, demographics)
2. Ask those qualifying questions in opt-ins, applications, and pre-appointment conversations
3. Score leads on a 1-5 or red-yellow-green system
4. Route best leads to best closers
5. Enjoy the fruits of your labor

**4. Segment Your Messaging**

Match messages to lead attributes. HubSpot improved their email marketing ROI by 7x after segmenting their list.

**Easy version:** Research the lead for 5 minutes before contact. Look at profiles, websites, recent activity. Reference something specific. At the very least, when you introduce yourself, have some idea of who they are and mention it upfront and early. People are more likely to listen and pay attention to someone who behaves like someone they know.

Example: "Hey Greg, I see that your site traffic is down 18% this quarter, we had another customer in a similar situation. They're now up 100% 90 days later. I prepped a walkthrough to show you how we can apply the same principles to your auto shop."

**Hard version:** Build separate email/text sequences for each lead category (revenue level, desired outcome, weight loss goal, etc.). Block a weekend, drink your energy drinks, and go ham. Get all the text and email sequences lined up for each situation. Once you have it, you will thank yourself later for higher response rates.

**5. Incentivize Showing Up**

Two approaches:

| Type | How It Works | Mechanism |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Push (Gift)** | Give something before they arrive to create reciprocity. They get it whether they show or not. | Reciprocity. Even when people know what you are doing, it still works. |
| **Pull (A/B Bribe)** | Offer a choice that assumes attendance. Both options assume they will show. You incur a cost by preparing it. | Mental commitment. Once they choose, they commit to showing up. |

**Push incentive (gift card):**

> "Hey Name! I just wanted to make sure you are caffeinated for our meeting tomorrow. Check your email. I just sent you a $5 gift card."

Do not obsess about the amount. Make it enough that it is not insulting. They get the gift whether they show or not. This is a gift. Gifts have no strings.

**Push incentive (brick-and-mortar):**

> Prospect opts in. 5-minute callback. "Hey Name! Since you are a first-time client, I'm going to set aside some free products for you. Would you want face cream or hand lotion?"
> "Face cream!"
> "Awesome, I will have it waiting for you tomorrow."

This also leads to an increase in close rate. Not the point but it does not hurt.

**A/B incentive ideas:** Quantity (one bottle or two?), start dates (tomorrow or Monday?), type (shirt or shorts?), payment preference (cash or card?), flavors (chocolate or vanilla?), time slots (morning or afternoon?), media (read or listen?), delivery speeds (standard or overnight?), sizes (small or medium?), colors (black or white?), materials (paper or plastic?), personnel (John or Sara?), communication (call or text?). With some creativity, you can make anything an A/B offer.

**A/B Incentive requirements:**
1. Leads should want the thing
2. It should have obvious connections to the thing you will offer them
3. You must incur some real cost by preparing it

**6. Demonstrate Proof**

Send testimonials and case studies matched to the lead's demographics. If talking to an older white male professional, show results of other older white male professionals. If talking to a female weight loss lead, send her results of someone her age, ethnicity, and around her weight. The closer the match, the better.

**Weave proof into every touchpoint:**
- After they schedule and before they show
- Between the first and second call
- If they ghost, every 90 days. Show them results of people who signed up about when they ghosted. This makes them feel left behind but gives them the option to make up for lost time.

## Pillar IV: Volume

**"Volume negates luck."**

Most communication attempts fail: inconvenient time, they wanted to respond but forgot, missed it in other messages, or they did not need it then but do now. If they opted in, they asked to be contacted. Contact them.

Nearly half of all salespeople give up after the first attempt. The data says 7+ attempts is optimal.

### 1. Scheduling Cadence (First Week)

| Day | Actions |
|-----|---------|
| **Day 1 (within 5 min)** | Call. If no answer, immediately double-dial (first call often blocked by phone, second gets through). Leave voicemail. Text immediately after voicemail. Two more double-dial + text attempts spaced hours apart. Total: 3 call-pairs + 3 texts on day 1. |
| **Days 2-3** | Call twice per day (once earlier, once later). Text after second call each day. |
| **Days 4-7** | Call and text once per day. If initial reach-outs were early in the week, this catches people who respond more on weekends. |
| **After Week 1** | Transition to long-term nurture: free value with soft CTAs to re-engage. Once they re-engage, start back at top. |

**Why front-load:** The more days that pass, the less likely they will schedule. Steps 6 and 7 are flexible (more or fewer days). Follow the laws in your area.

**Pro tip: Get an automatic dialer.** Improves team efficiency 2-3x. Automatically brings up highest-likelihood leads and maintains cadence. Team does not need to live on spreadsheets and sticky notes. Lets you focus full attention on actually working your leads.

### 2. Automated Reminders (After Scheduling)

1. **Immediately:** Confirmation with time, date, calling number/area code, and person they will meet.
2. **24 hours before**
3. **12 hours before**
4. **3 hours before**

**Tips:**
- Make it clear reminders are automated. People do not mind reminders. They mind being lied to.
- Include the area code you will call from (2x pickup rates).
- Use the lead's local time zone.

### 3. Manual Reminders (On Top of Automated)

Send from a separate number. These feel personal and cut through automated noise.

| When | Message Style |
|------|--------------|
| Night before (6pm-ish) | Include in sales team's end-of-day checklist |
| Morning of (8am-ish) | Short, personal check-in |
| 60 minutes before | Final confirmation |

**Manual reminder tips:**
1. Send multiple messages in a row (how people actually message, not one big blocky text)
2. Open with a probing question to get more information based on what you already know about them
3. Once they answer, compliment them if you can
4. Ask the next question. This is where you can pull up the appointment: "I've actually got an opening today at 4 if you're available later today. If not, no worries."
5. Once they respond, keep nurturing. Keep responding quickly.

### 4. BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting)

Origin: learned from Sharran Srivatsaa. Used at an insurance company where the biggest constraint was prospects dropping off between first and second calls.

At the end of every appointment, book the next one before they leave. NEVER get off the call with "We'll circle back later" or "I'll follow up and propose some times."

You are both there. Right now. Pull up the calendar. Pick a time. If you cannot schedule an appointment with them right then and there, you CERTAINLY will not after they are in limbo and you are in their rearview mirror.

Someone being dodgy about their next call is an objection. Handle it.

**Applies to:** Sales calls, onboarding, support, reviews, insurance follow-ups. Always end with the next scheduled touchpoint.

**Internally called:** "BAMFAM as a way of life."

Result at the insurance company: skyrocketed second-call show rates and sales increased.

## Execution: Building a Culture of Lead Nurture

**"Do the boring work."**

The hardest part about working leads is actually working your leads. It is tedious, boring work. But it gets very unboring results. Once you know what to do, the key to doing it is culture. Culture refers to the rules that govern good and bad behavior in an organization. We want a culture of execution: accepting of the behaviors that make us available, fast, personal, and persistent. And rejecting the behaviors that make us unavailable, slow, boring, and fragile.

### The Jacob Story (Origins of the Cultural Sayings)

[CREATOR_NAME] mentored a fifteen-year-old named Jacob. Advice given:
- Look at what the top guy on the team is doing, then do double. If he makes 200 calls a day, you make 400. If he works five days, you work six. If he takes a lunch break, you power through.
- When leads seem unqualified: reframe as "free practice." You can still take them through the script, hear their objections. Every opportunity is a swing of the hammer to chisel your skillset.
- "Be an equal opportunity salesman. Do not judge. Just execute." Some people who look homeless are actually very wealthy and appreciate not being judged.

Jacob offered to take all the under-qualified leads from the whole team. The older guys wanted to cherry-pick. So they gave him all their "garbage" leads. He turned them into sales. He developed the moniker "Yellow is the new gold" (yellows being less qualified leads in their red/yellow/green scoring system). Jacob rose through the ranks and finished as the top salesman out of 26 reps. He became Director of Sales at Acquisition.com.

### Three Metrics to Track Per Sales Rep

| Metric | What It Measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Show Rate** | Work ethic (how well they nurture leads) |
| **Close Rate** | Skill (how well they sell) |
| **Lead-to-Close Rate** | Holistic performance (nurture + close combined) |

If you are doing a good job, the same reps will be at the top of all three lists. If not, the lists will be all over the place. The nurture list measures work ethic. The closing list measures skill. One typically leads to the next.

**Insight:** The best closers are typically the best setters. Great way to have new reps come on in a low-risk setting environment. Same skill set, just higher stakes.

### Incentivize the Right Behaviors

**Monetary:** Small commission bump for show-up rates. Usually 5-30% of what a salesman makes on a sale, depending on ticket price.

**Non-monetary (three categories):**
1. **Attention** - give kudos to people with the highest show rates
2. **Affection** - ask them what they did; when they say "I just followed the process," give them more approval and attention
3. **Approval** - reinforce with cultural sayings

**Anti-pattern:** A rep with high close rate but who does not work their leads is seen as entitled and wasteful. And that person is made aware of it.

### Cultural Sayings That Reinforce Execution

- "Do the boring work" (the hardest part about working leads is actually working leads)
- "Volume negates luck"
- "Speed is king"
- "Yellows are the new gold" (less qualified leads still close)
- "Be a garbage man" (take all the leads others cherry-pick away from)
- "Every lead is free practice" (even unqualified leads build your skills)
- "BAMFAM as a way of life"
- "Process sits on a pedestal. No one is above the work."
- "Be an equal opportunity salesman. Do not judge. Just execute."

### Pro Tips

**Get your team iPhones for texting.** Spam and automated messages come as green messages. Real human messages come as blue messages. Blue messages get more responses. If there is ever a pseudo-human verification signal in the future, invest in it. Always worth the increased response rates.

**Use local area code call wrapping.** Local area codes have significantly higher pickup rates than unrecognized or out-of-area codes. Technology exists to dynamically switch your area code when calling prospects to their local area. Higher pickup rates by a lot. Worth the investment.

## Lead Nurture Checklist

### Pillar I: Availability
- Schedule appointments all seven days per week
- Schedule as many hours as you can manage. 9am to 9pm EST is a good goal.
- Give more scheduling options. Four per hour (every 15 minutes) is a good goal.
- Add an automated self-scheduling process
- Remove as many steps as possible from the self-scheduling process

### Pillar II: Speed
- Speed to first contact: Hire enough people to respond in under 5 minutes (extra credit: under 60 seconds)
- Speed to first appointment: Same day, next day, or day after
- Pull appointments forward whenever possible
- Outbound dial all leads who showed interest
- Speed of response: Respond to messages quickly. Use calendar gaps to keep leads warm.

### Pillar III: Personalization
- Contact them everywhere, then focus on places they respond
- Qualify leads with applications and demographic info
- Segment messaging: learn about leads from applications and public info, then focus on relevant topics
- Send best leads to best closers
- Incentivize showing up: A/B incentives and gift card incentives
- Show proof that matches their situation across all stages of follow-up

### Pillar IV: Volume
- Double-dial + text 3x the first day
- 2x the second day and the day after
- 1x for 1-4 days after that
- Automated reminders: 24 hours, 12 hours, 3 hours out (include name, date, time, phone number/area code)
- Manual reminders from a real phone: night before, morning of, 60 min prior
- Send multiple short messages rather than one big blocky message (more natural)
- Keep conversation going: Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask the next question. Use this to qualify and pull appointments forward.
- BAMFAM: Book a meeting from a meeting. Never let a lead go to no-man's land.

### Execution
- Track and rank: show rate, close rate, and lead-to-close ratio by sales rep
- Normalize loving "the boring work" that gets results
- "Yellows are the new gold" - do not sleep on less qualified leads
- Volume negates luck - do more than everyone else
- Be garbage men - do not judge a book by its cover. Every lead is an opportunity for practice.
- Process over all. No one is above the process. We all strive for that ideal.

## Quick Reference

1. **Availability is the #1 lever.** More slots = more schedules = more shows. Open 7 days, expand hours, use 15-min slots. Almost perfect correlation with throughput in the data.
2. **Speed kills the competition.** Contact within 5 minutes. Schedule within 72 hours. Respond instantly. 391% more conversions when contacted in 60 seconds. 78% of customers buy from the first company that responds.
3. **Personalize everything.** Qualify leads, route best to best, segment messages, incentivize showing up, and match proof to demographics.
4. **Volume negates luck.** 7+ reach-out attempts in the first week. Front-load the first 48 hours. Never give up after one attempt. Nearly half of all salespeople quit after the first try.
5. **BAMFAM.** Always book the next meeting from the current meeting. Someone dodging the next call is an objection. Handle it.
6. **Build a culture of execution.** Track three metrics per rep. Reward show rates alongside close rates. Cultural sayings reinforce the process. The best closers are typically the best setters.
7. **The data behind all of this:** Analysis of 4,000+ daily appointments and 20,000+ leads per day across portfolio companies. A data scientist analyzed a million rows of appointment data to find the four pillars.
