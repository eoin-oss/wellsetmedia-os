## Core Concept

Churn is the invisible hand that suppresses growth. A business with a revolving door of customers is hardly a business at all. It is a monster so small in the beginning you barely notice it, until it grows so big you cannot escape it. Reducing churn is the single highest-leverage activity because it compounds: longer customers mean higher LTV, which means you can outspend every competitor on acquisition.

**Key math:**
- At 15% monthly churn, you lose 83% of members in a year
- With 250 members on Jan 1st, you would need 208 new clients by Dec 31st just to stay the same
- With 12.5% margins, falling behind by even two customers per month kills the business
- Cutting churn from 10% to 3% = 3.3x increase in lifetime value (10 months to 33 months)
- A 5% increase in retention increases profits by 25-95% (Harvard Business Review)
- It costs 5x to 25x more to acquire a new customer than to retain one

**Core principle:** Instead of asking "How do I retain customers?", ask "What would make 100% of my customers leave?" Then do the exact opposite.

### What Would Churn 100% of Customers?

- Ignore them
- Break promises
- Miscommunicate
- Treat them poorly
- Set unrealistic expectations
- Hide progress and other updates from them
- Keep them away from other happy customers
- Make your stuff harder to use

### So to Keep 100% of Customers, Do the Opposite:

- Talk with them
- Keep your promises
- Communicate clearly
- Treat them like royalty
- Set realistic expectations
- Give them status updates
- Connect them with other happy customers
- Make it as easy as possible for them to get the most value they can

Seems obvious. But nobody does it. Because it is work.

## Origin Story: The Department of Defense Approach

When the gym owners in Gym Launch were losing customers as fast as they got them, [CREATOR_NAME] borrowed a problem-solving process from his consulting days at the Department of Defense:

1. Ask experts (plural) about the problem
2. Get all their answers
3. Find common threads to solve the problem (common factors analysis)

Following this process:

- Interviewed each about every single thing they did to reduce churn.
- Found 5 common factors, which became The 5 Horsemen of Retention.

## The 5 Horsemen of Retention

### 1. Track Attendance

If a member went to the gym 3+ times per week, they would stick. If they went twice per week or less, they would churn out.

**Typical churn pattern without intervention:**
- Week 1: 3 sessions
- Week 2: 2 sessions
- Week 3: 2 sessions
- Week 4: 1 session
- Week 5: CANCEL

**With intervention (reach out right when attendance drops to two):**
- Week 1: 3 sessions
- Week 2: 2 sessions (reach out)
- Week 3: 3 sessions
- Week 4: 3 sessions
- Week 5: 3 sessions

The key is reaching out immediately when attendance drops, not waiting until they cancel.

### 2. Reach Out 2x/Week

Praising customers about their participation and progress goes a long way. Solving little problems they have does too. Do both. Proactive contact, not reactive.

### 3. Handwritten Cards

Send hand-written cards at these moments:
- When they sign up
- When you ask for referrals at 3, 6, 12 month milestones
- Holidays
- Birthdays

There is never a bad time to send a handwritten card so long as you make it yourself.

### 4. Member Events

Hold regular events on an internal calendar. Keep them regular to you and random to them. Every 21, 42, or 63 days is a good cadence. Use handwritten invites. Reduces churn AND gets referrals. A win-win.

### 5. Exit Interviews

If a person says they want to leave, talk to them before they do. Do this right and save about HALF the phone and email cancellations.

**Implementation:**
- Set the expectation you will require an exit interview when you onboard new customers
- Use their cancellation notice as the sign to set up the exit interview
- Talk to them about it, solve their problems, get them back on the wagon

**Math:** Realistically, you cut churn by 25% (assuming half show to the interview). That is a 33% increase in LTV.

### Results Timeline

After implementing all five horsemen:

- **Month 1:** Churn UP by 50%. Spooky, but expected. This is "shaking the tree." First, people who would have canceled anyway. Second, ghost members on automated billing who get reminded and cancel.
- **Month 2:** Churn DOWN by 50%
- **Month 3:** Churn DOWN by another 50%

**Example numbers:**
- Month 1: 10% to 15% churn (up 50%)
- Month 2: 15% to 7% churn (down ~50%)
- Month 3: 7% to 3% churn (down ~50%)

A reduction in churn from 9% to 3% is a 3.3x increase in lifetime value. [CREATOR_NAME] has yet to see a business with less than 3% churn that does not make great money.

## Price, Value, and Churn

Warren Buffett: "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get." If you can keep the value customers get greater than the price they pay, customers will stick. Two ways to do this: provide more value (preferred) or lower prices.

### Relationship Between Price and Churn

The most sales at the highest LTV is the best price. This does not necessarily mean lowest churn.

| Price | Clicks | Conv Rate | Sales | Churn | LTV | Total Return | Difference |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------|-------|-----|-------------|------------|
| $10 | 100 | 5% | 5 | 10% | $100 | $500 | Baseline |
| $20 | 100 | 4% | 4 | 10% | $200 | $800 | +60% (best) |
| $100 | 100 | 2% | 2 | 33% | $300 | $600 | +20% |

**Rule:** If you can double your price and close only 20% fewer deals with all else staying the same, do it. Test, test, test.

Across the Skool platform, the higher the price, the higher the churn. Many businesses misprice their products. They try to create something recurring out of something that has one-time value. Match your business model with the pricing model that makes the most sense.

### Ongoing Value Principle

Think value per second, not seconds of value. Less but better is better than more and decent. **Overwhelm is the #1 reason for churn.**

**Case study:** A friend ran a newsletter business making $500K/month. When churn was lowest, he did one Q&A call per month (stayed until everyone's questions were answered) and one long physical newsletter per month. Then he added calls once per week, then other things. Everything he added increased churn. Sometimes less is more. There is wisdom in deletion.

Gym Launch is on its 10th version. Each version has gotten shorter and simpler because the #1 reason for churn is overwhelm.

**Key insight:** Retention comes down to making sure customers CONSUME the value, not overwhelming them with it. Make it as little and as good as possible. The vast majority of people start a YouTube video and do not finish it. They start a course and do not finish it. They set a goal and do not follow through. Make it as easy as possible to consume your product. Reduce effort and sacrifice. When customers stop consuming, contact them more and more often.

## Why Reducing Churn Matters

It is easier to sell customers than strangers. Your existing customers already trust you, already received value, already gave you their credit card, have no acquisition costs, and are your ideal customer. Invest effort accordingly.

**Three reasons to reduce churn:**

1. **Best returns on effort.** It costs 5x to 25x more to acquire a new customer compared to retaining one. Give yourself a budget of what you are willing to spend extra to keep a customer. Make it 1/5th of CAC and you would be amazed how far that goes.

2. **Tiny changes make huge profit differences.** Increasing retention by 5% increased profits by 25-95% (Harvard Business Review). Going from customers canceling in 1 month to 1.05 months on average could increase profits by 25-95%. Monstrous.

3. **The only path to compounding growth.** If referrals are greater than churn, you never stop growing. If 10% churn monthly but 10% in referrals, you stay flat. Decrease churn OR increase referrals and you grow. Bonus: activities that decrease churn create happier customers, and happier customers send more referrals.

**Industry note:** If you serve VSMBs (very small business owners), you will have "structural churn." They go out of business. That is OK. You still want to decrease it. Shopify and Skool are both examples of large businesses that serve very small customers and still succeed.

## The 9-Item Churn Checklist

### 1. Figure Out Activation Points

Activation points are leading indicators of retention. Template: "Every customer that does (X) or gets (Y result) stays longer than customers who don't."

**Skool example:** Hosts retain more customers when customers interact in the Skool community more often. Customer activity is broken into levels. When the customer reaches activity level "3" they have the highest chance of staying the longest. Current data shows hosts that get at least 3 group members stick with Skool way longer. So Skool helps hosts acquire members with custom tools, education, and other resources.

**How to find your activation point:**
- Gather all the data you can about churned customers
- Filter to those who stayed 3 months or longer (convention, not magic number)
- Order that list by who spent the most money. Take the top 20%.
- Learn everything you can about them: demographics, psychographics, income, business size, revenue, any other data. Survey customers if you do not know.
- Figure out how they used your stuff and how you treated them
- Common factors become your activation-point candidates. Narrow to 5 factors. Work your way down. Some will matter and some will not. Find the ones that do.

**PR Agency example:** They analyzed customers and found one specific avatar pays more, stays longer, and gets better results. They only served them. True story: the company 10x'd their business over the next 24 months.

**Common activation points by business type:**
- B2B service company: first time they get leads
- Software company: first time they log in and see the dashboard
- Physical product: first time they use the consumable product

**Then update two things:**

1. **Your messaging.** Advertise to your best customers. Update paid ads, organic content, and customer onboarding to select for the perfect customer. Gym Launch example: a competitor sold just as many people but [CREATOR_NAME] made 70x more profit. Same acquisition cost. The competitor ran ads to everyone in fitness. [CREATOR_NAME] only targeted people who had the highest chance of staying the longest. Competitor customers worth $5,000. [CREATOR_NAME] customers worth $42,000 in the first year. Found common factors in the top 20%, used those to qualify prospects ahead of time. Result: 70x more profit.

2. **Your onboarding.** At Gym Launch, churn was at 8% and the goal was 4%. They asked "When do people first get value?" The people leaving did not make their investment back quickly. The people who stayed longest made their investment back in the first 30 days. So they created "The Fast Cash Play" and pushed every client to recoup their investment in the first 30 days. Churn went from 8% to 3% within 6 months.

**Make finding activation points your top priority.** They have the greatest effect on customer churn.

**Retest every 6-12 months.**

### 2. Onboard Your Customers

Onboarding means "teach customers how to hit the activation points you painstakingly investigated." Guidelines ranked by effectiveness:

- Custom outperforms generic
- Personal outperforms group
- Live outperforms recorded
- Carrots outperform sticks
- Some beats none

**Onboarding should:**
- Outline how to get value. Drive them to the activation point.
- Resell the value of the purchase, framed within their goals
- Tell them how to unlock more value as they stay longer (e.g., some Skool groups give members free lifetime access at level 8)
- Tell them best practices to communicate with you, your team, and other customers
- Follow up. Use the onboarding to establish long-term communication. Customers should always know when you will speak to them next.

**Best Skool community onboarding practices:**
- Tell them to make a post with a specific structure and comment on 1-2 other posts
- Tell them where to start
- Connect the 4-6 people together after onboarding so they already have people they know
- Tell them what they unlock once they do their homework and get to level 3
- Show them what to cancel or do to save money for this membership. Have them do it as homework and show they did.

**Optional:** Sell onboarding as a product. Example: give a new gym member six personal training sessions before allowing them into group classes. Wildly effective. Let the customer know they have to earn the right to enter the general customer base.

**Real-world outcome:** One portfolio company went from group onboarding to 1-on-1 onboarding. 25% boost in ascensions. With the higher LTV, they scaled ad spend. One of the largest contributing factors to going from $2M/month to $2M/week. The more personalized the onboarding, the better.

### 3. Incentivize Customer Activation

Make it worth it for customers to do the things that keep them. A vast majority of Skool host cancellations come from people in the bottom two tiers of engagement. A vast majority of retained customers sit in the top tier.

**Incentive options (from Skool):**
- Courses that unlock at milestones
- 1-on-1 consulting calls or bundles of calls at certain duration or status
- Tickets to live or virtual events
- Access to another set of higher-tier calls
- Custom badges, profile images, and other indicators of status that they are "in"
- Free lifetime access at the highest engagement level (level 8 on Skool, very hard to attain)

**Timing trick:** Put incentive unlocks just past major churn points. If most customers leave after month 3, put something cool that unlocks on month 4. Easy and effective.

**Pro tip on usage churn:** A leading indicator of churn is "usage churn," which is when a customer is still subscribed but no longer uses the product. If you sell an annual contract but a customer stops using your service at 6 months, they will likely churn. But if you intercept them right when they stop using it and get them to keep using it, you will likely retain them.

### 4. Community Linking

Connect members to each other, not just to you. It is more scalable to facilitate multiple, deeper connections than one single tie to the group.

"It is easy to quit a membership. It is hard to leave a relationship."
"They come for the bikini. They stay for the community."

**How to make it happen:**
- **Group events.** One Skool group owner moved from 1-on-1 onboarding to 1-on-6 onboarding. Not to make it less personalized, but to intentionally connect people and introduce them to each other. Result: low churn. At gyms, group events were held frequently. People came for the six-pack and stayed for the community.
- **Manually connect people.** If you think a member would benefit from knowing another member, connect them.
- **Start a community podcast.** Interview members.
- **Elevate micro-celebrities within your community.** Call them out in public channels and praise them for their specific area of expertise.

### 5. Fire or Correct Bad Customers

Bad customers make it bad for everyone else. Incentivize positive behaviors and give feedback on bad ones.

**For communities (Skool model):**
- Delete bad posts and tell people why they were bad and what good looks like
- Three-strike policy on bad posters
- Pin the best 1-2 new posts daily. This elevates those people, provides fresh value to everyone else, helps them level up, and signals to the group what types of content you reward.
- Create topic categories for what you want people to post. Include these four: wins, fun, discovered, meetups (call them whatever you want)
  - **Wins** give you testimonials
  - **Fun** keeps it light, lets people talk about whatever
  - **Discovered** tells people to share what they have found using data and proof
  - **Meetups** gives people a place to connect on or offline

### 6. Add Annual Pricing Options

Customers who pay for longer stays, stay longer. When you add annual payment options, some customers will take them and stay for an entire year, extending average stay.

**Pricing your annual option:**
- If average months stayed is less than 12: price annual ABOVE your average number of months. Example: average stay is 6 months at $1,000/mo ($6,000 total), price annual above $6,000.
- If average stay exceeds 12 months: use "buy 10 get 2 free" (16% off)
- To increase the number of people who take annual without removing monthly, offer a steeper annual discount
- Typically 10-20% of people take the annual option with "buy 10 get 2 free" pricing

**Alternative 1: Big Head, Long Tail.** Large upfront payment for one-time value (education, setup), then small monthly for ongoing value (community, support).

- Example: $6,800 upfront for education or setup fee, then $199 for community and everything after. The recurring base stacks. You might get 30 months of LTV out of the $199 and increase LTV from $6,800 to $12,800 because you are appropriately priced.
- You can put the high-ticket one-time purchase in a free community, then sell them into the lower-ticket recurring paid community.
- Or someone can join your paid group, then you upsell them to the high-ticket one-time purchase over the phone.
- Make sure to include the recurring fee WITH the one-time upfront fee, not as a second sale. Otherwise, you lose the price anchor that drives retention.

**Alternative 2: Founder rates.** A discount for the business owner. A 50% decrease is a great starting point (provided gross margins are still there). Incentivizes both buying and staying.

**Agency example from $100M Offers (one-time setup fee model):**

|--------|-----------|------------|------------|
| Advertising Spend | $10,000 | $10,000 | Unchanged |
| Response Rate | 0.00013 | 0.00033 | 2.5x (more appealing) |
| Appts Booked | 40 | 100 | Result |
| Show Rate | 75% | 75% | Unchanged |
| Appts Showed | 30 | 75 | Result |
| Closing % | 16% | 37% | 2.3x (more value) |
| Appts Closed | 5 | 28 | Result |

| ROAS | 0.5:1 | 11.2:1 | Get paid to get customers |

### 7. Cancellation Call or Video

You can usually save half the people who hop on a cancellation call.

**Two save methods:**

1. **Save with a redo.** "Let me give it another shot and I will make it right."
2. **Save with an upsell.** You realize they need more help and should have been in your higher program. Credit their payment toward the higher program. "You should have been sold into that anyway. And that is on me." You still get a higher-ticket sale. They still want the result. Find the expectation that was unmet and see if you can meet it.

**For text/email cancellations:** "Hey, no pressure. I would love to hear all the reasons you hate me. It would really help me out so that in the future I can avoid it."
- Let them vent. Get more upset than they are. Only one person can be in the angry boat at the same time. "That is completely ridiculous. You are 100% correct. Will you give me the opportunity to make it right?"
- Because they just want to feel validated. They do not want to fight.
- "And just so we are clear, if these things happened, you would be happy?" Then that becomes a clear roadmap.

**Additional save tactics:**
- Resell them on why staying helps them achieve their goals
- Tell them what they will lose: custom URLs, posts, access, founder pricing, work they have done, data
- Many software companies are experts at this. If you lose your data, or it is hard to move it, or you lose work you have done, it is less likely you will cancel.

**For high-volume low-price businesses:** If hopping on the phone with churning customers does not make sense financially (although it almost always does by what you learn from them), add a cancellation video that resells the value and reminds them what they risk losing.

### 8. Survey Customers Regularly

Survey twice per year with this question set:
- "If I removed everything on this list but one, what would you want to keep the most?"
- "If I kept everything on this list but one, which would bother you the least to see me remove?"

**Portfolio company example:** They asked this question. Learned their customers loved the events they threw. Listened and events became one of their largest product lines.

**Between surveys:** Reach out 1-on-1 every 2-3 weeks. Follow the ACA framework:
- **A**cknowledge what you have seen them do
- **C**ompliment them on something
- **A**sk a question

Extra finding: counterintuitively, the wealthier the customers, the less handholding they typically want.

Do more of the stuff they like. Cut the stuff they do not. Reach out regularly so it looks like you care (because you should).

### 9. Create a Customer Journey

Four milestones every customer should hit:

| Milestone | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **1. Activate** | Hit the activation point (always first) |
| **2. Testimonial** | Capture proof of results |
| **3. Refer** | Send you new customers |
| **4. Ascend** | Buy your next product or higher tier |

Besides activation, milestones 2-3-4 may happen in different orders depending on the customer and business model. But ideally all four happen. They are definitely more likely to happen if you plan for them than if you do not.

**Ascension emphasis:** Customers get an itch to buy more stuff over time. They are going to buy it from you or the competitor down the street. Save yourself the churn and bring in new revenue all at once. Someone who just bought another thing from you is the least likely to churn. So ask them to buy.

You can incentivize by giving them something at each milestone. Works great when paired with unlockables.

## Benefits of Low Churn vs. High Churn

**When you keep customers (low churn):**
- Grow every year no matter what, even if sales stay the same
- Create more profit from the same customers
- Your business compounds
- Sleep great knowing all bills are paid every month and you have customers who love you
- Get positive word of mouth, getting you even more customers for free
- Keep the best talent because they see you are actually helping people

**When you have high churn:**
- Have to keep getting new customers every month just to stay the same size
- Never create a compounding business
- Make your business hard to sell (and thereby less valuable)
- Sleep poorly because you are only as good as your last month
- Get poor customer feedback
- Get negative word of mouth, preventing you from getting more customers in the future
- Have trouble keeping employees motivated because they see customers failing and falling off the back end
- Lose credibility because you always have to create "new stuff" to stay alive rather than benefiting from the tailwind of a good reputation

## Churn Checklist Summary (Tear-Out)

1. **Figure out activation point**
   - Who are the top 10-20% of your customers? This is your avatar.
   - What did the top 10-20% do? This is their activation point.
   - Update messaging to attract the right customers. Update onboarding to drive them to the activation point.
2. **Add onboarding calls**
   - Resell the value. Say names multiple times. Tell them how to get value. Tell them about unlockables. Tell them about community linking. Give them homework with a 7-day deadline. Schedule the next call.
3. **Use unlockables**
   - Options: course, 1-on-1 call, live or virtual event, higher-tier calls, custom badge, profile image, or status indicator
   - Time unlocks: put unlocks past average lifespan, then 30 days after, then 3 months later, then 3 months later. Pull from the list above.
4. **Community linking**
   - Manually introduce members
   - Host group events for the members
   - Create a community podcast
   - Elevate micro-celebrities within your community
5. **Fire or correct bad customers**
   - Incentivize good behavior (pin posts, praise in public, provide value to everyone else)
   - Eliminate bad behavior and tell people why it was bad and what good looks like (kick them out after 3 strikes)
6. **Add annual pricing**
   - Annual billing option
   - Try big upfront with small monthly (if it works for your business)
7. **Cancellation call/video**
   - Tell new customers you require cancellation calls
   - Resell them on why staying helps with their goals
   - Tell them what they will lose if they cancel
8. **Survey customers**
   - Make your product easy to consume
   - Send a survey twice a year with the keep/remove questions
   - Reach out to customers every few weeks (if price point allows). Follow the A-C-A framework.
9. **Have a basic customer journey**
   - Implement the four milestones: activate, testimonial, refer, ascend
   - Activation first, then the other three in the order that makes sense
   - Do not forget to sell them again
