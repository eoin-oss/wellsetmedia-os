## Part 1: Why Price Beats Everything

### The Genie Test

A business genie offers to double ONE metric. Everything else stays the same. Which do you pick?

**Baseline business:**
- 30 new clients/mo
- 33% churn (avg 3-month retention)
- $100/mo price
- 100 total clients
- 20% net margins
- $10,000/mo revenue
- $2,000/mo profit

### Option 1: Double Customers

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| New clients/mo | 30 | 60 |
| Churn | 33% | 33% |
| Price | $100/mo | $100/mo |
| CAC | $100 | $50 (half) |
| Lifetime revenue | $300 | $300 |
| Total clients (stabilized) | 100 | 200 |
| Net margins | 20% | 35% |
| Revenue/mo | $10,000 | $20,000 |
| Profit/mo | $2,000 | $7,000 |

**Result: 3.5x profit.** Revenue doubles. CAC halves because you got twice the customers for the same ad spend. Saved CAC drops straight to bottom line.

### Option 2: Double Purchases (Retention)

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| New clients/mo | 30 | 30 |
| Churn | 33% (3 mos) | 16.5% (6 mos) |
| Price | $100/mo | $100/mo |
| CAC | $100 | $100 |
| Lifetime revenue | $300 | $600 |
| Gross profit/customer | $150 | $300 |
| Total clients (stabilized) | 100 | 200 |
| Net margins | 20% | 35% |
| Revenue/mo | $10,000 | $20,000 |
| Profit/mo | $2,000 | $7,000 |

**Result: 3.5x profit.** Customers stay twice as long, so steady-state client count doubles. But delivery costs scale proportionally.

### Option 3: Double Price

| Metric | Before | After |
|--------|--------|-------|
| New clients/mo | 30 | 30 |
| Churn | 33% | 33% |
| Price | $100/mo | $200/mo |
| CAC | $100 | $100 |
| Lifetime revenue | $300 | $600 |
| Gross profit | $150 | $450 (3x) |
| Gross margin | 50% | 75% |
| Total clients | 100 | 100 |
| Net margins | 20% | 60% |
| Revenue/mo | $10,000 | $20,000 |
| Profit/mo | $2,000 | $12,000 |

**Result: 6x profit.** Revenue doubles but costs stay FLAT. The entire increase drops straight to profit. No extra delivery, no extra CAC.

### Summary

| Option | What Doubles | Profit Multiplier |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| 2x Customers | More customers, same price, same purchases | 3.5x profit |
| 2x Purchases | Same customers, same price, more repeat buys | 3.5x profit |
| **2x Prices** | **Same customers, higher price, same purchases** | **6x profit** |

**Why price wins:** Doubling price with the same customer count means costs stay flat but revenue doubles. The entire increase is profit.

### Profitwell 512-Company Study

Three findings that validate the genie test:

1. **1% pricing improvement is 2x as efficient** at increasing profit as a 1% retention improvement, and nearly **4x as efficient** as a 1% acquisition improvement.
2. **Companies that test pricing more frequently make more profit.**
3. **Companies that test pricing more frequently grow continually faster.**

Bottom line: If you are wondering where to focus first, focus on pricing. Lowest effort, highest returns.

## Part 2: Three Pricing Models

### 1. Cost-Plus Pricing

Whatever your costs are plus an arbitrary margin.

- **Pros:** Simple to understand. You cover your costs.
- **Cons:** Misses every customer who would pay more. Costs change. You don't always know all costs ahead of time. Customers have no idea what it costs you, so your costs are irrelevant to them.

### 2. Competitor-Based Pricing

Whatever the average of what everyone else charges.

- **Pros:** Simple. Might be closer to market willingness to pay.
- **Cons:** Copycat pricing based on someone else's business and customers. Their shoe does not fit your foot. Most competitors are broke anyway.

### 3. Value-Based Pricing (Target Model)

Based on what the customer is willing to pay (WTP).

- **Pros:** You can charge 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x market rates or more if you build something customers actually want. Forces you to improve the product because higher prices demand higher value. Drives ongoing customer conversations to discover what they want. New customers may pay more than what you currently charge.
- **Cons:** Takes more work. Requires thinking and building, not just copying. Focuses on providing maximum value per customer rather than maximum customer count.

**Value-based pricing is the target.** Charge based on value delivered. Force yourself to build a product worth the price.

## Part 3: Finding the Perfect Price

### Three Metrics for Value-Driven Pricing

1. **Conversion rate** at the current price (how many people buy)
2. **Churn** at the current price (how long they stay / how many times they buy)
3. **Lifetime gross profit** at each price point

### The Price Testing Table

Build this table at every price point you test:

| Price | Clicks | Conv Rate | Sales | Churn | LTV | Total Return | Difference |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------|-------|-----|-------------|-----------|
| $10 | 100 | 5% | 5 | 10% | $100 | $500 | baseline |
| $20 | 100 | 4% | 4 | 10% | $200 | $800 | +60% |
| $100 | 100 | 2% | 2 | 33% | $300 | $600 | +20% |

**Target:** The price that maximizes **conversion rate x lifetime gross profit** (the "Total Return" column). Not the price that gets the most customers.

### Testing Cadence

- Test prices **every quarter**
- Nudge price up **20% every 10 sales** until you notice a dramatic drop in conversion
- Then go back to the sweet spot
- Companies that test pricing most frequently grow fastest and profit most

## Part 4: Rules of Pricing

### Revenue Rules

1. **Raising prices makes money two ways.** More gross profit per customer AND fewer customers to deliver to. Revenue goes up, costs go down.
   - Case study: Tripled gym price from $99 to $299. Lost 30% of customers. Revenue: 200 x $99 = $19,800 became 140 x $299 = $41,860. Revenue more than doubled while costs dropped 30%. Massive profit impact.

2. **Higher prices get fewer customers but often make more money.** If you double price from $10 to $20, you rarely cut conversions in half. This is fundamentally what all price increases exploit.

3. **High close rate = prices too low.** Consistently closing over 50%? Room to raise.

4. **Full capacity = prices too low.** If you want to grow but you are full, raise prices. Fixed capacity businesses (artists, consultants who can only take X clients) must raise prices when they sell out.

5. **Keep raising until the extra money no longer offsets lost sales.** Going from 50% close rate to 30% but doubling price means more money. You will hear "no" 40% more often. Stomach it. 9 out of 10 times, raising prices yields more profit than the lost sales cost you.

### Value Rules

6. **Raising prices usually means raising value.** Bigger price increase requires bigger value increase. If you want to charge more, make the product better to justify it.

7. **Believe in your price.** If you do not believe the product is worth the price, make the product better.

8. **Customer surplus = goodwill.** The gap between price and value drives word of mouth and repurchase. Premium pricing funds a superior product that still delivers surplus.

### Measurement Rules

9. **Hearing more "no" does not mean less money.** Fewer customers at higher prices means more rejections but more profit. Business owners cave and lower prices to hear more "yes" even though they make less money.

10. **Too high = you stop making sales or NPS drops.** Measure objectively with NPS scores. If people complain about value for cost, you have gone too far.

### Segmentation Rules

11. **Different customers need different prices.** 2-3 customer avatars within one business may have 5x-10x difference in willingness to pay. Create multiple tiers and price levels to accommodate. Name tiers after aspirational titles the customer wants for themselves.

### Billing Rules

12. **Bill less frequently to reduce churn.** Daily billing churns faster than annual. The longer between billing cycles, the longer you have to provide value.

13. **Display price in smallest increment, bill on longest increment.** Say "$5/day" but bill $1,825/year. How you display and how you bill do not need to match.

14. **Match billing to value delivery.** Separate one-time value (education, information) from ongoing value (accountability, support). Bill a one-time fee for access to education. Bill a smaller recurring fee for ongoing accountability. Mixing them causes information to be underpriced and accountability to be overpriced.

### The Lookback Window of Value

If someone pays $5,000/month and you make them $60,000 in month one and $0 in month two, they will cancel. The $60,000 is compared within the lookback window of the last billing cycle, not the entire relationship. Longer billing cycles create less churn because customers evaluate value over a longer window. They also attract more qualified buyers who can pay larger amounts up front.

### Play 1: Monthly to 28-Day Billing Cycles (+8.3%)

**How it works:** Monthly billing = 12 cycles/year. Every 28 days = 13 cycles/year. That extra cycle is pure profit. People convert at the same rates.

**Impact on margins:** A 20% margin business goes to 26.1% margins. Net profit increases 41.5%.

**Billing comparison table:**

| Monthly Billing | New Billing | Old Annual | New Annual | Difference |
|----------------|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| $100/month | $100/28 days | $1,200 | $1,300 | +$100 (8.3%) |
| $100/month | $50 bi-weekly | $1,200 | $1,300 | +$100 (8.3%) |
| $100/month | $25 weekly | $1,200 | $1,300 | +$100 (8.3%) |
| $100/month | $300/12 weeks | $1,200 | $1,500 | +$300 (25%) |
| $100/month | $99/21 days | $1,200 | $1,716 | +$516 (43%) |

**Implementation:**
1. Change contracts for new customers immediately
2. Set a date to change for existing customers
3. Be upfront. Call it a price increase if needed. Frame as reinvestment in business.

**Best practice:** Display pricing weekly (lowest perceived price) but bill every 4 weeks. Weekly and bi-weekly billing causes billing hassle from short pauses. Stick with 4 weeks, 12 weeks, or longer.

### Play 2: Processing Fees + Second Form of Payment (+3-4%)

**How it works:**
1. After customer agrees to price, ask "How did you want to pay?"
2. Say "Great, it's just a 3.99% card processing fee."
3. If they hesitate: "No problem. Save the 3.99% by providing a second form of payment. About 7% of cards decline monthly. If you save us the effort, we pass the savings to you."

**Why the second card matters more than the fee:** Recurring payments get 1.2%-1.7% monthly involuntary churn from card info changes. If you have 5% monthly churn, involuntary churn could be 24-34% of total churn.

**LTV impact at 5% monthly churn ($100/mo service):**

| Situation | Price | Churn/Mo | LTV | Change |
|-----------|-------|----------|-----|--------|
| Old (baseline) | $100/mo | 5.0% | $2,000 | - |
| Accept fee | $104/mo | 5.0% | $2,080 | +4% |
| Add 2nd card (low) | $100/mo | 3.8% | $2,631 | +31% |
| Add 2nd card (high) | $100/mo | 3.3% | $3,030 | +51% |

**LTV impact at 10% monthly churn ($100/mo service):**

| Situation | Price | Churn/Mo | LTV | Change |
|-----------|-------|----------|-----|--------|
| Old (baseline) | $100/mo | 10.0% | $1,000 | - |
| Accept fee | $104/mo | 10.0% | $1,040 | +4% |
| Add 2nd card (low) | $100/mo | 8.8% | $1,136 | +14% |
| Add 2nd card (high) | $100/mo | 8.3% | $1,204 | +20% |

**Implementation:** Change sales scripting. Accept second form of payment. Authorize both. Re-run failed transactions on backup card next day.

**Case study (origin):** Ad agency friend added 3.99% CC processing fee. Added 33% to profit. Zero sales lost.

### Play 3: Sales Tax Passthrough (+0-10%)

**How it works:** Stop absorbing sales tax. Add "plus tax" to pricing. If you have 20% margins and pay 5% sales tax, you are giving away 25% of your profit.

**Case study:** Canadian marketer charging $25,000/mo. Invoice showed $27,000 (8% provincial tax). His position: "I don't cover taxes. My price is what I get."

**Implementation script:**
1. Get agreement on the price
2. On the invoice, add: "State tax code [1030a.o] mandates personal services are subject to 6% sales tax."
3. Keep it dry and matter-of-fact. Almost as if to say "as you already know."
4. Put tax as a separate line item before the total
5. If they balk, get more angry about it than them: "Think I like charging this tax? I don't even get it! I hand it right over to Uncle [REP_NAME_5]."

**Pro tips:**
- Menu pricing: Add a line at the bottom saying "all prices subject to sales tax and an additional 4% processing fee."
- Incorporate in a state that does not charge sales tax for your service category. Same savings without passing cost to customers. Check with lawyers first.
- If you do not get charged taxes, you cannot charge them. But if you do, recoup it.

### Play 4: Annual CPI Price Increases (+3-10%)

**How it works:** Include a right to increase rates annually in your contracts. Fixed percentage increase each year.

**5% annual increase compounding:**

| Year | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate | $100 | $105 | $110 | $116 | $122 |
| Cumulative | 0% | +5% | +10% | +16% | +22% |

**12% annual increase compounding:**

| Year | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate | $100 | $112 | $125 | $140 | $157 |
| Cumulative | 0% | +12% | +25% | +40% | +57% |

**Inflation reality check:** $100 in 2024 was $79 in 2017. If you charged $100 in 2017 and never changed, you have 21% less spending power. At 20% margins, seven years of unchanged prices could erase all profit.

**Case study:** Warren Buffett raised See's Candies prices over 50 times in 51 years. Sometimes as high as 17% in a single year. He demands control over one thing in the business: prices. Result: over $1B in lifetime profit to himself.

**Implementation:**
- Pick 5%-15% annual increase. Add to new contracts and customers.
- Sales script: "In order for us to reinvest in keeping our integrity and delivering [X] at the highest possible quality, we keep our prices standard with the CPI. That way, we don't have any incentive to cut on quality as the price of goods goes up."
- Mention during paperwork, not during the sale itself.
- Few people balk under 15%. A 15% annual increase = 57% price increase over the long term.

### Play 5: Longer Duration Billing / Annual Plans (+10-15%)

**Churn by billing frequency (Profitwell data, 14,000 memberships):**

| Billing Cycle | Monthly Churn | Price Example | LTV | LTV Gain vs Monthly |
|---------------|---------------|---------------|-----|-------------------|
| Annual (1x/yr) | 2% | $100/mo | $5,000 | 5.35x |
| Quarterly (4x/yr) | 5% | $100/mo | $2,000 | 2.14x |
| Monthly (12x/yr) | 10.7% | $100/mo | $935 | baseline |

**Key insight:** You can 5x LTV by getting customers to pay annually. The question is whether conversions drop 5x (usually not).

**Conversion rates by offer method:**
- Annual option on a page (16% discount): 10-15% select annual
- Annual option as default selection: 30% select annual
- Over the phone: 35-40% select annual

**Revenue impact:** If 30% of customers prepay annually with 16% discount ($1,000 vs $1,200), average first transaction goes from $100 to $370 per customer.

**Pro tip - High conversions AND annual billing:** Sell a short duration (6-12 weeks) up front. Build trust. Then upsell into prepaying a full year. Easier conversion + long-term stick.

**Implementation (descending price approach):**
1. Start with the highest price: full annual rate with no discount ($1,200)
2. Offer prepaid annual discount of 17% ($1,000)
3. If no, offer prepaid quarterly discount of 8% ($275 vs $300)
4. If still no, offer standard monthly with no discount

**Always start with the highest price.** The first number anchors the conversation. Downsell the discounts for prepayment. Positions the cheaper prepayment as a benefit rather than making monthly payments look like a penalty.

### Play 6: Round Up Pricing (+1-3%)

**How it works:** Round all prices up to the nearest clean number. Change 7s to 9s. Add .99 to everything.

**Case study (gym):** Three weekly pricing tiers rounded up:

| Old Price | New Price | Old Annual | New Annual | Increase |
|-----------|-----------|------------|------------|----------|
| $47/week | $49/week | $2,444 | $2,548 | +$104 (4.25%) |
| $37/week | $39/week | $1,924 | $2,028 | +$104 (5.4%) |
| $27/week | $29/week | $1,404 | $1,508 | +$104 (7.4%) |

Then added .99:

| Old Price | New Price | Old Annual | New Annual | Increase |
|-----------|-----------|------------|------------|----------|
| $47/week | $49.99/week | $2,444 | $2,599 | +$155 (6.36%) |
| $37/week | $39.99/week | $1,924 | $2,079 | +$155 (8.1%) |
| $27/week | $29.99/week | $1,404 | $1,559 | +$155 (11.1%) |

No change in conversion. Average gym runs 12.5% margins, so adding 6-11% can nearly double profits.

**Exception - luxury items:** Luxury goods end on round numbers (0 or 5). The fact that it is expensive and not a bargain is what makes luxury inherently valuable. People who buy luxury do not want a deal. For everything else, .99 endings work.

**Premium vs luxury:** Premium goods reflect value in the price. Luxury goods derive value FROM the price. You can still add .99 and round up on premium offerings.

**Implementation:** Change 7s to 9s. Add .99 to all fees. Change contracts for new customers immediately.

### Play 7: Annual Renewal Fee on Top of Monthly (+10%)

**How it works:** Monthly rate + annual renewal fee charged on anniversary of contract signing.

**Case study (LA Fitness model):** People focus on the monthly rate but ignore the annualized cost.

| Fee Structure | Annualized | Effective Monthly |
|--------------|------------|-------------------|
| $39/mo x 12 | $468 | $39/mo |
| $39/mo x 12 + $99 annual fee | $567 | $47/mo (+20%) |

Advertise $39/month. Effectively collect $47/month. Get the benefit of the low price for sales/advertising and the benefit of the higher price for profits.

**Renewal fee revenue impact by multiplier:**

| Fee Amount | Revenue Increase |
|-----------|-----------------|
| 0.5x monthly rate | +4.15% |
| 1x monthly rate | +8.3% |
| 2x monthly rate | +16.6% |
| 3x monthly rate | +24.9% |

**Framing scripts:**
- "You pay this for rate protection. If you don't agree to the fee, you'll be subject to any price changes we make."

**Pro tip:** Have both a setup fee AND an annual renewal fee. You can waive one to get the other. Useful for incentivizing customers to take action.

**Implementation:** Add to contracts. Have customer initial next to rate and renewal fee. Call it "rate protection" or "cancellation fee prepayment." If they balk, drop it and they lose the benefit.

### Play 8: Automatic Continuity (+10%)

**How it works:** When someone finishes a program, package, or initial term, they auto-enroll into a stripped-down, low-cost recurring version unless they opt out.

**Core concept:** Create the most paired-down, zero-work version of your offering. Price it at 5-20% of the main thing. Tack it onto the end of whatever you sell on the front end.

**Psychology:** Sunk cost fallacy. They already spent $X on the main thing. Paying 5-10% to "keep it" feels logical. Like storage for services. They do not want to use it, but they do not want to lose it.

**Case study:** Workshop speaker doing $5M/year in education. Added one line to contract: after one year of course access, auto-rolls into $99/month "Continued Access." Did not affect front-end sales. Added $750,000/year.

**Case study ([CREATOR_NAME]):** Added automatic continuity. Generated $150,000/month in pure profit. So passive his COO asked what the revenue line item was.

**Math example:**

Current service:
- Price: $2,000/mo, Duration: 4 months, Gross margins: 70%
- LTV: $5,600 ($2,000 x 4 x 0.70)

After adding automatic continuity:
- Price: $200/mo (10% of main), Gross margins: 90%, Duration: 20 months, Conversion into this level: 50%
- Added LTV: +$1,800 (from $5,600 to $7,400 = 32% increase)
- Profit impact: 25-100% increase depending on margins

**What to sell as continuity:**
- Continued support / tech support / priority support
- Price protection
- Continued access / community access
- Insurance / warranty
- Any small feature that people need but is super low cost to maintain

**Implementation:**
1. List every product and service you sell on the front end
2. Find the feature(s) with value but low delivery cost from each service
3. Pick a price at 5-20% of normal rate
4. Bolt it onto every front-end purchase. Tell them they earn that rate after completing X time (improves stick rate too)
5. Collect high-profit revenue. Remarket to those customers to sell more over time

**Rule:** This is not hidden or forced continuity. Customers must agree to it upfront. Be clear about what happens after X time period. People like knowing there is a cost-effective version at the end.

### Play 9: Ultra-High-Ticket Anchor (+10-15%)

**How it works:** Add something to your product/service menu that is 10x or more expensive than your core offer. The "mac daddy" version.

**Psychology:** Price anchoring. The first expensive number anchors all subsequent prices. The $16,000 suit makes the $2,000 suit feel like a relief. The $500 customer was prepared to spend $500 max. He spent $2,000.

**Case study (tailor):** Showed $16,000 suit first. Customer ([CREATOR_NAME]) had a $500 budget. Bought the $2,000 suit. The gasp from the high price made the real price feel reasonable.

**Financial impact example:**

Before (two tiers):

| Price | Take Rate | Added LTV |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| $500 | 80% | $400 |
| $200 | 20% | $40 |
| **Old LTV** | | **$440** |

After (three tiers with ultra-high anchor):

| Price | Take Rate | Added LTV |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| $5,000 | 10% | $500 |
| $500 | 70% | $350 |
| $200 | 20% | $40 |
| **New LTV** | | **$890** |

LTV jumps from $440 to $890. The high anchor also shifts more people to the $500 tier because it now seems affordable by comparison.

**Case study:** Close friend implemented ultra-high option. Almost overnight, everyone started buying the premium product. Now it is all he sells. Made him millions per year.

**Implementation:** Think of the most absurd version of your offering. Price it at what would make you happy to deliver. Offer it first. Get the gasp. If they cannot afford it, present your main offer (which now looks reasonable).

**Rule:** Make sure you are willing to deliver the high-ticket offer. If you feel stressed when people buy it, raise the price until you smile when they buy.

### Play 10: Guarantee and Warranty Upsells (+5-20%)

**How it works:** Sell a guarantee or warranty as an add-on after the customer agrees to buy. Price at 5-30% of the main product.

**Case study (Apple):** Originally replaced damaged iPhones for free. Later turned it into AppleCare. Billions per year in revenue from something they used to give away.

**Case study (portfolio company):** High-end physical products with a free 10-year warranty. Started charging 10% of product price for the warranty. Huge uptake. Covered almost half the sales team's commissions.

**Math:**
- $1,000 product, $100 guarantee, $100 cost to replace
- 1 in 20 customers uses the warranty
- Revenue: 20 x $100 = $2,000
- Cost: 1 x $100 = $100
- Guarantee profit: $1,900

**Margin tip:** If your COGS is 10% of price, make the guarantee price equal to COGS. You never lose money. All profit.

**Example guarantee structures:**

| Product | Price | Guarantee | Add-on Price | Take Rate | Refund Rate | Added LTV | Revenue Lift |
|---------|-------|-----------|-------------|-----------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Wooden table | $1,000 | No watermarks or replacement | $100 | 45% | 5% | $43 | 4.3% |
| Personal training | $3,000 | Lose weight or money back | $2,000 | 35% | 10% | $630 | 21.0% |
| Dry cleaning | $200 | No tears/stains or refund | $20 | 25% | 5% | $5 | 2.4% |

Even a 4% revenue increase from warranties can mean 20%+ profit increase.

**Sales script:** "You just want the standard warranty on that?" They ask what it is. "Oh, it's for anyone who likes that extra bit of peace of mind. If anything happens to your X, you're covered. A lot of people do it."

**Implementation:**
- Works at every price point (shipping insurance proves this for cheap items)
- If someone did not buy the warranty, you can still honor it as a goodwill gesture (you probably do this for free already)
- People hate risk and will pay you to take it from them. As long as you price it right, it is very profitable.

## Part 6: Why You Must Optimize Pricing

### Benefits of Optimized Pricing

- More profit without more customers
- Attract better customers who are ready to buy
- Steal customers from competitors and gain market share
- Higher customer satisfaction (people feel good about fair pricing)
- Faster sales (prices match willingness to pay)
- More repeat customers
- Better cash flow for reinvestment
- Fewer discounts needed
- Boosts brand value
- Maximizes revenue per sale

### Consequences of Never Changing Prices

- Eroding margins from inflation
- Driving away good customers (too cheap signals low quality)
- Constant discounting creates "price terrorists" who always negotiate
- Unsold inventory tying up cash
- Undervaluing your product
- Competitors with better pricing outcompete you
- Cash flow problems
- Missing growth opportunities from insufficient reinvestment capital

## Part 7: Quick Reference for AI

### Decision Framework

1. **Pricing beats everything.** A 1% pricing improvement is worth 2x a 1% retention improvement and 4x a 1% acquisition improvement.
2. **Value-based pricing is the target.** Charge based on what customers will pay, not what competitors charge or what it costs you.
3. **Test every quarter.** Nudge up 20% every 10 sales. Go back to the sweet spot when conversions drop dramatically.
4. **The perfect price maximizes conversion rate x lifetime gross profit,** not raw sales volume.
5. **Stack micro-plays for macro results.** Ten small pricing optimizations (1-10% each) compound into massive profit increases.
6. **Small revenue increases create large profit increases.** A 10% price increase on a 10% margin business doubles profit.

### Core Math to Remember

- 2x price with same customers = 6x profit (costs stay flat)
- 10% price raise on 10% margin business = 2x profit
- 4% processing fee on 9% margin $1M business = +44% profit
- 28-day billing on 20% margin business = +41.5% profit increase
- Annual billing can 5x LTV vs monthly
- Stacking all 10 plays = 26.8%-63.8% revenue increase = potential 4x-6x profit on a 9% margin business
