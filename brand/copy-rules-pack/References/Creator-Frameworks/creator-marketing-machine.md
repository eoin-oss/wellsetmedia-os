## Core Concept

The Marketing Machine is a system that turns normal business operations into a steady stream of customer-generated ads. Instead of the founder being the face of all advertising, customers become the creative engine. The business captures proof of results as a byproduct of what it already does.

**Why it matters:**
- A business dependent on the founder's face is less valuable and harder to sell
- 80% of Gym Launch's top 50 performing ads did not feature [CREATOR_NAME]'s face. They were customer-generated.
- This system drove a $46.2M exit because it proved the business could grow without the founder
- Without a marketing machine, you are doomed to always figuring out the next ad. You are only as good as your last campaign. Getting new business always relies on you.

**Core principle:** The bottom of the operations list (sales calls, onboarding, support, upsells, coaching, events) feeds the top of the list (advertising creative and copy). Capture the moments that already happen.

## Origin Story

2019. Gym Launch was making millions per month, but the business depended on [CREATOR_NAME]'s face to bring in the money. Bankers told him the business was unsellable because of keyman risk.

**The "hit by a bus" question:** "If I got hit by a bus today, what would keep the business growing tomorrow?" Two-part answer: list everything you do, then build systems to do those things without you.

**Everything [CREATOR_NAME] did in the business:**

- Making advertising funnels
- Sales calls
- Onboarding calls
- Support calls
- Upsell calls
- Coaching calls

The bottom half of the list (sales, onboarding, support, events) could be hired and trained for. The top half (advertising) was the problem. How to find another him?

**The breakthrough:** At an awards dinner for high-achieving gym owners, they shared stories about their experiences. Sales calls, advertising breakthroughs, revenue records. Then it hit: the bottom of the list feeds the top of the list.

- Cut up sales, onboarding, and upsell calls to create a "life journey of a customer"

A few weeks later, the member-generated ads were outperforming even the best direct-to-camera founder ads. New ones were coming in every day.

12 months later, the second meeting with bankers: "Take a look at our media. I haven't been in any of it for the past twelve months." Sorted top ads by sales generated. All customer-generated. Months later, closed the deal for $46.2M.

## How The Marketing Machine Works

Think of the machine as a checklist. Every item you check off represents a reliable stream of videos, images, and words you can use to advertise your business. Use them in paid ads, content, outreach, sales pitches, webinars, and video sales letters. Anywhere you need to sell stuff, you can plug in stuff from the machine.

**The 7 content streams:**
1. Lifecycle
2. Social Media
3. Events
4. Communication, Communities, and Review Site Scrape
5. Bonuses, Milestones, and Awards
6. Competition
7. Delivery

**One > None.** Do not get overwhelmed. If you are starting out, one or two of these processes can completely change your life and marketing. Read them all. Start with the one that works best for your business and is easiest to implement. Come back and add another, and so on. You will not make less money by capturing more proof that you are good.

## Stream 1: Lifecycle Ads

These ads harness the "moments" that occur in normal conversations in your business. Since Gym Launch was remote, all client interactions occurred over tech platforms. All calls were recorded, creating a massive gallery of content to pull moments from.

| Stage | Source | What to Capture |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **Before** | Sales calls | The prospect at their pain point, describing their struggle |
| **During** | Support/coaching calls | Progress moments, small wins, breakthroughs |
| **After** | Ascension/milestone calls | Victory moments, big results, emotional payoffs |

This allowed showing rather than telling a client's entire journey through time. Rather than recalling hard times, you can actually see the person at their hard time and at their victorious after moment. Super powerful.

### Lifecycle Ads Checklist

- Legal: Add compliance and permission language to all customer contracts
- IT: Record all calls: sales, onboarding, support, ascension, milestone calls (when clients get good results)
- IT: Buy extra storage space so calls do not get deleted. Turn off auto-delete.
- All departments: Create calendar events for all calls and include the client's name in the title
- Sales: Send the happy-customer calls to marketing each week
- Marketing: Search your system for all calendar events with that person's name. Cross reference calendar events to make sure names and dates match.
- Marketing: Clip the "before" moment from the initial sales calls, a "during" moment from 1-2 support calls, and the "after" moment from an upsell, milestone, or testimonial call
- Marketing: Create a friendly labeling system for these clips so you can grab them at any time and at a moment's notice
- Marketing: Run them and print money

**Note:** All clients consent to using recordings for compliance, training, and advertising purposes. Even though they sign a document, still ask if they feel OK with using their story. If they say no, honor their wishes. Plenty more successful clients feel ecstatic to share.

### For Local/Physical Businesses: "Upon Delivery" Ads

These only work if you have some sort of physical delivery. Think of these like a "ribbon cutting" ceremony:
- The person has their eyes closed then sees their kitchen for the first time
- Hops on the scale after X days
- Sees their teeth after getting veneers
- For a recruiting firm, "you got the job!" reactions

Many businesses have these big moments but fail to capture them. You want those raw reactions. Those become the hook (first 3-5 seconds) of the ad. Then make ads of each one, and highlight reels of only the reactions (these crush).

## Stream 2: Social Media Scrape

Harvest customer-generated content from social platforms.

### Social Media Scrape Checklist

- Marketing: Have a saying or hashtag for your community/customers to post on social media. Definitely have a profile they can tag you in.
  - Gym Launch examples: #gymlord #gymlaunch #gymowner
  - Prestige Labs: #poweredbyprestige
  - [CREATOR_NAME]: #neverskipdessert #acquisition #100moffers #100mleads
- Marketing: Set regular reminders to grab tagged media across all platforms. Especially things like Instagram/Snapchat/Facebook stories, which disappear within 24 hours.
  - Like the call storage, tag/save what you find with relevant facts and create searchable names.
- Marketing: Separate studs from the duds. Create a separate folder for media that outperformed typical posts. Good organic performance correlates highly with good ad and outbound performance.
- Marketing: Use the best-performing images and videos as creative with already-performing ad copy. Yes, including stuff you posted.

## Stream 3: In-Person Events

If you are an in-person business you have an insane advantage over virtual businesses. Every day is an in-person event. If you are virtual, be strategic about capturing moments from events. If you are not doing events at least 1-2x per year, start. They give you great marketing assets.

[CREATOR_NAME] did not even sell anything at Gym Launch events. Just provided value. But documented as much as possible for advertisements. Since events cost a fortune and he did not sell anything, that media was the only way to get returns. Had to get good at squeezing every last drop of marketing power from every event.

Three types of event ads: stage ads, professional testimonials, and man in the street.

### Stage Ads

- Get a picture while you are on stage with people celebrating behind you. Great visual proof of the size and scope of your event. These ads and short clips do very well.
- Get multiple selfie images and a video if you can swing it. You only get one take, so make it count.
- Do this at the end of day one or before lunch on day two.

### Professional Testimonials (6-Point Script)

- Reserve two separate rooms at the event. One for the event itself, one small room close by for recording. If not possible, get 2-3 extra hotel rooms for testimonial setup.
- Hire at least one extra camera team (sometimes two). One records the event. The other collects testimonials. Testimonials are more important, so put the better team on those. At least two people on cameras and one runner to bring clients to and from the recording area.
- Set up lighting and sound so it looks good. Have multiple mics and cameras. Then TEST. TEST. TEST. The worst thing that happens is you lose audio or video from every testimonial for an entire event. You will want to kill everyone.
- Pick out the customers you want testimonials from ahead of time. Confirm in writing.
  - Let them know it will not be during any big keynote and you will give them notes for the few minutes they miss.
- Print out a hit list. Put it on a clipboard. Check names off as they come in.
- Announce at the beginning of the event that your team will be doing this. Introduce the handler to everyone so when they get a tap on the shoulder they know exactly what to do.
- Give people an incentive to leave a testimonial. Give the handler an extra $50 for every person he captures not on the list. When going up to people on the list, he can usually grab people off the list too.
- Plan when you will pull people back for recordings. Avoid big keynotes. If someone pushes back, put them on a separate list to grab during mornings, lunches, breaks, and end of day.

**Walk them through the 6-point testimonial script:**

| Point | Question |
|-------|---------|
| 1. Internal Struggle | What was rock bottom before doing this? What did you have to do that you hated? What things did you want to do that you could not? |
| 2. External Struggle | What were your objective metrics? |
| 3. Skepticism | What were your main concerns about buying/clicking/joining? |
| 4. Overcome | What made you do it anyway? |
| 5. External Victory | What are your objective metrics now? |
| 6. Internal Victory | What has been the best moment since? What can you do now you could not do before? What things did you hate doing you do not have to do anymore? |

**Critical details:**
- The most important part of the testimonial is the hook. Be explicit. Have them say a few different hooks to see what works better before they get into their story.
- Have them say their name at the beginning of the video. Your camera team often has no idea who they are, so this helps match names to videos later.
- Save them. Upload them. Add music. Run them.

### Man in the Street

Quick one-question interviews during breaks. Think of these as one-question interviews that provide lots of proof around a key metric or concept.

- If you do man in the street in addition to pro testimonials, hire a second two-man camera team for these.
- Prepare 10 core questions you want many answers to ahead of time. You will want 10+ responses for each. These take seconds to capture. Turn them into fast-hitting highlight reels of everyone saying a specific outcome related to one question.

**Example questions:**
- How many new customers have you gained since you started?
- What were you making before X vs now?
- How much extra income has X added?
- How much weight have you lost since you started here?
- How much has your credit score improved?
- What was your old credit score and what is it now? From what to what?

**6-point rapid fire option:** You can also ask the 6 core questions from the interview script, but piecemeal them one at a time. Then make a combined testimonial that has fast-hitting answers for each point from 5+ people. Even more compelling.

- If you do the 6-point script rapid fire, ask all six questions to each person in under 2 minutes. Weave the same characters through the testimonial reel.
- Print out the questions for the camera team. Put a space below each question to tally up the number of responses.
- Ask only that question until you get the number of answers you want.

## Stream 4: Communication and Communities Scrape

Three sources to scrape regularly. This process weaponizes all the great little moments during customer interactions, then bundles them into proof.

### Support/Account Rep Chats

Screenshot the juicy messages customers send whenever things go well or they get some great result.

- Create a list of all key terms people say in messages that indicate a win. Example list: PIF, Sales, $, Profit, Revenue, Monthly, close, closed, leads, show, scheduled, win.
- Add a folder that you give all your support reps access to.
- Set a reminder or add to your team's checklist to search all key terms in their messages.
- Sort by date and screenshot the new ones.
- Add them to the folder.
- Optionally, add subfolders for specific types: sales, marketing, profit, specific programs or tiers of service.
- Set a reminder for managers to note who turned in screenshots and give them kudos. If you are the manager, that is you. If you do not reward them for doing it, they will not do it.
- Optional: anyone who gets a winning ad from their screenshot gets a $100 gift card every week.

### Community Wins/Posts

Comments and posts made in whatever community you manage for customers.

- Create a post category called "wins." If your community platform cannot do this, skip it.
- Create a community wins folder. Optional subfolders by tier, program, service level.
- Every Friday encourage customers to comment/post with their "wins of the week."
- Set a reminder to make the win of the week post and add it to the weekly end of week checklist/report to the community manager.
- Monday: start of week checklist, screenshot the wins that came in over the weekend.
- Search for the same key terms the support team does but inside the group. Sort by newest.
- Screenshot the best ones. Add them to the respective folders.
- BONUS: Screen record scrolling through the comments/wins of the community for the week (or over multiple weeks). This gives you yet another ad to run.

### Scraping Reviews and Comments

Works the exact same way as the above two, just different source.

- Create a list of all the sites you have reviews on: Glassdoor, Yelp, Google, Facebook, Trustpilot, Amazon, etc.
- Create a folder for review site testimonials.
- Set a calendar reminder to go to each site and sort by newest.
- Screenshot the good ones. Add to folder.
- Set a reminder for the manager to check them and reward the person for doing it (or they will not).

**Important:** Adding stuff to folders will not get you more customers. You need to USE all that stuff in your advertisements.

## Stream 5: Bonuses and Awards

### Bonus Unlocks

Create some giveaway that you charge separately for. It could be a training, a tiny project, one-time access, or a status tier. Anything you can think of. But make it big and sexy. For Gym Launch, sales scripts or ad training for different platforms did the trick.

- Make the sexy thing. Attach a price to it. Make it for sale.
- Sell people on how great it is. Then tell people they can buy it, or, if they make a testimonial video, they can unlock it for free. Put the recording of you pitching it somewhere all customers see.
- Tell them what to do to unlock it:
  - Send a video under 5 minutes that hits the 6 points to [EMAIL_ADDRESS]
  - Use this video as a reference/example (put your best one)
  - Post the video in the group with your stats (this way you automatically get a second testimonial in written format)
  - Tag the community manager in your post
  - Include other keywords to make them easy to scrape later

### Award Testimonials (4 Proofs Per Person)

If you do not already have an aspirational goal for your customers, make one. For gym owners, it was hitting a $1M/year gym. For your customers, it may be a bikini picture, a photoshoot of their remodeled kitchen, whatever fits. People like to get recognized in meaningful ways for their meaningful results. Create an award. Associate it with status. Bestow it on people in exchange for testimonials.

**Making the award:** Make the trophy, trinket, model something tied to your community. Make it big and cool. Spend some money on it. It will make you more back. People have to crave this thing. Do not do a boring thing. At Gym Launch, they did a sword and shield for the Gym Lords community. Swords are common in the fitness world as trophies for bodybuilding shows. People associate them with prestige.

**How to get 4 forms of proof from one award per person:**

1. **PROOF 1:** Make a post showing the actual number they hit in the group using the template you use for testimonials. Tag the community owner.
2. **PROOF 2:** Send email with video to [EMAIL_ADDRESS]
3. **PROOF 3:** Record a video of them unboxing the award when it arrives
4. **PROOF 4:** Record them in person or on stage when you present them with one in front of the community. (This means you have a personalized/engraved one you mail, and a generic one you present on stage.)

## Stream 6: Competitions

Run 1-2x per year maximum. People tire of them. This is a raw iPhone-style testimonial competition where people compete for the best one. Measure the best by its performance as an ad. The one with the most conversions over 30 days wins.

### Competition Checklist

- Set an announcement date and a deadline for submission
- Figure out a crazy award for the person who wins (e.g., 6 months of free service/product)
- Give them instructions on how to submit:
  - "Fill in this results template" (create your own)
  - Attach their video, tag the community manager, and email the raw file to [EMAIL_ADDRESS]
- Give them last time's winner (or your best one to date) as a reference
- Attach the 6-point testimonial script
- Announce the competition via all internal channels (email, group messages, community posts)
- Countdown to the date
- Make personal invitations and do regular follow up with the top 50 or so people you want testimonials from
  - Ask them to do it in the moment. "Like right now, it takes 5 minutes, just take out your phone so you don't have to think about it." The rawer the better.
- Cut off the competition submissions
- Format them as ads then run them
- Set a calendar reminder thirty days in the future to count which ads did best and who the winner is
- Announce the award publicly

## Stream 7: Delivery (Lifecycle Compilation)

Cut up the customers who won awards, hit milestones, or ascended into one "lifecycle" video showing the complete customer journey.

## Implementation Priority Order

If starting from scratch, implement in this order:

| Priority | Stream | Reason |
|----------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Competition | Big influx of content all at once |
| 2 | Chat/messaging scrape | Already have this content. Minimal editing. |
| 3 | Community scrape | Already have this content. Minimal editing. |
| 4 | Reviews scrape | Already have this content. Minimal editing. |
| 5 | Social media scrape | Already have this content. Minimal editing. |
| 6 | Bonus unlocks | Requires creating something to give away. Worth it, but more effort. |
| 7 | Award unlocks | Requires creating a trophy/award. Worth it, but more effort. |
| 8 | Events | Lots of work to produce, but more than pay for themselves in proof alone |
| 9 | Lifecycle | Most editing horsepower required. Very unique, but time-consuming. Good thing there are people who will work in exchange for money. |

**Reason for this order:** The first one gives you a big influx. The next four, you already have this stuff and it takes minimal editing. Bonuses and awards require making something to give away, so a tiny bit more effort (well worth it). Events are a lot of work to put on, but they more than pay for themselves in proof alone. Lifecycle ads are loved, but take the most editing horsepower.

## Weekly Marketing Machine Output

Every week your team should deliver:

- A list of names of people who got great results
- Scraped social media from you, tags, and customers (both images and videos)
- Chat/messaging screenshots from your account reps
- Community post and comments testimonials (screenshots, videos, comments section screen recordings)
- All new 5-star reviews on external sites pushing into your business (screenshots + scroll recordings) for even more ads
- Bonus unlocks (post screenshots + videos to inbox)
- Award deliveries and other customer recognition stuff (screenshots, emailed raw testimonials)
- Customer reaction videos (unboxing, first-time user, long-time user, customer demonstrations)

Make this the marketing team's start-of-week checklist. Check all the above every Monday.

Every six months, you will get an influx from events and competitions. Run the testimonial competitions between your other events. If you run one competition per year and two events per year, you get a big flow from them every twelve weeks. For some, this is all the ad content they will ever need.

## Quick Reference

1. **Customers are your best creative engine.** 80% of top-performing ads came from customers, not the founder. The business sold for $46.2M because it did not depend on the founder's face.
2. **Capture, do not create.** The moments already exist in your business operations. Build systems to capture them. The bottom of the operations list feeds the top.
3. **The 6-point testimonial script is the backbone.** Internal struggle, external struggle, skepticism, overcome, external victory, internal victory.
4. **Start with competitions and scraping.** They give you the most content with the least new effort.
5. **Every week should produce new proof.** Make it a checklist, not a one-time project.
6. **Scaling advertising = more, better, and new ads.** More better new ads means more better new customers reliably. Reliable customers means a very valuable business.
7. **Without a marketing machine, you are a camera slave with an unsellable business.** Prospective customers want to see real customer results anyway. Show them.
8. **If you have a small number of customers:** Refer to Warm Outreach in $100M Leads. Work for free. Get those testimonials. Then start the flow of customers that follow and begin to spin the wheel.
