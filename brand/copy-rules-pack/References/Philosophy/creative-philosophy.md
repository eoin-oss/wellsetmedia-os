# Our Philosophy on Creatives

## The Goal of Marketing

Marketing has one job: show the prospect that we are exponentially better, newer, and different from everything they've tried or seen before.

Not marginally better. Not better in the same way. **Exponentially better, in a way they haven't encountered.**

This is the bar. If the creative doesn't achieve this, it will produce weak results regardless of targeting, budget, or creative quality. The message is the mechanism — not the production.

---

## The 3 Questions Every Creative Must Answer

Before producing any ad, answer these three questions about the target prospect:

**1. What do they want?**
Not what the offer delivers — what they actually, emotionally want. The outcome. The identity shift. The life that becomes possible. This must be specific, not generic.
- Generic: "they want to make more money"
- Specific: "they want to stop feeling like they're grinding alone and finally have a system that brings clients to them consistently, so they can stop posting every day for zero results"

**2. What has already failed them?**
Every prospect has tried something before this ad appeared. What was it? Why didn't it work? The ad must acknowledge this — specifically. When a prospect hears their own failed attempt named and explained, they feel understood. Feeling understood is the prerequisite to considering anything new.

**3. What do they see every day?**
What other ads, offers, gurus, and promises are they exposed to? The creative must be positioned against this landscape — not just different in content, but visually, tonally, and structurally distinct. If the ad looks like every other ad in the space, it competes on a level where all claims start to blur together.

---

## The Creative Framework

Every high-performing creative follows this structure:

**Hook → Prove the Claim → Reason Why → CTA**

**Hook**
The first 3 seconds. One job: stop the scroll. The hook must speak directly to the specific desire or pain in Question 1 or 2. Abstract hooks do not stop scrolls.

Good hook pattern: [specific situation the ICP recognises] + [unexpected element or result]
> "I had $200 in my bank account when I figured out how to generate 40 strategy calls per month without posting content"

**Prove the Claim**
Don't assert — demonstrate. Show the result, show the mechanism, show the evidence. The moment you say "our system is the best" without proof, you've lost the sophisticated viewer. Proof mechanisms:
- Screen recordings of results
- Client testimonials speaking to the specific outcome
- Walking through the exact process step by step
- Showing the before and after with specific numbers

**Reason Why**
Explain why this works when other things haven't. This is the mechanism — the new, specific process or principle that makes the outcome possible. The reason why must be:
- New (not something they've heard before)
- Logical (they can follow the reasoning)
- Specific (not vague language like "our unique approach")

**CTA**
One action. Make it frictionless. The call-to-action should feel like a natural next step, not a pressure close. For VSL funnels: "Watch the free training" or "See how it works."

---

## What This Means in Practice

**Write hooks as specific situations, not statements.**
Not: "Are you struggling to get clients?"
Yes: "I was sending 50 cold DMs a day and booking zero calls before I realised I was doing this one thing wrong..."

**Name the failed solutions explicitly.**
Not: "Most methods don't work"
Yes: "If you've tried posting content daily, going viral, or cold outreach and still can't get consistent bookings — here's why none of those actually solve the problem..."

**Lead with proof before promise.**
Not: "We'll get you 20+ strategy calls per month"
Yes: "[Client name] went from 0 to 23 strategy calls in 30 days. Here's exactly what changed."

---

## What Great Creatives Are Not

- Not long production values. A phone-shot video with the right message outperforms a studio shoot with the wrong one.
- Not clever. Clever is forgettable. Clear and specific wins.
- Not selling the service. The ad sells curiosity and the next click. The VSL sells the call. The call sells the offer.
- Not for everyone. A creative that tries to appeal to everyone appeals to no one. Narrow the specificity until it feels too niche — then you're close.
