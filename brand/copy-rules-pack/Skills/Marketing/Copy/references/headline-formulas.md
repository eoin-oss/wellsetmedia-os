# Headline Formula Library

> Comprehensive reference for the copywriter agent. Write 5-10 headline options before selecting one. One headline can outpull another by 19.5x with the same product and offer. Headline selection is the highest-leverage copy decision you make.

---

## How to Use This Reference

1. Identify the **awareness level** of your traffic (from the Pre-Writing Analysis)
2. Identify the **market sophistication stage**
3. Select 3-4 formula categories that match
4. Write 2-3 variations per formula
5. Pick the top 5-10 for the headline bank in copy-foundation.md
6. Recommend the top 2-3 for A/B testing

**Awareness Level to Headline Type Map:**

| Awareness Level | Best Headline Types |
|----------------|-------------------|
| 1 - Unaware | Story, Contrarian, Question, Curiosity |
| 2 - Problem Aware | Curiosity, Question, How-To, Pain-Based |
| 3 - Solution Aware | Benefit, Comparison, Proof, How-To |
| 4 - Product Aware | Proof, Benefit, Urgency, Comparison |
| 5 - Most Aware | Urgency, Proof, Direct Offer |

---

## 1. Curiosity Headlines

> Open a loop the reader must close. Works across all awareness levels. Strongest for Levels 1-3 where you need to pull the reader in before making claims.

**Formula 1a: The Secret**
`The [surprising] secret behind [desirable outcome]`

- "The 11-minute morning routine behind $50K months"
- "The hiring mistake behind 73% of failed agencies"
- "The forgotten ad format behind our best-performing Q4 campaigns"

**Formula 1b: The Unexpected Connection**
`Why [unexpected thing] is the key to [desired outcome]`

- "Why your best clients are costing you $4,200/month"
- "Why 80% of your landing page doesn't matter"
- "Why your competitor's worst ad is outperforming your best one"

**Formula 1c: The Warning**
`[Number] [things] you're doing right now that [negative consequence]`

- "5 things in your sales script that kill deals before the close"
- "3 email habits that are training your list to ignore you"
- "7 ad targeting settings that are burning your budget overnight"

**Formula 1d: The Forbidden Fruit**
`What [authority/competitor] doesn't want you to know about [topic]`

- "What your ad agency doesn't want you to know about ROAS reporting"
- "What top closers never say on a sales call"
- "What the course industry doesn't tell you about completion rates"

**When to use curiosity:** Cold traffic, unaware audiences, email subject lines, ad hooks. Avoid for most-aware audiences who already know what they want.

## 2. Benefit Headlines

> Lead with the outcome the reader wants. The more specific the benefit, the stronger the pull. Works best at Levels 3-5. [Hopkins, Caples]

**Formula 2a: Specific Result + Timeframe** [Caples]
`[Specific outcome] in [specific timeframe]`

- "47 qualified leads in your first 30 days"
- "$12,000 in recurring revenue within 90 days of implementation"
- "Cut your sales cycle from 45 days to 12 days"

**Formula 2b: Result + Without Sacrifice**
`[Desirable outcome] without [thing they fear losing]`

- "Scale to $100K/month without hiring a single employee"
- "Fill your calendar with sales calls without spending a dollar on ads"
- "Double your close rate without memorizing a single script"

**Formula 2c: Result + Comparison**
`Get [result] [comparison to current situation]`

- "Get 3x the leads at half the ad spend"
- "Close deals in one call instead of three"
- "Build landing pages in 20 minutes that outperform pages that took you 2 weeks"

**Formula 2d: Transformation Statement**
`From [painful before state] to [desirable after state]`

- "From 4 sales calls a week to 4 a day"
- "From guessing what to post to having 90 days of content ready in one afternoon"
- "From dreading your inbox to opening it to find $8,400 in new orders"

**When to use benefit:** Solution-aware and product-aware audiences. When you have specific proof to back the claim. Avoid for unaware audiences (the benefit won't land if they don't know they have the problem).

## 3. Proof Headlines

> Lead with evidence. Let the numbers or the social proof do the selling. Strongest at Levels 4-5. [Ogilvy, Hopkins]

**Formula 3a: Specific Number** [Ogilvy]
`[Specific number] + [unexpected detail]`

- "At 60 miles an hour, the loudest noise in this Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock" [Ogilvy]
- "1,847 business owners switched to this CRM in January. Here's why."
- "We've spent $14.3 million on Meta ads. This is the only framework that survived."

**Formula 3b: Case Study Lead**
`How [named person] achieved [specific result] in [timeframe]`

- "How Sarah Chen went from $3,200/month to $41,000/month in 127 days"
- "How a 3-person agency landed a $180K retainer using one email"
- "How Marcus Rivera closed 22 deals in 30 days after 4 months of zero sales"

**Formula 3c: Aggregate Proof**
`[Large number] [people] have used [method] to [result]`

- "12,400 coaches have used this system to book sales calls on autopilot"
- "Over $240M in client revenue generated using this ad framework"
- "638 agencies have replaced their entire cold outreach with this 3-step process"

**Formula 3d: Testimonial Headline**
`"[Verbatim client quote with specific result]"`

- "'I went from 2 clients to 14 in 6 weeks. My pipeline has never been this full.'"
- "'We cut our cost per acquisition from $187 to $31 in the first month.'"
- "'I made back my investment in 11 days. Wish I'd started 6 months ago.'"

**When to use proof:** When you have strong, specific proof. Product-aware and most-aware audiences. Landing pages and sales pages where credibility is the primary barrier. Avoid when proof is vague or unverified.

## 4. Urgency Headlines

> Create legitimate time pressure. Only use when urgency is real. Manufactured urgency destroys trust. [Schwartz]

**Formula 4a: Deadline**
`[Offer/Opportunity] closes [specific date/time]`

- "Enrollment closes Friday at midnight. 23 spots remain."
- "This pricing disappears after March 15th."
- "The Q2 cohort starts April 1st. Applications close in 72 hours."

**Formula 4b: Market Timing**
`[External event] is creating a [window/opportunity] that won't last`

- "The iOS 17 update just changed email marketing forever. Here's how to adapt before Q4."
- "AI is making this strategy obsolete by 2026. There's still time to profit from it."
- "Your competitors haven't figured this out yet. That window is closing."

**Formula 4c: Cost of Waiting**
`Every [time period] without [solution], you're losing [specific amount]`

- "Every month without a follow-up system costs the average agency $23,400 in lost revenue"
- "Every unoptimized ad dollar is 3 dollars of revenue you'll never see"
- "Every week your sales page stays unchanged, you're leaving 40% of buyers on the table"

**When to use urgency:** Most-aware audiences ready to buy, launch sequences, cart-close emails, limited enrollment offers. Only when the scarcity or deadline is real.

## 5. Question Headlines

> Engage the reader's mind directly. Questions force internal responses. Best for problem-aware audiences. [Caples]

**Formula 5a: Do You Make This Mistake?** [Caples]
`Do you make these [number] [mistakes/errors] in [activity]?`

- "Do you make these 5 mistakes on your sales calls?"
- "Are you making the same pricing error as 80% of agency owners?"
- "Is your email sequence accidentally pushing buyers away?"

**Formula 5b: What If**
`What if you could [desired outcome] by [surprisingly simple method]?`

- "What if you could fill next month's calendar with one 15-minute task?"
- "What if your worst-performing ad could be fixed with a 4-word change?"
- "What if the reason your funnel isn't converting has nothing to do with your copy?"

**Formula 5c: Diagnostic**
`Which of these [number] [categories] describes your [situation]?`

- "Which of these 4 business stages are you in? Your growth strategy depends on it."
- "Which of these 3 funnel problems is costing you the most revenue?"
- "Which type of buyer is your sales page accidentally repelling?"

**Formula 5d: Rhetorical Challenge**
`Can you [modest standard] or is [unexpected blocker] holding you back?`

- "Can you explain what you sell in one sentence? Most business owners can't."
- "Can your landing page pass the 5-second test?"
- "Are you charging enough, or are you leaving $50K a year on the table?"

**When to use questions:** Email subject lines, ad hooks for problem-aware audiences, VSL openings. Avoid closed questions that can be answered with "no" and dismissed.

## 6. Contrarian Headlines

> Challenge conventional wisdom. Position against what everyone else is saying. Powerful for sophisticated markets (Stage 4-5). [Schwartz, Halbert]

**Formula 6a: Everything You Know Is Wrong**
`Everything [authority/industry] told you about [topic] is wrong`

- "Everything your marketing agency told you about ROAS is wrong"
- "The 'post every day' advice is killing your engagement. Here's the data."
- "Why 'know your avatar' is the worst advice in marketing right now"

**Formula 6b: The Opposite Works**
`Stop [common advice]. [Opposite approach] works better.`

- "Stop split-testing your headlines. Fix your offer instead."
- "Stop chasing new leads. Your past buyers are worth 8x more."
- "Stop writing long-form content. One paragraph did more for our SEO than 40 blog posts."

**Formula 6c: The Heretic**
`I [did the opposite of accepted practice] and [positive result]`

- "I fired my entire marketing team and revenue went up 34%"
- "I stopped posting on social media for 6 months. My business grew $220K."
- "I raised my prices 400% and got more clients than ever"

**Formula 6d: Industry Myth**
`The [industry] myth that's costing you [specific amount]`

- "The 'more traffic' myth that's costing agencies $10K/month"
- "The conversion rate myth that made us rewrite every sales page"
- "The 'content is king' lie that kept my business stuck at $15K/month for two years"

**When to use contrarian:** Sophisticated markets where audiences have heard every claim. Expert-positioned brands. Thought leadership content. Avoid for brand-new markets where conventional wisdom hasn't been established yet.

## 7. How-To Headlines

> Promise a method. Works for problem-aware and solution-seeking audiences. The more specific the how-to, the stronger it pulls. [Caples, Schwartz]

**Formula 7a: How To + Specific Result**
`How to [achieve specific result] in [timeframe/conditions]`

- "How to book 15 sales calls a week without cold outreach"
- "How to write a VSL that converts at 12%+ in a single afternoon"
- "How to build a $50K/month agency with zero paid advertising"

**Formula 7b: How To + Avoid Pain**
`How to [avoid specific pain] even if [common obstacle]`

- "How to stop losing clients to cheaper competitors even if you refuse to lower your prices"
- "How to avoid the feast-or-famine cycle even if you have zero marketing skills"
- "How to prevent ad account shutdowns even if Meta keeps changing the rules"

**Formula 7c: Step-by-Step**
`The [number]-step process for [desired outcome]`

- "The 5-step process for turning one client into a $240K/year revenue stream"
- "The 3-step framework that took us from $0 to $1M in ad spend management"
- "The 7-step sales call structure that closes 40% of qualified prospects"

**Formula 7d: How I / How We**
`How [I/we] [achieved specific result] (and how you can too)`

- "How we generated 2,300 leads in 14 days for $0.87 each"
- "How I went from freelancer to agency owner in 6 months using one strategy"
- "How we rebuilt our client's entire funnel and 4.7x'd their revenue in 90 days"

**When to use how-to:** Educational content, lead magnets, blog posts, YouTube titles, VSL hooks for solution-seeking audiences. Avoid when the audience doesn't know they have a problem yet.

## 8. List Headlines

> Numbers in headlines increase click-through rates. Odd numbers outperform even numbers. Specific numbers outperform round numbers. [Caples]

**Formula 8a: The Numbered List**
`[Number] [ways/methods/strategies] to [desired outcome]`

- "11 ways to reduce your cost per lead without changing your targeting"
- "7 email subject line formulas that get 40%+ open rates"
- "23 sales objections and exactly how to handle each one"

**Formula 8b: The Surprising Number**
`[Unexpected number] + [context that makes it surprising]`

- "We tested 147 ad hooks. These 3 outperformed all others combined."
- "93% of landing pages fail this one test"
- "$4.2 million in revenue from a 187-word email"

**Formula 8c: The Ranking**
`The #1 [thing] for [desired outcome] (it's not what you think)`

- "The #1 reason sales calls fail has nothing to do with your pitch"
- "The #1 factor in landing page conversion isn't your headline or your offer"
- "The #1 lead source for $10M+ agencies isn't ads, SEO, or referrals"

**When to use lists:** Social media posts, email subject lines, blog posts, ad hooks. Best for audiences actively researching and comparing options. Avoid in long-form sales environments where a list format may feel lightweight.

## 9. Comparison Headlines

> Position against alternatives. Helps the reader make a decision by reframing the choice. Best for product-aware audiences evaluating options.

**Formula 9a: Us vs. Them**
`[Your solution] vs. [alternative]: [key difference]`

- "Done-for-you ads vs. DIY: why one costs $15K less per year with 3x the results"
- "Coaching vs. courses: which one actually gets clients to $50K/month"
- "Retainer vs. performance-based: the pricing model that changed our agency"

**Formula 9b: Before/After**
`Before [solution]: [painful state]. After: [desirable state].`

- "Before the system: 60-hour weeks, 3 clients, constant stress. After: 25-hour weeks, 12 clients, predictable revenue."
- "Before the framework: writing one email took all day. After: a complete 5-email sequence in 90 minutes."
- "Before the process: 2% close rate. After: 38% close rate. Same leads."

**Formula 9c: Cost Comparison**
`The real cost of [alternative] vs. [your solution]`

- "The real cost of running your own ads: $47,000/year in wasted spend and 600 hours of trial and error"
- "Hiring an in-house copywriter: $85K/year. This system: $997 once."
- "The cost of 'figuring it out yourself' over the next 12 months"

**When to use comparison:** Product-aware audiences deciding between options. Competitor-heavy markets. Sales pages and long-form landing pages. Avoid when the audience hasn't yet decided they need a solution.

## 10. Story Headlines

> Lead with narrative. The oldest and most reliable advertising format. Stories lower defenses, build rapport, and make claims feel natural. [Caples, Halbert, Schwartz]

**Formula 10a: They Laughed When** [Caples]
`They laughed when [I did unexpected thing]... but when I [achieved result]...`

- "They laughed when I told them I'd close $500K in 90 days. Then I showed them the dashboard."
- "My business partner said this ad would never work. It generated $127,000 in its first week."
- "Everyone said I was crazy to raise my prices. That decision added $340K to my annual revenue."

**Formula 10b: The Origin Story**
`I was [relatable starting point] until [discovery/event changed everything]`

- "I was a burned-out freelancer working 14-hour days until I discovered the one system that changed everything"
- "Two years ago I was $43,000 in debt with zero clients. Then I found a 3-step process that turned it all around."
- "I used to spend $8,000/month on ads with nothing to show for it. Then I made one change."

**Formula 10c: The Case Study Narrative**
`[Named person] was [relatable struggle]. [Timeframe] later, [specific result]`

- "Jessica was a personal trainer making $4,200/month and working 60 hours a week. 90 days later, she hit $27,000/month working 25 hours."
- "David's agency had been stuck at $30K/month for two years. After implementing this system, he crossed $100K in month four."
- "A struggling SaaS founder. A counterintuitive pricing change. $2.1M ARR in 14 months."

**Formula 10d: The Confession**
`I [admitted failure/weakness] and it [unexpected positive outcome]`

- "I told my prospects exactly why my service costs twice as much. My close rate doubled."
- "I admitted to my list that I'd been wrong for 3 years. That email had the highest click rate I've ever seen."
- "I showed my clients the behind-the-scenes disaster of our process. They stayed longer than any previous cohort."

**When to use story:** Cold traffic, unaware audiences, email hooks, VSL openings, long-form sales pages. Story headlines work at every awareness level because they engage before they sell.

| Headline | Author | Principle |
|----------|--------|-----------|

## Headline Combination Rules

The strongest headlines combine 2-3 types. Tested combinations:

| Combination | Example |
|------------|---------|
| Curiosity + Benefit | "The counterintuitive pricing strategy that added $340K to my revenue" |
| Proof + Story | "How Sarah went from 2 clients to 14 in 6 weeks (her exact playbook)" |
| Question + Contrarian | "What if everything you know about lead generation is wrong?" |
| How-To + Proof | "How we generated 2,300 leads for $0.87 each (step-by-step breakdown)" |
| Urgency + Benefit | "The 48-hour window to lock in $997 pricing before it doubles" |
| Story + Curiosity | "I lost $40,000 on Facebook ads before discovering the one metric that changed everything" |
| List + Benefit | "7 sales call frameworks that close 40%+ of qualified prospects" |
| Comparison + Proof | "Our clients pay $997. Agencies charge $15K. Same result. Here's the data." |

---

## Headline Testing Priority

When you have 10+ headline options, test in this order:

1. **Strongest benefit claim** (backed by proof) vs. **Strongest curiosity hook**
2. Winner vs. **Best story headline**
3. Winner vs. **Best question headline**
4. Winner vs. **Format variation** (same angle, different structure)

Minimum test duration: 500 impressions per variant or 72 hours, whichever comes second.
