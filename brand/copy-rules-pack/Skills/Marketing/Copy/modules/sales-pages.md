# Sales Pages Module

## What This Module Adds

Deep long-form sales page copy methodology beyond the core SKILL.md protocol. SKILL.md covers the universal copywriting frameworks (awareness levels, market sophistication, headlines, proof pyramid, objection handling, CTAs). This module adds the format-specific methodology for written sales pages: section ordering psychology, above-fold copy requirements, scroll depth optimization through copy structure, section transitions and bridge copy, objection handling placement, proof stacking order, long-form vs. short-form decision criteria, testimonial integration rules, price presentation copy, and guarantee section architecture. Built for sales pages that convert cold and warm traffic into buyers for offers ranging from $97 to $50,000+.

## Section Ordering Psychology

Sales page sections are not arbitrary. Each section serves a psychological function and must appear in the correct sequence to match the reader's shifting mental state as they scroll.

### The 12-Section Sales Page Backbone

This is the full-length structure. For shorter pages, sections can be combined or reduced, but the sequence logic must remain intact.

**Section 1: Hero / Above the Fold**
- Psychological function: Pattern interrupt + promise. The reader decides in 3-8 seconds whether to scroll or leave.
- Required elements: Headline, subheadline, hero CTA, supporting visual (product mockup, results screenshot, or founder photo).
- See "Above-Fold Copy Requirements" below for detailed rules.

**Section 2: Problem Identification**
- Psychological function: "This person understands me." Build rapport by articulating the reader's situation better than they can.
- Copy approach: List 3-5 specific symptoms of the problem. Use the reader's language, not clinical terms.
- Format: Bullet list or short paragraphs, each starting with "You..." to create identification. "You've tried running ads and watched your budget evaporate with nothing to show for it."
- Length: 150-300 words.

**Section 3: Problem Amplification**
- Psychological function: Raise the emotional stakes. Show the consequences of NOT solving the problem.
- Copy approach: Future-pace the failure state. "In 12 months, if nothing changes, you'll still be trading hours for dollars, watching competitors with half your talent outpace you because they have a system and you don't."
- Connect the business problem to a personal/emotional cost (time with family, financial stress, self-doubt).
- Length: 200-400 words.

**Section 4: The Mechanism Reveal**
- Psychological function: Hope. After feeling the weight of the problem, the reader sees a new way forward.
- Copy approach: Name the mechanism. Explain how it works in 3 steps. Use an analogy. This is the intellectual anchor of the entire page.
- Format: Give the mechanism a visual treatment (diagram, numbered steps, icons). Copy and design work together here.
- Length: 300-500 words.

**Section 5: Proof Section 1 (Case Studies)**
- Psychological function: "It works for people like me." Proof immediately after the mechanism prevents the reader from dismissing it as theory.
- Copy approach: 2-3 case studies. Each follows: Name, starting situation, obstacle, what they did (mechanism), specific result, timeframe.
- Format: Card layout or alternating text/image blocks.
- Length: 300-600 words (100-200 per case study).

**Section 6: The Offer (What You Get)**
- Psychological function: Desire. Transform the mechanism from a concept into something tangible they can own.
- Copy approach: Value stack. Each component gets a name, a description of what it does (not what it is), and an explanation of what it replaces.
- See "Price Presentation Copy" below for value stack and pricing rules.
- Length: 400-800 words.

**Section 7: Proof Section 2 (Social Proof Wall)**
- Psychological function: Consensus. After seeing the offer, the reader thinks "but will it work for ME?" Social proof answers that with volume.
- Copy approach: 6-12 testimonials in a grid or carousel. Mix formats: text quotes, screenshots, video thumbnails with quotes.
- See "Testimonial Integration Rules" below.
- Length: Variable (depends on number of testimonials).

**Section 8: Objection Handling**
- Psychological function: Safety. Remove every reason not to buy.
- Copy approach: FAQ format or objection-then-reframe blocks. Address 5-8 objections.
- See "Objection Handling Placement Strategy" below for sequencing.
- Length: 400-800 words.

**Section 9: Guarantee**
- Psychological function: Risk reversal. Make saying YES feel safer than saying NO.
- Copy approach: State the guarantee clearly. Explain the terms. Flip the risk.
- See "Guarantee Section Copy" below.
- Length: 150-300 words.

**Section 10: Price Reveal + CTA**
- Psychological function: Decision point. After proof, objection handling, and guarantee, the reader is ready for the number.
- Copy approach: Anchor high, reveal the actual price, present payment options, strong CTA.
- See "Price Presentation Copy" below.
- Length: 200-400 words.

**Section 11: Final Proof + Urgency**
- Psychological function: Last push. For readers who scrolled past the price and are still undecided.
- Copy approach: One more powerful testimonial or case study (the strongest one, saved for last). Real urgency if it exists (cohort closing, limited spots, price increase date).
- Length: 200-400 words.

**Section 12: Final CTA + Closing Copy**
- Psychological function: Resolution. The reader either clicks or leaves. Make the decision feel clear.
- Copy approach: Recap the transformation in 2-3 sentences. Restate the guarantee in one sentence. Final CTA button.
- Close with a "two paths" frame: "You have two options right now. Keep doing [current approach] and getting [current results]. Or [take action] and [achieve outcome]."
- Length: 100-200 words.

## Above-Fold Copy Requirements

The above-fold section is the most important real estate on the page. On mobile, this is roughly the first 600-800 pixels. On desktop, 800-1000 pixels.

**Required elements (no exceptions):**

| Element | Rule | Common Mistakes |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| Headline | 10-15 words. Specific result or transformation. | Too vague ("Transform Your Business"), too long, or too clever. |
| Subheadline | 15-25 words. Expands the headline with specificity or a mechanism hint. | Repeating the headline in different words. The subheadline must ADD information. |
| Hero CTA | Button with command verb + specific outcome. "Get the Free Training" not "Learn More." | Vague CTA ("Submit"), multiple CTAs above fold, or no CTA at all. |
| Supporting visual | Product mockup, results screenshot, or founder photo. Not a stock image. | Generic stock photos that could appear on any website. |
| Pre-headline (optional) | 5-8 word qualifier. "For [audience] who want [outcome]." | Making it too broad or too clever. It should filter the reader IN. |

**Above-fold headline formulas:**

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| [Outcome] + [Timeframe] + [Without Objection] | "Get 30 Qualified Sales Calls in 60 Days Without Cold Outreach" |
| [Audience] + [Transformation] | "How Coaches Turn Expertise Into $50K Months" |
| [Specific Number] + [Mechanism Hint] | "The 3-Phase System That Generated $2.1M For [Niche] Businesses" |
| [Question that presupposes desire] | "Ready to Fill Your Calendar With Clients Who Already Want to Buy?" |

**Above-fold copy rules:**
- No paragraphs above the fold. Only headlines, subheadlines, bullet points, and CTAs.
- The headline must be readable in under 3 seconds. If it requires re-reading, simplify it.
- On mobile, the headline should appear within the first 400 pixels without scrolling.
- The hero CTA button must be visible without scrolling on both mobile and desktop.

## Scroll Depth Optimization Through Copy

Average scroll depth on sales pages is 50-60%. That means half your page is unseen by nearly half your visitors. Optimize copy to keep readers scrolling.

**Scroll depth benchmarks:**

| Page percentage | What should be here | Why |
|----------------|--------------------|----|
| 0-10% | Hero, problem identification | Highest attention. Must hook immediately. |
| 10-25% | Problem amplification, mechanism | Readers deciding whether to keep going. Copy must create momentum. |
| 25-50% | First proof section, offer (what you get) | Readers who reach here are interested. Give them substance. |
| 50-75% | Social proof, objections, guarantee | The readers still here are considering buying. Remove barriers. |
| 75-100% | Price, final proof, closing CTA | Buyers live here. Don't waste this space on fluff. |

**Copy techniques to increase scroll depth:**

1. **Bucket brigades between sections.** Place transition sentences at the bottom of each section that pull the reader into the next one. "But here's where it gets interesting..." / "That's the surface level. Here's what's really going on..." / "And I haven't even shown you the best part yet."

2. **Open loops early, close them later.** In the problem section: "In a moment, I'll show you the exact system that solves this. But first, you need to understand why everything else failed." This creates psychological tension that keeps them scrolling.

3. **Visual pattern interrupts every 3-4 sections.** Alternate between text-heavy sections and visually distinct sections (testimonial cards, comparison tables, value stack with icons). Monotonous text blocks cause drop-off.

4. **Subheadlines as standalone hooks.** Every section subheadline should function as a mini-hook. If a reader scans past the body copy, the subheadline should still pull them back in. "Why 94% of Agencies Fail at This (And What the Top 6% Do Instead)" is better than "Our Approach."

5. **Progressive commitment markers.** Add subtle progress indicators: "Step 1 of 3," section numbering, or "you're almost there" language near the bottom. These give the reader a sense of completion momentum.

## Section Transitions (Bridge Copy)

The space between sections is where readers drop off. Bridge copy connects the end of one section to the beginning of the next, maintaining the narrative thread.

**Bridge copy patterns:**

| Transition | Pattern | Example |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Problem to Mechanism | "Now that you see the real problem, here's what actually fixes it." | Shifts from negative emotion to hope. |
| Mechanism to Proof | "That's the theory. Here's what it looks like in practice." | Shifts from intellectual to evidential. |
| Proof to Offer | "Now imagine getting the same system they used." | Shifts from third-person to second-person. |
| Offer to Objections | "If you're wondering whether this will work for your situation..." | Anticipates the reader's inner objection. |
| Objections to Guarantee | "And just so there's zero risk on your end..." | Shifts from logic to safety. |
| Guarantee to Price | "So what does all of this cost?" | Addresses the elephant directly. |
| Price to Final CTA | "Here's what happens after you click the button below." | Reduces CTA friction by previewing the next step. |

**Bridge copy rules:**
- Keep bridges to 1-3 sentences. They are transitions, not sections.
- Bridges should address the reader's likely mental state at that moment. After the problem section, they're feeling pain. The bridge acknowledges that and promises relief.
- Never start a new section without a bridge. Abrupt topic shifts cause cognitive whiplash and increase drop-off.

## Objection Handling Placement Strategy

Objections should be handled in a specific order based on their psychological weight. Address lighter objections first, heavier ones last.

**Objection placement sequence:**

| Order | Objection Type | Where on Page | Why This Order |
|-------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | Relevance ("Is this for me?") | After the mechanism or first proof | This is the earliest objection. If they don't see themselves in it, they leave. |
| 2 | Credibility ("Why should I trust you?") | After proof section 1 | Proof answers this, but lingering doubt needs direct acknowledgment. |
| 3 | Complexity ("Is this hard?") | In the offer section (value stack) | Show how each component simplifies the process. |
| 4 | Time ("I don't have time for this") | In objection handling section | Reframe: "This saves time" or "You'll spend the time on something." |
| 5 | Past failures ("I've tried this before") | In objection handling section | Differentiate via mechanism: "This isn't [what they tried before] because [mechanism difference]." |
| 6 | Risk ("What if it doesn't work?") | Guarantee section | The guarantee exists specifically for this objection. |
| 7 | Price ("It's too expensive") | After price reveal | Always last. It is never truly about price. Handle the underlying objections first. |

**Objection handling format options:**

**Option A: FAQ format**
Best for pages with 6+ objections. Clean, scannable, allows the reader to find their specific concern.
```
Q: "What if I've tried [alternatives] before and they didn't work?"
A: "[Mechanism Name] works differently because [specific difference]. [Client Name] tried [alternative] for 6 months with no results. After implementing [Mechanism], they [specific result] in [timeframe]."
```

**Option B: Objection-reframe blocks**
Best for pages with 3-5 objections. More narrative, better for building emotional connection.
```
"You might be thinking: 'I don't have time to learn a new system.'"
"That's exactly why we built [Mechanism Name] to be implemented in [timeframe]. [Client Name] set up the entire system in a single weekend. By Monday, [result]."
```

## Proof Stacking Order

Proof is not a single section. It is layered throughout the page. Each type of proof serves a different purpose and should appear at a specific moment.

**Proof type deployment order:**

| Proof Type | Where to Place | Purpose |
|------------|---------------|---------|
| Anchor statistic | Hero section or subheadline | Establishes scale and credibility immediately. "Join 10,000+ [audience]" |
| Mechanism proof | After mechanism reveal | Shows the mechanism works (data, logic, demonstration). |
| Case study (relatable) | After mechanism | "Someone like me got results." The first case study must be maximally relatable. |
| Case study (impressive) | After offer/value stack | "The results can be huge." The impressive case study justifies the price. |
| Aggregate social proof | Social proof wall section | Volume of results. 8-12 testimonials create consensus. |
| Authority proof | Near the guarantee or final CTA | Media mentions, certifications, expert endorsements. Placed late to reassure, not to lead. |
| Recency proof | Near the final CTA | "Last week, [Client Name] hit [result]." Shows the system is working NOW, not just historically. |

**Proof stacking rules:**
- Place proof near every CTA button. The reader should see evidence within one scroll of every action point.
- Alternate between emotional proof (stories) and rational proof (numbers). Never stack three of the same type.
- Name, company, and specific numbers in every testimonial. "It changed my life" without specifics is worthless.
- If the client has fewer than 5 testimonials, use fewer but make each one longer and more detailed. Volume can be replaced with depth.

## Long-Form vs. Short-Form Decision Matrix

Not every offer needs a 5,000-word sales page. Use this matrix to determine the right length.

| Factor | Short-Form (500-1,500 words) | Long-Form (2,000-8,000+ words) |
|--------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Price point | Under $200 | Over $200 |
| Awareness level | 4-5 (Product/Most Aware) | 1-3 (Unaware to Solution Aware) |
| Traffic temperature | Warm/hot (email list, retargeting) | Cold (paid traffic, organic search) |
| Offer complexity | Simple (one deliverable, clear value) | Complex (multiple components, mechanism required) |
| Market sophistication | Stage 1-2 (direct claim works) | Stage 3-5 (mechanism, proof, identity needed) |
| Purchase decision | Impulse or low-consideration | Considered purchase, involves research |

**Short-form page structure (5-7 sections):**
Hero, Problem (brief), Offer, Proof (3-5 testimonials), Guarantee, CTA, FAQ (optional).

**Long-form page structure (10-12 sections):**
Full 12-section backbone described above.

**Hybrid approach:** Start with a short-form page. If conversion rate is below benchmark for the traffic quality, expand into long-form by adding mechanism, deeper proof, and objection handling sections.

## Testimonial Integration Rules

Testimonials are proof, but poorly placed or formatted testimonials are wallpaper. Follow these rules to make testimonials work as conversion tools.

**Testimonial formatting requirements:**

| Element | Required | Format |
|---------|----------|--------|
| Full name | Yes | "Sarah Mitchell" not "S.M." or "Sarah" |
| Company or title | Yes (B2B) / Optional (B2C) | "CEO, Acme Marketing" |
| Specific result | Yes | "$47K in 37 days" not "great results" |
| Timeframe | Yes | "In 6 weeks" not "recently" |
| Objection addressed | Preferred | Each testimonial should implicitly answer an objection |
| Photo | Preferred | Real photo, not stock. Adds 30%+ credibility to the testimonial. |

**Testimonial placement rules:**
- Never cluster all testimonials in one section. Distribute them across the page.
- Place the most relatable testimonial after the mechanism (to prove the mechanism works for regular people).
- Place the most impressive testimonial after the price reveal (to justify the investment).
- Place testimonials that address specific objections in the objection handling section.
- On mobile, limit the social proof wall to 4-6 visible testimonials with a "see more" toggle. Endless scrolling through testimonials creates fatigue.

**Testimonial copy editing rules:**
- You may edit testimonials for clarity, grammar, and length. You may NOT change the substance of what was said.
- If a testimonial is too long, pull the most impactful sentence as a callout quote and place the full version below it.
- Bold the specific result within the testimonial text so scanners can see the number without reading the full quote.

## Price Presentation Copy

How you present the price determines whether the reader sees it as an investment or a cost. The copy sequence before, during, and after the price reveal matters more than the number itself.

**Pre-price copy (build the anchor — never a fabricated component total):**
1. Recap the core transformation the complete system delivers (by outcome, not assigned dollar values).
2. Show the "if you hired someone else" comparison. "A single funnel build from an agency: $5,000-15,000."
3. Lead with the "cost of doing nothing" comparison — the strongest, most honest anchor. "Every month without this system costs you an estimated $X in lost revenue."

**Price reveal copy:**
1. Transition from the cost of inaction: "That's $X walking out the door every month you wait."
2. Reveal the actual price: "Your investment today is [price]."
3. If payment plan available: "Or [X] payments of [amount]." Place the payment plan directly below the full price. Do not hide it.
4. Context line: "That's [price per day] for [duration of access/results]."

**Post-price CTA copy:**
1. CTA button text: Action verb + outcome. "Get Instant Access" / "Start Your [Transformation]" / "Join [Program Name] Now."
2. Below the button: "Click above to get started. You'll be taken to a secure checkout page."
3. Trust badges: Payment logos, security seal, guarantee reminder (small text).
4. Sub-CTA reassurance: "[Guarantee term] guarantee. [Support contact]. Cancel anytime."

**Price presentation rules:**
- Never reveal the price before anchoring. The reader needs context for what the number means.
- Always show the payment plan if one exists. Some buyers need the lower monthly number to say yes.
- Use specific, non-round numbers when possible. $997 feels more deliberate than $1,000. $2,497 signals precision.
- If the offer is over $2,000, include a "most popular" payment plan option. Frame the full payment as the "best value" option.

## Guarantee Section Copy

The guarantee is not legal fine print. It is a conversion tool. Write it as persuasive copy, not a disclaimer.

**Guarantee copy structure:**
1. Guarantee name: Give it a name. "The 90-Day Results-or-Refund Guarantee" is stronger than "Our Guarantee."
2. Clear terms: State exactly what happens if they're not satisfied. "If you [implement the system / complete the program / follow the process] and don't [see specific result], email [support address] within [timeframe] and we'll refund every dollar."
3. Risk reversal statement: "You're not risking [price]. You're risking [continued cost of the problem]. The guarantee means the only risk is staying where you are."
4. Visual treatment: Bold headline, distinct background color, badge or seal icon. The guarantee should be visually unmissable.

**Guarantee framing patterns:**

| Guarantee Type | Copy Pattern | Best For |
|---------------|-------------|----------|
| Unconditional | "100% money-back guarantee. No questions asked. No hoops. No guilt." | Low-ticket offers, first-time buyers, trust-building |
| Conditional | "Follow the system for 90 days. If you don't [result], we'll refund you in full." | High-ticket offers, programs with clear steps |
| Better-than-money-back | "If it doesn't work, we'll refund you AND [additional compensation]." | Premium offers, high trust required |
| ROI guarantee | "If you don't [generate X revenue / save X hours / achieve X metric], you don't pay." | B2B, performance-based offers |

**Guarantee copy rules:**
- State the guarantee in the reader's language, not legal language. "Full refund within 90 days" not "Purchaser may request a refund subject to terms and conditions within ninety (90) calendar days of the original transaction date."
- Place the guarantee BEFORE the price reveal or immediately after. The reader should feel safe before seeing the number.
- Mention the guarantee again near every CTA below the price. A one-line reminder: "Protected by our [Guarantee Name]."

## Module-Specific Quality Standards

- [ ] Above-fold section contains: headline, subheadline, hero CTA, supporting visual (no exceptions)
- [ ] Headline is under 15 words and communicates a specific result or transformation
- [ ] Subheadline adds new information (does not repeat the headline in different words)
- [ ] Hero CTA uses a command verb + specific outcome (not "Learn More" or "Submit")
- [ ] Section ordering follows the psychological sequence (problem before mechanism, mechanism before proof, proof before offer, guarantee before or with price)
- [ ] Bridge copy connects every section transition (no abrupt topic jumps)
- [ ] Proof is distributed across the page, not clustered in one section
- [ ] Proof appears within one scroll of every CTA button
- [ ] Proof types alternate between emotional (stories) and rational (numbers)
- [ ] Every testimonial includes full name, specific result, and timeframe
- [ ] Objections are handled in the correct sequence (relevance before price)
- [ ] Price is anchored before being revealed
- [ ] Payment plan is visible (not hidden) if it exists
- [ ] Guarantee has a name, clear terms, and risk-reversal language
- [ ] Guarantee is mentioned near every CTA below the price reveal
- [ ] Page length matches the decision matrix (price, awareness, traffic temperature, complexity)
- [ ] Scroll depth optimization techniques applied: bucket brigades, open loops, visual interrupts, scannable subheadlines
- [ ] Mobile experience verified: headline visible without scrolling, CTA button reachable, testimonial section not endless
