# VSL Scripting Module

> Activates when `vsl-scripting` is listed in knowledge.md `modules_active`.

## What This Module Adds

Deep VSL architecture beyond the core SKILL.md protocol. SKILL.md covers the universal copywriting frameworks (awareness levels, market sophistication, headlines, proof pyramid, objection handling, CTAs). This module adds the format-specific methodology for video sales letters: pacing rules per section, emotional arc mapping, video-to-text timing conversions, act structures (3-act and 5-act), B-roll and visual cue integration, and viewer retention optimization. Built for VSLs that sell high-ticket offers ($2K-$50K+) through 15-60 minute video presentations.

## VSL Architecture: Section Sequence

Every VSL follows this backbone. Sections can be reordered slightly based on awareness level, but the emotional arc must remain intact.

### Section 1: Hook (0:00-0:45)

**Purpose:** Stop the scroll. Create a pattern interrupt strong enough that the viewer commits to the next 30 seconds.

**Pacing rules:**
- First sentence: under 8 words. Declarative or provocative. No warmup.
- First 10 seconds: one single idea. Not two. Not a compound sentence. One idea.
- 10-30 seconds: expand the hook with a specificity anchor (number, name, timeframe, unusual detail).
- 30-45 seconds: transition to the "who this is for" qualifier. Filter the audience IN, not out.

**Hook variants to produce (minimum 3):**
1. Result-first hook: Lead with the outcome. "$47K in 37 days using a system I built on a Sunday afternoon."
2. Contrarian hook: Challenge a belief. "Everything your marketing agency told you about funnels is designed to keep you paying them monthly."
3. Story hook: Drop into a moment. "I was sitting in a Starbucks parking lot, staring at my phone, watching a stranger buy my $8,000 program while I waited for my coffee."

**What NOT to do in the hook:**
- Do not introduce yourself. Nobody cares who you are yet.
- Do not say "in this video." The viewer knows it is a video.
- Do not ask a yes/no question as the first line. These get skipped.
- Do not use music stingers or logo animations. They signal "ad" and trigger skip behavior.

### Section 2: Credibility Bridge (0:45-2:00)

**Purpose:** Earn enough trust to keep the viewer through the problem section. Not a full credentials dump. Just enough to justify why they should listen.

**Pacing rules:**
- Keep under 90 seconds. Viewers at this stage are evaluating, not committed.
- Lead with results, not credentials. "I've helped 340+ business owners" beats "I have an MBA from..."
- Use one specific case study as proof, not a list of accomplishments.
- Transition into the problem with a pivot: "But before any of that worked, I had to solve one problem nobody talks about..."

**Structure:**
1. One-line identity statement (what you do, for whom)
2. Proof anchor (single most impressive metric)
3. Relatability statement (I was where you are)
4. Pivot to problem

### Section 3: Problem Amplification (2:00-6:00)

**Purpose:** Make the viewer feel the full weight of their problem. They should be nodding, thinking "this person gets it." The problem section is where you earn the right to present a solution.

**Pacing rules:**
- Slower cadence than the hook. Let statements breathe.
- Use 2-3 second pauses after heavy emotional statements. In text: paragraph break.
- Build from surface pain to root pain over 3-4 minutes.
- Each pain point gets its own beat: state it, show the consequence, show the emotional toll.

**Problem amplification sequence:**
1. State the obvious surface problem (what they already know)
2. Reveal the hidden cost (what they haven't calculated)
3. Show the ripple effects (how it bleeds into other areas of life/business)
4. Name the real enemy (the underlying cause, not the symptom)
5. Future-pace the failure state (where they end up if nothing changes)

**Emotional arc within this section:**
- Start: recognition ("that's me")
- Middle: frustration ("I've been doing it wrong")
- End: urgency ("I need to fix this now")

**B-roll cues:** Frustrated business owner at laptop. Empty calendar. Competitor's success shown on screen. Bills/invoices. Clock imagery for time wasting.

### Section 4: Failed Solutions (6:00-9:00)

**Purpose:** Eliminate alternatives before presenting yours. The viewer should feel that they've already tried (or considered) every other path, and those paths don't work.

**Pacing rules:**
- Faster rhythm than problem section. Dismissive energy.
- Each failed solution gets 30-60 seconds max. Don't dwell.
- Use the pattern: name it, explain why people try it, reveal why it fails, show the hidden cost.

**Failed solutions to address (pick 3-4 relevant to the offer):**
- DIY approach ("doing it yourself")
- Cheaper alternatives ("the $47 course")
- Expensive alternatives ("the $20K agency")
- Doing nothing ("hoping it fixes itself")
- Generic solutions ("one-size-fits-all")

**Transition to solution:** "So if [failed solution 1] doesn't work, and [failed solution 2] leaves you worse off, and [failed solution 3] costs more than it produces... what actually works?"

### Section 5: Mechanism Reveal (9:00-14:00)

**Purpose:** Introduce your unique mechanism. This is HOW your solution works, not WHAT it is. The mechanism is what makes your claim uncopyable.

**Pacing rules:**
- Slower, more deliberate than failed solutions. This is the intellectual center of the VSL.
- Give the mechanism a name. Always. A named thing feels real and proprietary.
- Explain the mechanism in 3 steps maximum. More than 3 steps feels complicated.
- Use analogies. Compare the mechanism to something the viewer already understands.

**Mechanism presentation structure:**
1. Name it: "We call this the [Mechanism Name]"
2. Origin story: Where did this come from? (Discovery, accident, research, client work)
3. How it works: 3-step simplified explanation
4. Why it works: The underlying principle (science, psychology, market truth)
5. Why nobody else does this: What makes it different from everything in the failed solutions section

**Visual cues:** Whiteboard or diagram showing the 3 steps. Before/after comparison. Simple animation or slide showing the mechanism in action.

### Section 6: Proof Stack (14:00-20:00)

**Purpose:** Back up the mechanism claim with layered evidence. Follow the Proof Pyramid from SKILL.md, but deploy it in a specific video-optimized sequence.

**Pacing rules:**
- Alternate between story-based proof and data-based proof. Never stack three of the same type.
- Each case study gets 60-90 seconds. Name, situation, result, timeframe.
- Place the strongest case study first and last (primacy and recency effects).
- After every 2 proof points, reconnect to the viewer: "And [Client Name] isn't special. They're just like you."

**Proof deployment sequence for VSL:**
1. Lead case study (strongest result, most relatable person)
2. Data point (aggregate number: "340+ clients," "average 47% increase")
3. Second case study (different demographic or starting point than the first)
4. Authority proof (media mention, certification, expert endorsement)
5. Third case study (addresses the biggest objection through their story)
6. Social proof number (waitlist size, community members, applications received)

**B-roll cues:** Screenshot of results. Testimonial video clips. Dashboard showing metrics. Client photos with their permission. Logos of media outlets.

### Section 7: Offer Presentation (20:00-28:00)

**Purpose:** Present what they get, structured to maximize perceived value before revealing price.

**Pacing rules:**
- Energetic but controlled. Not salesy, not breathless.
- Each component gets its own beat: name it, explain what it does, explain what it replaces (the painful alternative). No assigned dollar value.
- Pause after each item. Let the outcome land.
- Build the cost of NOT solving it before revealing the price (not a fabricated total value).

**Outcome stack presentation order:**
1. Core offer (the main thing they get)
2. Component 2 (the thing that makes the core offer work faster)
3. Component 3 (the thing that removes the biggest obstacle)
4. Component 4 (the thing that protects them from failure)
5. Bonus 1 (solves a related problem they haven't asked about yet)
6. Bonus 2 (adds convenience or speed)
7. Cost-of-inaction recap
8. Price reveal with anchor comparison

**First CTA placement:** Immediately after price reveal. "If you're ready, click below now." Then continue for those who need more.

### Section 8: Objection Handling (28:00-35:00)

**Purpose:** Address every reason the viewer is still watching but hasn't clicked. They're interested (they're still here) but something is holding them back.

**Pacing rules:**
- Conversational tone. Like you're sitting across a table from them.
- Each objection gets its own mini-section: acknowledge it, reframe it, prove the reframe, move on.
- Address price last. It's never really about price. Handle the underlying objections first.

**Objection sequence (order matters):**
1. "Does this work for my specific situation?" (Relevance)
2. "I've tried things like this before." (Skepticism)
3. "I don't have time." (Bandwidth)
4. "What if it doesn't work for me?" (Risk) -- Guarantee goes here
5. "It's too expensive." (Price) -- ROI reframe goes here

### Section 9: Guarantee (35:00-37:00)

**Purpose:** Remove the final barrier. The guarantee should make saying YES feel risk-free and saying NO feel risky.

**Pacing rules:**
- State the guarantee clearly. No hedging language.
- Explain the terms simply. What they get if it doesn't work.
- Flip the risk: "You're not risking [price]. You're risking [continued cost of the problem]."

### Section 10: Final Close (37:00-40:00+)

**Purpose:** Bring it home. Summarize the transformation, restate the offer, create urgency if real, and give the final CTA.

**Pacing rules:**
- Rising energy toward the end. Not shouting, but confident momentum.
- Recap the transformation in 2-3 sentences (where they are now vs. where they'll be).
- Restate the offer in one sentence.
- Final CTA: command verb + specific next step + what happens after they click.

**Close patterns:**
1. Future-pace close: "Imagine six months from now..."
2. Decision close: "You have two options right now..."
3. Urgency close: "This offer closes [specific date/condition]..."
4. Identity close: "The kind of person who [identity statement] doesn't wait..."

## Emotional Arc Mapping

Every VSL follows a predictable emotional trajectory. If the emotion flatlines in any section, viewers leave.

```
Hook:          CURIOSITY ████████░░ (8/10)
Credibility:   TRUST     ████░░░░░░ (4/10)
Problem:       PAIN      ██████████ (10/10) -- peak negative emotion
Failed Sols:   FRUSTRAT  ███████░░░ (7/10)
Mechanism:     HOPE      ██████░░░░ (6/10) -- turning point
Proof:         BELIEF    ████████░░ (8/10)
Offer:         DESIRE    █████████░ (9/10)
Objections:    SAFETY    ███████░░░ (7/10)
Guarantee:     CERTAINTY ████████░░ (8/10)
Close:         URGENCY   █████████░ (9/10) -- peak positive emotion
```

**Key transitions:**
- Hook to Problem: curiosity transforms into recognition
- Problem to Failed Solutions: pain transforms into frustration with alternatives
- Failed Solutions to Mechanism: frustration transforms into hope (this is the emotional turning point of the entire VSL)
- Proof to Offer: belief transforms into desire (the viewer shifts from "does this work?" to "I want this")

## Video-to-Text Timing Conversions

Use these ratios when converting VSL scripts to on-screen text or estimating video length from word count.

| Speaking pace | Words per minute | 1,000 words = | 5,000 words = |
|--------------|-----------------|---------------|---------------|
| Slow/deliberate (problem, guarantee) | 120 WPM | 8:20 | 41:40 |
| Conversational (proof, objections) | 150 WPM | 6:40 | 33:20 |
| Energetic (hook, offer, close) | 170 WPM | 5:53 | 29:25 |
| Average across full VSL | 145 WPM | 6:54 | 34:29 |

**Target lengths by offer price:**
- $97-$497 offer: 15-25 minute VSL (2,200-3,600 words)
- $500-$2,000 offer: 25-40 minute VSL (3,600-5,800 words)
- $2,000-$10,000 offer: 35-60 minute VSL (5,000-8,700 words)
- $10,000+ offer: 45-75 minute VSL or webinar format (6,500-10,900 words)

## 3-Act Structure

For shorter VSLs (15-30 minutes) or when simplicity is preferred.

**Act 1: Setup (25% of total time)**
- Hook + Credibility + Problem
- End of Act 1: The viewer fully understands and FEELS the problem.

**Act 2: Confrontation (50% of total time)**
- Failed Solutions + Mechanism + Proof
- End of Act 2: The viewer believes the solution works and wants it.

**Act 3: Resolution (25% of total time)**
- Offer + Objections + Guarantee + Close
- End of Act 3: The viewer clicks the button or decides it's not for them.

## 5-Act Structure

For longer VSLs (40-75 minutes), webinar-style presentations, or complex offers with multiple components.

**Act 1: Inciting Incident (10%)**
Hook + credibility. Establish the world and the problem in broad strokes.

**Act 2: Rising Action (25%)**
Deep problem amplification. Failed solutions. Build tension to maximum.

**Act 3: Turning Point (20%)**
Mechanism reveal. This is the midpoint reversal. Everything changes here. The viewer goes from "this is hopeless" to "wait, what?"

**Act 4: Falling Action (25%)**
Proof stack. Offer presentation. The evidence cascade that turns hope into conviction.

**Act 5: Resolution (20%)**
Objection handling. Guarantee. Close. The final push from conviction to action.

## B-Roll and Visual Cue Integration

For every section of the VSL script, include visual direction in brackets. This bridges the gap between copy and production.

**Visual cue format in scripts:**
```
[VISUAL: Description of what appears on screen]
[TEXT ON SCREEN: Exact text to display]
[B-ROLL: Description of supplementary footage]
[TRANSITION: Type of transition to next section]
```

**Section-specific visual guidance:**

| Section | Primary visual | Supporting visuals |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Hook | Speaker direct to camera, tight frame | Text overlay of key number/claim |
| Credibility | Speaker + results on screen | Logo bar, client photos, media logos |
| Problem | Speaker, wider frame, slower cuts | B-roll of frustrated person, empty metrics |
| Failed Solutions | Speaker with dismissive body language | X marks over failed approaches, comparison charts |
| Mechanism | Whiteboard/diagram/slide | Step-by-step animation, process flow |
| Proof | Testimonial clips or screenshots | Before/after dashboards, result numbers |
| Offer | Product mockups, component visuals | Value stack building on screen, price crossed out |
| Objections | Speaker, conversational framing | FAQ-style text cards |
| Guarantee | Bold text on screen | Calendar/timeline visual |
| Close | Speaker, direct to camera, tight | CTA button animation, countdown if applicable |

## Viewer Retention Optimization

Retention curves follow predictable patterns. Optimize for the known drop-off points.

**Known drop-off points and countermeasures:**

| Timestamp | Drop-off cause | Countermeasure |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| 0:03-0:10 | Weak hook, looks like an ad | Open with speech, not graphics. First words matter more than first visuals. |
| 1:30-2:30 | Credibility section too long | Keep credibility under 90 seconds. Get to the problem fast. |
| 5:00-7:00 | Problem section too abstract | Use specific stories and scenarios, not vague pain descriptions. |
| 12:00-15:00 | Mechanism too complex | Simplify to 3 steps. Use analogy. If you can't explain it simply, simplify the mechanism. |
| 20:00-25:00 | Proof fatigue | Vary proof types. Don't stack 4 case studies in a row. Alternate story/data/authority. |
| Post-price reveal | Price shock | Never reveal price without anchoring first. Always show the math. |

**Retention boosters:**
- Open a curiosity loop at minute 3: "In about 10 minutes, I'm going to show you the exact system. But first, you need to understand why everything else fails."
- Use pattern interrupts every 3-5 minutes: change camera angle, show a slide, display text, shift vocal tone.
- Place a micro-CTA at the midpoint for eager buyers: "If you've already seen enough, the link is below. For everyone else, let me show you the proof."
- Foreshadow the offer at minute 8-10: "I put all of this into a system you can deploy in your business. I'll show you exactly what's included in a few minutes."

## Howitzer VSL Variant

When loading Context/Marketing/Funnels/howitzer-vsl, apply these modifications:

**Frontloaded CTA pattern:** Place the first CTA BEFORE the full offer presentation. Tease the result, show 1-2 proof points, and give the CTA. Then continue with the full VSL for those who didn't click. This captures high-intent viewers early.

**Sequence:**
1. Hook (standard)
2. Quick proof (1 case study, 30 seconds)
3. First CTA: "If you want [result], click below now. If you want to see exactly how it works first, keep watching."
4. Full VSL continues from Credibility through Close

## Webinar VSL Variant

When the deliverable is a webinar script, extend the VSL structure:

**Key differences from standard VSL:**
- Add a "Content" section between Mechanism and Proof (teaching 2-3 actionable tactics)
- Extend total length to 45-90 minutes
- The content section serves as proof-by-demonstration (you're proving competence by teaching)
- Add audience interaction points (poll, question, chat prompt) every 10-15 minutes
- Close with a live Q&A section or anticipated FAQ

## Module-Specific Quality Standards

- [ ] Hook section is under 45 seconds when read aloud at energetic pace
- [ ] Credibility section is under 90 seconds and leads with results, not credentials
- [ ] Problem section uses specific scenarios, not abstract pain descriptions
- [ ] Failed solutions section addresses 3-4 alternatives the audience has actually considered
- [ ] Mechanism has a name and is explained in 3 steps or fewer
- [ ] Proof stack alternates between story-based and data-based proof (never 3+ of same type)
- [ ] First CTA appears immediately after price reveal
- [ ] Objections are addressed in the correct sequence (relevance before price)
- [ ] Guarantee language is specific, not hedged
- [ ] Visual cues are included for every section in brackets
- [ ] Emotional arc follows the mapped trajectory (peak negative at Problem, turning point at Mechanism, peak positive at Close)
- [ ] Total word count matches target length for the offer price point
- [ ] Minimum 3 hook variants produced
- [ ] Curiosity loops are opened and closed (no orphaned loops)
- [ ] Pattern interrupts are placed every 3-5 minutes of script time
