# Ad Copy Module

> Activates when `ad-copy` is listed in knowledge.md `modules_active`.

## What This Module Adds

Deep platform-specific ad copy methodology beyond the core SKILL.md protocol. SKILL.md covers the universal copywriting frameworks (awareness levels, market sophistication, headlines, proof pyramid, objection handling, CTAs). This module adds the format-specific methodology for paid advertising copy: platform character limits and best practices for Meta, Google, and TikTok, hook-to-CTA flow architecture, A/B testing copy variant strategy, native vs. direct copy style decisions, compliance and disapproval avoidance, and copy segmentation by funnel stage. Built for ad campaigns that need to produce 10-50+ copy variants per creative round across multiple platforms.

## Platform-Specific Rules: Meta (Facebook/Instagram)

Meta ads have three copy fields, each with different character limits and behavioral rules. Writing for Meta means understanding that most users see the primary text truncated, rarely read the description, and make decisions based on the headline and the first 2-3 lines of primary text.

### Meta Ad Copy Fields

**Primary Text (above the creative):**
- Character limit: 125 characters before "See More" truncation on mobile. Full text can be up to 1,000+ characters, but assume most people see only the first 125.
- Rule: The first sentence must work as a standalone ad. If they never click "See More," the first 125 characters should still communicate the hook and hint at the value.
- Structure for short-form primary text (125 characters):
  1. Hook statement (pattern interrupt or result)
  2. One-line bridge to the CTA
  3. CTA with clear next step
- Structure for long-form primary text (300-800 characters):
  1. Hook (first 125 characters, functions as standalone)
  2. Problem agitation or story (2-3 short paragraphs)
  3. Mechanism tease or proof point
  4. CTA with specificity

**Headline (below the creative, bold):**
- Character limit: 40 characters before truncation. Write for 40, not 27 (the old standard).
- Rule: The headline is NOT a traditional headline. On Meta, it functions as a CTA reinforcement or value summary. Most users see it after seeing the creative and primary text.
- Winning patterns:
  - Result statement: "From $0 to $47K in 37 Days"
  - Value offer: "Free Training: [Specific Outcome]"
  - CTA echo: "Watch the Free Masterclass"
  - Curiosity pull: "The 3-Step System Inside"

**Description (below the headline, smaller text):**
- Character limit: 30 characters before truncation.
- Rule: Most placements don't show the description at all. Write it, but don't put critical information here.
- Best use: Reinforce urgency, add a secondary proof point, or repeat the CTA.
- Examples: "Limited spots available" / "Join 10,000+ members" / "Free for a limited time"

### Meta Copy Variants to Produce

For every Meta ad campaign, produce minimum 3 primary text variants per creative:

**Variant 1: Direct response (short-form)**
Lead with the result. State the mechanism. Give the CTA. Under 125 characters if possible. This is for warm audiences and retargeting.

**Variant 2: Story-based (long-form)**
Open with a personal story or client story. Build to the revelation. Present the offer as the solution. 400-800 characters. This is for cold audiences at awareness levels 1-2.

**Variant 3: Social proof lead (medium-form)**
Open with a specific client result or aggregate number. Expand into what makes this possible. CTA. 200-400 characters. This is for solution-aware audiences (level 3).

**Variant 4 (optional): Contrarian/pattern interrupt**
Challenge a common belief in the market. "Stop running more ads. Your funnel is the problem." Use when the market is at sophistication stage 4-5.

## Platform-Specific Rules: Google RSA (Responsive Search Ads)

Google RSAs require a fundamentally different copy approach than Meta. Users are searching with intent. They typed a query. The copy must match that intent precisely, not create desire from scratch.

### Google RSA Copy Fields

**Headlines (up to 15, Google shows 3):**
- Character limit: 30 characters each.
- Rule: Each headline must function independently. Google assembles combinations. You cannot control which 3 headlines appear together.
- Pinning strategy: Pin your strongest headline to Position 1. Pin your CTA headline to Position 3. Let Google rotate the rest.

**Headline categories to fill (minimum 8 headlines, ideally 12-15):**

| Category | Example | Purpose |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Keyword match | "[Exact Keyword] Services" | Relevance signal to Google and the searcher |
| Result/benefit | "Get 3X More Leads in 30 Days" | Self-interest appeal |
| Unique mechanism | "The [Mechanism Name] Method" | Differentiation |
| Social proof | "Trusted by 10,000+ Businesses" | Authority and trust |
| CTA | "Get Your Free Strategy Call" | Action directive |
| Location (if local) | "[City Name] [Service]" | Geographic relevance |
| Price/offer | "Starting at $997" or "Free Audit" | Qualifier and value signal |
| Urgency (if real) | "Limited Spots This Month" | Scarcity |

**Descriptions (up to 4, Google shows 2):**
- Character limit: 90 characters each.
- Rule: Descriptions carry the persuasion load. Headlines catch attention and establish relevance. Descriptions sell.
- Structure per description:
  - Description 1: Primary benefit + proof element. "Our clients average 47% more revenue in 90 days. See how it works."
  - Description 2: Mechanism + CTA. "The [Mechanism Name] system automates your [process]. Book a free strategy call today."
  - Description 3: Objection handler. "No long-term contracts. Cancel anytime. Results in 30 days or your money back."
  - Description 4: Social proof + urgency. "Join 2,400+ [industry] owners. Only 5 spots left this quarter."

**Keyword insertion rules:**
- Use {KeyWord:Default Text} syntax for dynamic headline insertion.
- Always set a sensible default that fits within the 30-character limit.
- Do not use keyword insertion in every headline. Mix dynamic and static headlines.
- Never use keyword insertion in descriptions. It creates awkward, unnatural copy.

## Platform-Specific Rules: TikTok

TikTok ad copy is the shortest and most informal of all platforms. The creative (video) does 90% of the selling. The copy supports the video, it does not replace it.

### TikTok Ad Copy Fields

**Caption (appears below the video):**
- Character limit: 150 characters including hashtags. Truncates after approximately 80 characters on most devices.
- Rule: The caption is a hook amplifier, not a sales pitch. It either echoes the video hook or adds context that makes the video more compelling.
- Tone: Casual, conversational, lowercase acceptable, slang acceptable if audience-appropriate. TikTok copy that sounds like a Facebook ad will be scrolled past instantly.

**Caption patterns that work:**

| Pattern | Example | When to Use |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Echo the hook | "wait for the part about the $47K" | When the video hook is strong |
| Add context | "I tried this for 30 days. here's what happened." | When the video is a journey or experiment |
| Create curiosity | "nobody talks about step 3" | When the video reveals information |
| Social proof | "this is how 300+ people got [result]" | When the video shows results or testimonials |
| Direct CTA | "link in bio to get the free [thing]" | When the audience is warm and ready |

**TikTok-specific rules:**
- No hashtags that scream "ad." Avoid #sponsored or #ad unless required by platform disclosure rules.
- 2-3 relevant hashtags maximum. More than 3 looks spammy.
- Never write TikTok captions in all caps. It breaks the native feel.
- Match the energy and language of the video. If the video is casual, the caption must be casual.

### Native vs. Polished: The TikTok Decision

Every TikTok ad falls on a spectrum from "native" (looks like organic content) to "polished" (looks like a produced advertisement).

**Native style:**
- Looks like a regular TikTok a user would post.
- Shot on phone, natural lighting, casual wardrobe.
- Caption matches organic TikTok language.
- Works best for cold audiences, awareness levels 1-2, and products under $500.

**Polished style:**
- Clearly a produced piece of content with intentional lighting, framing, and editing.
- Caption can be slightly more structured.
- Works best for retargeting, awareness levels 3-5, and products over $500.

**Rule:** When in doubt, go native. TikTok's algorithm rewards content that feels organic. Polished ads require strong creative to overcome the "ad penalty" in user scroll behavior.

## Hook-to-CTA Flow Patterns

Every ad, regardless of platform, follows a hook-to-CTA flow. The flow structure changes based on awareness level and ad length.

### Short-Form Ad Flow (under 125 characters / under 15 seconds video)

```
HOOK (1 line / 3 seconds)
  -> CLAIM or RESULT (1 line / 3 seconds)
    -> CTA (1 line / 3 seconds)
```

Best for: Retargeting, most-aware audiences, simple offers, Meta feed ads.

### Medium-Form Ad Flow (125-500 characters / 15-30 seconds video)

```
HOOK (1 line / 3 seconds)
  -> PROBLEM AGITATION (1-2 lines / 5 seconds)
    -> MECHANISM TEASE (1 line / 5 seconds)
      -> PROOF POINT (1 line / 5 seconds)
        -> CTA (1 line / 3 seconds)
```

Best for: Warm audiences, product-aware to solution-aware, lead gen offers.

### Long-Form Ad Flow (500-1000 characters / 30-60 seconds video)

```
HOOK (1 line / 3 seconds)
  -> STORY or IDENTIFICATION (2-3 lines / 10 seconds)
    -> PROBLEM + CONSEQUENCE (2-3 lines / 10 seconds)
      -> MECHANISM REVEAL (1-2 lines / 10 seconds)
        -> PROOF STACK (2-3 lines / 10 seconds)
          -> CTA with URGENCY (1-2 lines / 5 seconds)
```

Best for: Cold audiences, unaware to problem-aware, high-ticket offers, story-driven brands.

### The 3-Hook Test

For every ad, produce minimum 3 hook variants:

**Hook Type 1: Result-first**
Lead with the outcome. "$47K in 37 days using a system I built on a Sunday."

**Hook Type 2: Pattern interrupt**
Challenge an assumption. "Your marketing agency doesn't want you to know this."

**Hook Type 3: Curiosity gap**
Open a loop. "I spent $200K on ads before I discovered this one setting."

**Hook testing rules:**
- Test hooks before testing body copy. The hook determines whether anyone reads the rest.
- Run each hook variant for minimum 48 hours before judging performance.
- The winning hook often surprises you. Never assume. Always test.

## A/B Testing Copy Variants

### What to Test (In Priority Order)

1. **Hook/opening line.** This determines click-through rate more than any other element.
2. **CTA copy.** "Get Your Free Training" vs. "Watch the Masterclass" vs. "See How It Works."
3. **Primary text length.** Short-form vs. long-form for the same creative.
4. **Proof type.** Specific case study vs. aggregate number vs. authority mention.
5. **Emotional angle.** Pain-forward vs. aspiration-forward vs. fear-of-missing-out.

### Testing Rules

- Test one copy variable at a time against the same creative. Changing the copy AND the image means you learn nothing about the copy.
- Minimum spend per variant: $50-100 before evaluating. Below that threshold, the data is noise.
- Primary metric for copy tests: Click-through rate (CTR) for top-of-funnel. Cost per acquisition (CPA) for bottom-of-funnel.
- Document every test result. What variant won, by what margin, against what audience. This data compounds over time.

### Variant Naming Convention

```
[Client]_[Platform]_[Funnel Stage]_[Hook Type]_[Length]_V[Number]
Example: AcmeSaaS_Meta_TOF_ResultHook_LongForm_V3
```

Use consistent naming across all tests. When reviewing performance data, you need to identify exactly which copy version ran.

## Native-Feeling vs. Direct Copy Styles

### When to Use Native Copy

Native copy sounds like a person sharing something interesting, not a brand selling something. It blends into the feed.

**Signals to use native:**
- Cold audience (no prior brand awareness)
- Platform where organic content dominates (TikTok, Instagram Reels, Twitter/X)
- Low-ticket or free offer (lead magnet, free training)
- Market sophistication stage 4-5 (audience is ad-fatigued)

**Native copy characteristics:**
- Lowercase acceptable (especially TikTok)
- Conversational tone, as if texting a friend
- Story-driven, not feature-driven
- Proof embedded in narrative, not listed
- CTA feels like a recommendation, not a command

**Example:** "spent 6 months testing every lead gen strategy. this one weird approach brought in 47 qualified leads in a week. dropping the full breakdown for free (link in bio)"

### When to Use Direct Copy

Direct copy is clearly an advertisement. It leads with the offer, states the value, and commands action.

**Signals to use direct:**
- Warm or hot audience (retargeting, email list, past buyers)
- Platform where ads are expected (Google Search, Meta retargeting)
- High-ticket offer where trust is already established
- Market sophistication stage 1-3 (audience is receptive to direct claims)

**Direct copy characteristics:**
- Professional tone, proper capitalization
- Benefit-led, feature-supported
- Proof displayed prominently (numbers, names, testimonials)
- CTA is explicit and commanding

**Example:** "Get 3X More Qualified Leads in 30 Days. The [Mechanism Name] system automates your entire outbound process. Trusted by 2,400+ B2B companies. Book Your Free Strategy Call Today."

## Compliance and Disapproval Avoidance

Ad platforms reject copy that violates their policies. Disapprovals waste time and can trigger account-level penalties. Write compliant copy from the start.

### Meta Compliance Rules

**Income/earnings claims:**
- Do not guarantee specific income results. "Make $10K/month" will be disapproved.
- Reframe as: "Our clients have generated results like [specific case study with disclaimer]."
- If showing results, include "Results not typical" language in the ad or landing page.

**Personal attributes:**
- Do not call out personal characteristics directly. "Are you overweight?" or "Struggling with debt?" will be disapproved.
- Reframe as general statements: "For business owners who want to scale revenue" instead of "Are you a struggling business owner?"

**Before/after claims:**
- Do not use before/after imagery that implies unrealistic transformation.
- Use before/after with data (revenue, metrics, business results) rather than physical appearance.

**Prohibited content:**
- No misleading claims about product capabilities.
- No sensationalized language about health outcomes.
- No use of Facebook/Meta branding in ad copy.

### Google Compliance Rules

**Trademark usage:**
- Do not use competitor brand names in ad copy unless you are an authorized reseller.
- You CAN bid on competitor keywords, but the ad copy must reference your own brand.

**Superlative claims:**
- "Best" or "#1" requires third-party verification. If you don't have it, don't claim it.
- Reframe as: "Rated 4.9/5 by 500+ clients" instead of "Best [service] in [city]."

**Punctuation and formatting:**
- No excessive capitalization (ENTIRE HEADLINES IN CAPS will be disapproved).
- No excessive punctuation (multiple exclamation marks).
- No gimmicky characters or symbols.

### TikTok Compliance Rules

**Disclosure requirements:**
- Paid partnerships must be disclosed using TikTok's branded content toggle.
- Supplement and health claims have strict limitations.

**Content restrictions:**
- No copy that could be interpreted as targeting minors.
- No exaggerated health or financial claims.
- Copy must match the content of the landing page (no bait-and-switch).

## Copy by Funnel Stage

The same offer requires different copy depending on where the reader is in the funnel. Never use bottom-of-funnel copy for top-of-funnel traffic.

### Top of Funnel (TOF): Cold Traffic

**Awareness level:** 1-2 (Unaware to Problem Aware)
**Goal:** Stop the scroll, create awareness, generate a click.
**Copy approach:**
- Lead with the problem or a pattern interrupt. Not the product.
- Use story-based or native copy styles.
- CTA is low-commitment: "Watch the free training" / "Get the free guide" / "See how it works."
- No price in the ad. No product name in the hook. No selling.

### Middle of Funnel (MOF): Engaged Traffic

**Awareness level:** 3 (Solution Aware)
**Goal:** Build belief in your specific solution.
**Copy approach:**
- Lead with proof or mechanism. They know the problem. Show them why your approach works.
- Use case studies, specific numbers, and named results.
- CTA is medium-commitment: "Book a call" / "Apply now" / "Join the waitlist."
- Product name can appear. Price should not (unless qualifying).

### Bottom of Funnel (BOF): Hot Traffic

**Awareness level:** 4-5 (Product Aware to Most Aware)
**Goal:** Overcome final objections and close.
**Copy approach:**
- Lead with the offer, the guarantee, or the deadline.
- Use direct copy style. They know who you are. Sell.
- CTA is high-commitment: "Enroll now" / "Buy today" / "Claim your spot."
- Include price, guarantee, and urgency (if real).
- Testimonials that address the specific objection holding them back.
