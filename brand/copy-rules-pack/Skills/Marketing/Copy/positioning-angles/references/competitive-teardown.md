# Competitive Offer Teardown

Detailed framework for structured competitive analysis that informs positioning. Load when running a teardown to feed angle development.

---

## When to Use This Framework

- Before creating a new offer (to find white space)
- When an existing offer isn't differentiating (to find what's missing)
- When a prospect says "How are you different from [competitor]?" (to build a clear answer)
- During Phase II angle development (to inform positioning decisions)
- When entering a crowded market and need to stand out immediately

---

## Step 1: Identify the Competitive Set

List 3-5 direct competitors. These are businesses the prospect would consider instead of you. Not aspirational comparisons. Not tangentially related businesses. The actual alternatives a prospect evaluates.

**How to find them:**
- Ask prospects directly: "Who else are you considering?"
- Search the exact phrase a prospect would use to find this service
- Check who is running ads in the same space (Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency)
- Look at who is active in the same communities, podcasts, and content spaces
- Ask existing clients: "Who did you almost go with instead of us?"

**Important:** Include the "do nothing" and "DIY" alternatives. These are competitors too. The prospect choosing to handle it themselves or ignore the problem entirely is often the biggest competitor.

---

## Step 2: Gather Intelligence on Each Competitor

For each competitor, document the following. Use their website, landing pages, sales pages, ads, case studies, social media, and any public-facing materials.

| Data Point | What to Look For |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Core Promise** | What result do they promise? How specific is it? Is there a number attached? |
| **Deliverables** | What do they actually deliver? What's in the package? |
| **Pricing** | What do they charge? What's the pricing model (retainer, one-time, performance)? |
| **Guarantee** | Do they offer one? What kind? What are the conditions? |
| **Positioning** | How do they describe themselves? What angle are they using? What identity do they claim? |
| **Packaging** | How is the offer structured? Tiers? Single offer? Custom? |
| **Bonuses** | What extras do they include? Templates, community access, calls? |
| **Social Proof** | What case studies and testimonials do they show? How specific are the results? |
| **Mechanism** | What's their unique method or process? Do they name it? |
| **Audience** | Who are they targeting? How specific is their avatar? |

**Sources for competitive intelligence:**
- Their landing pages and sales pages (screenshot everything)
- Meta Ad Library (search their brand name and their niche keywords)
- Their YouTube, LinkedIn, and Twitter content
- Their podcast appearances
- Review sites and testimonials pages
- Community posts from their clients (wins channels, forums)
- Their lead magnets and email sequences (sign up for everything)

## Step 4: Find the Exploitable Gaps

With the matrix complete, look for these specific gap types:

### Gap Type 1: The Promise Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors promise vague results ("more leads," "grow your business") while leaving room for a specific, quantifiable claim.
**How to exploit:** Attach a number. If they say "more leads," you say "30 qualified leads per month." Specificity always beats vagueness.

### Gap Type 2: The Risk Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors have no guarantee, or their guarantee is weak and heavily conditional.
**How to exploit:** Offer a stronger guarantee with clear terms. Performance-basis pricing or a money-back guarantee with reasonable stipulations immediately differentiates.

### Gap Type 3: The Delivery Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors promise results but their deliverables don't logically connect to those results. They promise "clients" but deliver "leads." They promise "growth" but deliver "content."
**How to exploit:** Close the gap between promise and deliverable. Own the full chain. If they stop at leads, you go all the way to booked calls. Apply the Transfer of Logistical Intensity framework.

### Gap Type 4: The Proof Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors make big claims but show generic testimonials, no case studies, or results from a different business model than what they're currently selling.
**How to exploit:** Build and display specific, quantified case studies. Even one real case study with dollar figures beats a wall of "Great experience!" testimonials.

### Gap Type 5: The Avatar Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors target a broad audience ("business owners," "agencies," "coaches"). Nobody is speaking directly to the specific sub-segment your client serves.
**How to exploit:** Get hyper-specific. "For SaaS founders doing $1-5M ARR who need to reduce churn" is infinitely more compelling to that specific person than "For SaaS companies."

### Gap Type 6: The Mechanism Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors promise results but don't explain HOW. No named process, no proprietary method, no unique approach.
**How to exploit:** Name your mechanism. Even if your process isn't truly unique, naming it and explaining why it works differently makes it feel proprietary. "The [Named] Method" immediately stands apart from competitors who just say "our team of experts."

### Gap Type 7: The Experience Gap
**What to look for:** Competitors deliver results but the client experience is painful. Slow communication, no visibility into progress, feeling like they're in the dark.
**How to exploit:** Make the experience a selling point. "Weekly video updates, real-time dashboard, dedicated Slack channel" solves a pain that most prospects have experienced with previous service providers.

---

## Step 5: Position Your Offer as the Obvious Choice

With the gaps identified, rebuild your positioning to exploit every relevant opening. The goal is to make the comparison feel unfair.

**Positioning template after teardown:**

"Unlike [what competitors typically do], we [your key differentiator]. This means [specific benefit to the prospect]. That's why we're able to [your guarantee or specific promise]."

**How to use the teardown in sales conversations:**

When a prospect asks "How are you different?" do NOT trash competitors by name. Instead:
1. Acknowledge the landscape: "There are a lot of people doing [service] right now."
2. Name the common problem: "What most of them do is [common weak pattern you identified]."
3. Contrast with your approach: "What we do differently is [your gap-exploiting differentiator]."
4. Back it with proof: "And here's what that looks like in practice: [specific case study or result]."

**How to use the teardown in marketing copy:**

The teardown gives you the raw material for:
- **"Not like the rest" positioning:** Headlines that call out the common failure pattern without naming names
- **Comparison landing pages:** Side-by-side comparisons showing your strengths against generic "typical provider" columns
- **Objection-handling content:** Blog posts or videos that address the exact frustrations prospects have with competitors
- **Ad angles:** Each gap you identified becomes a potential ad angle (use the angle generators from this skill to develop them)


---

## Teardown Quality Checks

- [ ] Analyzed 3-5 actual direct competitors (not aspirational comparisons)
- [ ] Included "do nothing" and "DIY" as alternatives
- [ ] Every data point in the matrix is based on observable evidence, not assumptions
- [ ] Identified at least 2-3 distinct exploitable gaps
- [ ] Positioning exploits real weaknesses, not manufactured ones
- [ ] No competitor is named or attacked directly in prospect-facing materials
- [ ] Teardown findings feed directly into angle development
- [ ] Acknowledged competitor strengths to avoid positioning against their strong points
