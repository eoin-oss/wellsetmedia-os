# Haynes Demographic Spectrum — Selling to the General Population

**Source:** Jeremy Haynes, Inner Circle weekly call, 2026-02-14. Full transcript: `_sources/haynes-demographic-spectrum-2026-02-14-transcript.md`. Distilled 2026-09-17.

**What this is:** the doctrine for selling to "financially qualified regular people". Sister doc to `haynes-financial-modeling.md` (the math side). This is the messaging side: how to aim copy at one pocket of the general population and repel the others.

---

## 1. The spectrum

The general population splits into 4 pockets. The amount of money someone has determines everything else about how they buy.

| | Super poor | Regular poor | Middle class | Upper middle class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Income | Negative, in debt, overdrafted | ~$2k-$5k/mo, under $60k/yr | ~$4k-$12k/mo, usually 2 household incomes | Better jobs, dual income, "their s— is together" |
| Savings | None | None, rations every paycheck | Very small | Real savings, 401k/IRA, home equity, HELOC |
| Credit | None | Not creditworthy | Some (car-loan grade, torched rates) | Decent to good |
| Household | Large support network, roommates, family | Same | Decisions made as a couple | Joint accounts, decisions as a couple |
| Time horizon | Today, tomorrow | 2 weeks to 1 month | 2 weeks to 1 month | 1 month to 1 year |
| Certainty needed | Absolute (and still can't buy) | Absolute | Absolute or near-absolute | Somewhat certain is enough |
| Safety needed | Extreme | Extreme | High (guarantee + proof of guarantee) | Less. Too much safety reads as a red flag pointed below them |
| Behavior in a funnel | Chargeback-heavy, refund-oriented | Whiny, loud, refund/chargeback | Convertible, needs hand-holding | The target for high-ticket call funnels |

**Core law:** the more money someone has, the longer they can think and the less certainty they need. The less money, the shorter the horizon and the more certainty they demand. Their risk profile IS their bank balance.

## 2. The 6 messaging levers (each one sifts the spectrum)

1. **Time.** Talk in months ("this takes 2 to 6 months to see the full potential") and everyone short-horizon drops off on their own. Talk in days and you invite the poor. Time-in-copy is the cheapest filter that exists.
2. **Certainty.** Talk in absolutes, transfer total conviction → middle class converts. Talk in high-probability-but-not-guaranteed ("high hit rate, not 100%, you do the work") → filters up-spectrum. Full certainty removal (openly "no assurances at all") targets the actual rich. DFY offers still require absolute certainty at any level; the dial applies to DWY / info / coaching.
3. **Safety (guarantees).** Guarantees are safety mechanisms, not persuasion. The middle needs the guarantee PLUS live proof it's honored (the Charles bookkeeper stack: money-back + buy-your-pipeline-for-double + $200k screenshot refreshed live). The upper middle needs a guarantee to exist but softened; the extreme stack actively signals "this is for people poorer than you". Removing safety entirely rugs sales to regular buyers (the Shane case: cut an 18% refund rate by killing the guarantee, killed all sales with it).
4. **Dollar types.** Cash-from-savings buyer = calmest, but feels vulnerable after purchase; that vulnerability is the refund/chargeback liability. Financed = must see it be real inside 30 days. Credit card = highest pressure of all. Name the dollar type in the close ("some of you will use cash you've set aside...") and tailor certainty to it. The percent-of-savings question ("what % of your savings would this take up, drop it in the chat") is a top-tier webinar/call close tactic.
5. **Self-image.** Communicate to people the way they view themselves, never the way you view them. Regular people gaslight themselves into loving their circumstances; call them stressed or failing and they go unresponsive. For the upper end: congratulate first ("you finally got money set aside, you're finally positioned to invest"), then aim the desire one level up. Aspirations run ONE milestone above current, not five.
6. **Speed to external proof.** Down-spectrum buyers judge themselves externally and quit when nothing external happens fast. Structure the offer and the copy around a fast, externally-visible first win ("first sale in week one"), then let the longer arc carry.

## 3. Talk one price level above

Whatever level you speak to, you pull buyers down to roughly 25% of that number.

- Haynes says "$100k/mo minimum" → real flow arrives down to ~$25k/mo.
- Need $1,000 buyers → talk to $4,000 people. Need $2k → talk $5k-$8k.
- If you name the true number required, you attract people below it. Name double the true number and the call reveals it's less, that's a conversion assist.
- Americans are conditioned that credit = available money, so they self-qualify above their cash position, especially if the ad implies financing exists.

## 4. Price transparency

With the general population: never transparent on price in ads, VSLs or webinars UNLESS going direct-to-checkout (then always transparent). Call funnels stay price-silent; these buyers need hand-holding to justify the decision on the phone.

## 5. Pixel conditioning: find the datapoint that correlates with buyers

Application answers are the starting point, not the truth. The Haynes client case: high-ticket qualification ratio stopped correlating with revenue; a credit-check tool (SimpleCheck/LeadFi) revealed 650+ credit score was the real buyer signal. They switched CAPI feedback to send back 650+ scores regardless of application answers, and lead quality plus revenue recovered. Rule: keep hunting for the datapoint that actually correlates with closed cash (credit band, financing approval, sales-team manual intent marking via CAPI) and optimize toward that, not toward form answers.

## 6. Urgency for the comfortable

Comfortable/upper prospects don't respond to manufactured scarcity. They move on narrative shatter: real third-party data, opportunity windows, an article circulating in their world (the "Something Big Is Happening" AI piece, Matt Schumer, Feb 10). Method: articulate their current story back to them, then show the same story in a new context that makes sitting still feel like the risk. Real data only; invented urgency reads instantly.

## 7. Watch the tiny red flags

Regular buyers key on details operators never consider (the Odell 4:30am posts → "do I have to wake up at 4:30 too?"; sharks in the office → scammer association). Everything the prospect can see, founder socials included, is part of the ad. Audit creative signals against the life the buyer actually wants.
