Objective of This Document


The majority of the heavy lifting of an ad campaign comes from the creative itself. If the creative can’t get people to stop, watch, listen, and take action… then the rest of the funnel doesn’t matter.


This document outlines the best practices for creating high-performing video ads that grab attention, keep viewers engaged, and drive them to take action. 


These best practices, coupled with strong messaging and a solid understanding of your target audience, are what will bring the most results and get prospects to click through to your landing page, ready to convert.


But even with a great offer and a high-converting landing page, if your ads are not getting attention, you will fail. That’s why the visual elements of an ad are so crucial.






Key Metrics That We Track On The Creative Level & When To Pull These Metrics


Whenever we DETERMINE that the CREATIVE is the main bottleneck in the funnel, we typically look at these sub-statistics that make up overall creative performance that gives us an idea of what is actually wrong with the ads (Messaging? The hook? The body? Retention?).

These metrics provide insight into how well your ads are capturing and retaining viewer attention.


The MAIN KPI we look at as a baseline of ad performance is a Thumb-Stop Rate of 20%+.


This means that 20% of people who see your ad are stopping to watch it, which is a strong indicator that your hook (whether visual, verbal, or both) is strong.


That means people are resonating with your ad.


We then look to see how steep the retention/dropoff is 25% of the way through the video, then 50%, then 75%, then 95%.


Keep in mind, if you have a good Thumb-Stop Rate AND Unique CTR, but a low 25%, 50%, or 75% retention rate, that can be great. Especially if your ad has an early CTA in it or hits a pain point/value benefit hard early on.


That just means people are resonating hard with your hook or the first part of your ad, and clicking through. 


Now, if you have this scenario but your cost per result is NOT good and your video play through to 25% is low, that can mean that people are stopping and clicking through early, but they aren’t properly framed when they get to the page so they are bouncing quick.


See how we can use these metrics to gauge how users are behaving towards our ads and how our ads are impacting them?


When To “Drill Down”


Again - we dive into these SUB metrics whenever we see that the ADS ARE THE MAIN BOTTLENECK.


For example, if you launch a new campaign and see no results (no leads, no booked calls, and a click-through rate below 1% after giving it some ad spend), you then need to investigate the front end of the marketing funnel. 


If you are NOT getting people to even click onto the page, start applications, etc, that’s where we deem the issue to be an ad creative issue.


So that’s where we look into the ads themselves and see if we have some sort of issue with the performance of the creative (by looking at those metrics above to see if we are even grabbing or holding attention in the first place) 


OR audit our messaging to see if it is strong, lazy, or if it even resonates with our target audience.

Component 1: Dressing the Part


What you actually wear and how you present yourself can have a HUGE impact on who you target, and how you are perceived by your target audience. 


It is crucial to dress in a way that aligns with their expectations and the image you want to project.


You have to “play the character” you are trying to be or portray. 


Here are some examples:


If you are targeting accredited investors, a professional and polished look is far more appropriate than trendy or casual wear like Gucci shirts or All Saints graphic T-shirts. You should look like somebody who actually raises money.


If you are a selling YouTube content agency services to coaches and agency owners, you should “look like you do YouTube for coaches and agency owners”. Play the “internet marketer” character. Graphic T-shirts, hoodies, and casual clothing fit more here, versus a full-blown suit, for example.


If you are selling to home service professionals like HVAC owners, roofers, and electricians, try to dress like them. Put a hat on and a polo shirt.


We had a client who sells Google ads to home services, and initially, he was filming with an info product-style agency background with wood panels, lights, and LEDs, and he had normal graphic T-shirts he was wearing. 


The problem with that?:


He looked like every other agency that had previously ripped off these contractors. He looked like an info marketer, not somebody who is a home service marketer. 


So, we had him instead film in front of a Home Depot wearing a hat and a collared shirt, the same attire that electricians and home service people wear, and the performance improved drastically (200+ calls at $60 a call).

If you’re an internet marketer selling to coaches, what do they all look like? Black T-shirts, graphic T-shirts, and just typical “work from home attire”. Sometimes, designer clothes like Balenciaga, All Saints, Off-White, etc.  


Now, say I am an internet marketer or a YouTube content agency owner trying to attract a more professional crowd, like IT Company owners or accounting firm owners. 


What would I do then?


Rent a professional office for the day, put a collared shirt on, nice watch, and make sure I look clean cut. 


This principle applies across all niches; you must look and act the part to build trust and credibility with your target audience.


Looks like you do what you say you do.


Look trusted.

Component 2: Your Environment


Your environment is another critical component of the visual presentation of your ad. 


The setting of your ad should be congruent with your message, your brand, and resonate with your target audience. 


Here’s an example that will encapsulate this.


We had a client who runs a YouTube content agency, and in his first batch of ads, he was filming outside on an outdoor couch.


Sure, everything still looked clean, but guess what?


He didn’t LOOK like a “YouTube guy” because he wasn’t in that setting.


He actually filmed his VSL inside a bedroom that just looked like any standard bedroom. And he actually got comments from leads that canceled the call, saying, “Well, I saw your setup on your landing page and you don’t like you do YouTube”. 


So what did we do then?


We had him refilm everything in a new environment. A podcast studio with multiple camera angles and a clean background.


And what happened then?


The ads took off. VSL play rate went up. The results came pouring in. 


Even with similar messaging.


For the home services client mentioned earlier, filming in front of a Home Depot with his Chevy Truck in the background proved to be far more effective than some generic “marketing agency” or “marketer” background with wood panels and LED lights. 


If you’re a funnel builder, for example, what should your environment be?


Have some awards in the background, some cool money paintings/graphics hung up, LED lights, a clean office environment, etc. 


Your environment needs to match who you say you are and ALSO needs to resonate with the people on the other side of the screen so they think, "This guy's trustworthy. He looks like he does what he says he does." 


You need to play the character convincingly, and the environment you are in plays a massive role in this.


Also, think about Tai Lopez’s ads. 


Most of his ads start with him in his mansion speaking, or walking up to a jet.


What does that scream? 


Success. It makes you want to listen to the guy. 👇
  
  
  



Other times, he is playing basketball because he wants to reach people who resonate with sports. It also adds a visual hook and element of curiosity to the ad.


Or he is washing some of his super cars because he wants his ads to reach people who resonate with super cars.


Guess what we did for one of our Day Trading coaching clients, too?


Had him film ads in front of his trading desk with a ton of charts up. IMMEDIATE resonance & attraction. 👇


  



Meta and these ad platforms take into account the actual environment you are in, what physical items are around you, and what you are saying when they decide who they are going to serve the ad to.


That’s why we need to keep these key things in mind when we are planning ad shoots.








Component 3: Presentation


How you present yourself on camera can make a massive difference as well in ad performance.


And presentation comes down to 2 things:


1. How You Speak & Present The Information


How you present the information verbally can make a big difference in who stops to watch your ad and who keeps watching.


Are you stuttering? 


Too monotone? 


Talking too fast? 


Too slow? 


Is the audio too quiet? 


Too loud/mic is distorting?


You should have strong intonation and energy when filming. 


And really look and present yourself like a confident individual (unless you are going for a different vibe like being more “sarcastic” or “funny” in your ad of course).


2. How You Look On Camera


Investing in a decent studio setup or finding a podcast/filming studio to shoot in is worth every penny.


If your camera quality sucks or your lighting is off, you’ll get punished with bad ad results.


Make sure your setup is looking clean, you have good lighting, and a good camera.


Camera quality can very well be a scroll stopper, so keep that in mind.


Use this filming guide & equipment SOP if you need help on this front: [GOOGLE_DOC_URL]


Don’t cheap out on your equipment.


If you need to hire a videographer or spend a day setting up a studio. Do it.

We see time and time again big lifts in ad performance just by improving the actual quality of the ads themselves and how the speaker looks on camera.


If the quality is bad, people will swipe away and have a bd perception of you and your brand right off the bat.


































Component 4: Messaging


The words you speak are just as important as the visuals you present on your ad… if not more.


Your messaging does a majority of the targeting nowadays with your creatives.


That’s why we focus so much on the messaging in our ads and diversifying our messaging.


Your hooks must be punchy, direct, and highly relevant to your audience's needs and desires. 


Avoid fluffy, generic statements and instead focus on articulating the prospect’s REAL-TIME problems, pain points, desires, and circumstances that they are experiencing right now.


Again, all in a direct and clearly articulated way. 


Do not go in circles and repeat yourself a ton of times in your hook, saying the same exact thing 3 different ways. 


Be direct and highly articulate.


If your messaging is loose, fluffy, or lazy at the beginning, especially in the hook, then it's going to get skipped.


While we have a ton of training on ad scripting and ad messaging, here is a quick breakdown of how to write killer ads.


First, we need to sit down and write down WHO we are trying to target in the first place.


Get very granular.


For example, say we are targeting coaches who are spending $500+ per day on ads and want to scale them.


We want to write down that singular avatar and then write down as many pain points, problems, situations they are in, and desires they have as possible.


You can also use an LLM like Manus to help you ideate these things.


But here is what that exercise can look like:


Target Avatar: Coaches spending $500+ per day on ads who want to scale and are running their ads themselves right now 


(you can also have a separate avatar for coaches who have agencies running their ads but they outgrew them or they are underperforming… see how granular we can get when we break out these avatars?)


Pains/Desires/Circumstances
* They scaled up to this point in spend, but have no idea how to scale it further
* They don’t have time to ideate ads and learn how to scale
* They simply want someone to take over their ads now who is an expert
* They keep hitting a wall with their ad spend and can’t break past it


These are all “reasons why” the avatar should buy our stuff and resonate with our ad.


Ideally, we want to think of as many of these reasons as possible, and what we do from here is make individual ads (or hooks if you are doing the “hook and body” ad scripting method) for EACH reason, and we test those.


Now our hooks again need to be the focal point of scripting and filming.


If your hook fails, the ad fails.


The messaging HAS to hit the prospect right in the face, stop them in their tracks, and make them think “whoa, this guy knows what I am experiencing right now/need right now. How does he know that? Is he stalking me?”


That’s why we invited what we call “Stalker Hooks” (shoutout to my COO Cam here).


Stalker Hooks are statements that are so specific and targeted that they make the viewer feel as though you are reading their mind. 


These hooks should make them think, "Oh yeah, this is crazy. This is exactly what I was thinking about. This is exactly what I was talking about and what I'm experiencing. These guys know my problems. They know what I want. I need to listen more to this ad."


Examples of effective stalker hooks include:


* "If you’re spending a couple of hundred dollars per day on ads, I bet you’re hitting this annoying scaling wall at $500 or $1,000 per day in spend. Everything just seems to break there. Let me tell you why you can’t get past that wall.”


* “Are your leads just feeling super cold and guarded lately? Like, they just sling out a ton of objections at you, and they hold onto their wallet super tight? Here’s how you can change that.”


* "If you’re using Google LSA or getting leads from Home Advisor, I bet you’re tired of sharing leads with every other roofer in your area, and are really tired of getting price shopped as a result. Let me show you how you can change that."


* "If you’ve been posting on YouTube for months but barely booking calls, I’ll step in and get you your next high-ticket client through YouTube in the next 30 days guaranteed." 


See how these are articulations of specific scenarios that people experience?


You need to articulate their real desires, real pain points, and urgent reasons they need to listen to you and take action. 


At the same time, if you're pulling in bad leads that are unqualified, you're probably not articulating the problems and pain points of your target market sufficiently, so they're not actually resonating with what you're saying and stopping to watch.


You’re either reaching a less qualified demographic of people through what you’re saying, or what you’re saying just isn’t hitting a nerve with your target market at all.


Messaging is so powerful when you dial it in.

Visual Editing Hack: Using Banners On Video Ads


Sometimes we'll add actual banners on the ad above the video itself (not overlaid on the video). 


This involves using a 4x5 aspect ratio instead of the typical 9x16 format. 


The top part has a banner that usually features a black or colored background, taking up about 10-15% of the actual frame. 


In this banner, we'll include some sort of pain statement, question, desire, or articulate the offer itself. 


This tactic helps get people to stop scrolling by immediately communicating the value proposition before they even hear the audio.


It’s adding a “visual hook” to the ad in text format.


To see banner examples, scroll down to the “banners” section in our ad editing SOP: https://gamma.app/docs/Editing-Ad-Creatives-That-Make-Our-Clients-Violently-Rich-c8vg01t92776br5


  





Using Scene Changes for Retention


To keep retention high, try varying up your scenes, camera angles, or visuals every few seconds.


Introduce VARIETY.


Even minor background changes throughout a long body can keep viewers engaged and watching.


Sit down for a line. Then stand up. Walk towards the camera. 


Vary up what you are doing, what environment you are in, your props, etc. as you progress through the ad.


This technique, borrowed from short-form content, helps to keep the viewer engaged and entertained, preventing them from losing interest and scrolling away.


And guess what?


If you can get someone to watch more of your ad, they will be more likely to click and head to your landing page, AND they will be framed way better/way more informed when they land there.


Component 6: The Editing


Finally, the technical aspects of your ad - editing, audio quality, and text overlays - play a crucial role in your creative’s overall effectiveness. 


The editing itself can either turn people off or keep them engaged. 


Hyper editing can look good for example, but just make sure that it doesn’t distract from the overall message of the ad.


Also… Don’t be cheap when hiring an editor. 


Get someone who is good, and vet their work.


You need a good editor who understands how to edit ads, ideally someone who has edited viral short-form content or e-commerce ads.


And also make sure after you run the ads that they edited to give them feedback on the performance and metrics of those ads.


After you get a batch of ads edited, make sure to look at it from an objective, non-biased perspective. 


Does this actually look good? 


Is the editing trash or mediocre? 


Is it not edited enough? 


Is it boring? 


Is it TOO overly edited? 


Should we have less overlays? 


More overlays? 


Change the times the overlays slide into frame?


Ask yourself all of these questions and more when you are quality checking your ads. And of course, make sure you don’t have spelling errors and such on the captions.


A good idea as well is to get feedback from communities you are in, partners, employees, or friends on your ads.


Critical editing guidelines:


* Avoid stock images and stock footage at all costs. They look like cheap garbage and perform terribly.
* Don’t use lazy editing with generic icons
* Visuals should not distract from the messaging in the ad, only aid in it.
* Don’t use crazy or confusing to read fonts
* Don’t have weird gradients and filters
* Use captions
* If I were to watch this ad with the sound off, do I still know what is going on and what the message is?
* Make sure to follow our full ad editing SOP here and also keep main elements out of the “dead zone”: https://gamma.app/docs/Editing-Ad-Creatives-That-Make-Our-Clients-Violently-Rich-c8vg01t92776br5


Audio quality is also non-negotiable. 


If the audio from the person speaking is bad, it can completely ruin ad performance. 


Similarly, if the background music is too loud or inappropriate, it can negatively impact results.

Diversifying Your Creatives


To maintain long-term success and avoid ad fatigue, it is crucial to continuously test a wide variety of messaging, styles of ads, and creatives in general.


Especially during Meta Andromeda. 


This again includes diversifying your messaging, exploring different reasons why a customer should buy your product or service, and experimenting with the various visual elements discussed in this document.

Component 7: The Offer


Your actual OFFER itself can also pull people through an ad and get them to stop scrolling and listen.


That’s why we focus on having strong and attractive front-end offers that are cold traffic optimized.


Look at Listkit for example. 


Their cold email setup offer when it launched was unheard of. 


Totally new. Fresh. Attractive, Interesting. 


And it had an INSANE value proposition (we will install a system for a one-time payment that helps you send hundreds to thousands of cold emails per month to decision makers in your market to book calls). 


A front-end offer we ran for a while was we will beat your best performing ad campaign guaranteed for $297.


We of course had to go through a rigid qualification process on the standard event we were using through the funnel to ONLY get people who were actively spending $300+/day on ads (our target market for that offer so we could ascend them), but this was a super attractive offer that pulled people in and stopped the scroll.


Especially when we added a TON of proof into the hook/body itself so people thought “wtf, this is a smoking deal and super interesting”.
Conclusion


The primary objective of mastering visual hooks is to get more people to stop scrolling and watch your ads.


 However, the benefits extend far beyond simple engagement metrics. 


By creating more engaging and informative ads, you are also pre-framing and educating your prospects before they even reach your landing page. 


This leads to a more qualified and informed customer, resulting in higher conversion rates, lower cost per call, and a more efficient sales process.
