## What this covers

The creative-side rules: what the words, visuals and audio of an ad may and may not do. This is the pre-flight check for hooks, primary text, headlines, video scripts, UGC scripts, static ads and VSL-style ad cuts. Targeting restrictions, Special Ad Categories and the financial-products list live in the sibling file `policy-and-special-ad-categories.md`. Load both for any finance, trading or lending offer.

This is an internal compliance summary, not legal advice. Meta's pages are the source of truth.

## How ad review works

- Review is mostly automated and runs before the ad goes live: "Our ad review system relies primarily on automated tools to check ads and business assets against our policies. Our ad review process starts automatically before ads begin running, and is typically completed within 24 hours, although it may take longer in some cases." (Advertising Standards index)
- The whole unit is reviewed, not just the copy: "This review process may include the specific components of an ad, such as images, video, text and targeting information, as well as an ad's associated landing page or other destinations, among other information." (Advertising Standards index)
- Rejection can reach past the ad: "If a violation is found at any point in the review process, the ad will be rejected, and the Business Account or its assets may be restricted." (Advertising Standards index)
- Rejected ads can usually be edited and resubmitted: "Advertisers whose ads are rejected will typically be provided an opportunity to edit their ads in order to bring them into compliance and can request another review." (Advertising Standards index)
- Meta names 3 common points of confusion on the index page: Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes, sexually suggestive content, and advertising restrictions on business assets.

**What that means for a script:** the spoken words, on-screen captions, text baked into the image, the headline, the primary text and the landing page are all in scope. A clean caption does not save a script that says the banned thing out loud, and a clean ad does not save a landing page that makes the banned claim.

## Rule 1: Personal Attributes (the one that hits our copy most)

Policy (last updated Jun 26, 2024):
> "Ads must not contain content that shares or asks for private information about users. In addition, ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes."

The listed attributes: race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), vulnerable financial status, voting status, membership in a trade union, criminal record, or name.

Ads cannot:
- Share or ask for personal attributes of a user or their family
- Imply the advertiser knows someone's personal attributes
- Imply knowledge of financial information
- Imply awareness of personally identifiable information like names
- Imply knowledge of medical information

Ads can:
- Broadly reference attributes that are not on the list
- Make passing references to gender, age groups or age ranges
- Use celebrity or fictional character names
- Use "you/your" language that does not carry a listed attribute
- Run health public service announcements that do not assert someone has a condition

Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/

## Rule 2: Prohibited Commercial Practices (income, outcome and guarantee claims)

Ad policy: "Ads Must Comply with the Community Standard on Prohibited Commercial Practices." Rationale: "We aim to protect users and businesses from being deceived out of their money, property, or personal information."

Items on the Community Standard that touch our verticals (as returned by the 2026-10-02 pull, selected, not the full list):
- "Offers of investment opportunities where returns on investment are guaranteed"
- "Offers of investment opportunities claiming...quick returns (such as under 24 hours)"
- "Offers to turn a certain sum of money into a larger one through flipping"
- "Offers of opportunities of unrealistic financial reward for unclear or minimal effort"
- "False offers of a guaranteed reward of real money or consumer goods"
- "Falsely promises money in exchange for an up-front fee/wire transfer/payment"
- "Celebrity-bait scams, which involve falsely using the name, likeness, or imagery"
- "Brand-bait scams, which involve falsely claiming to represent...an established business"
- "Loan offers requiring the user to pay an upfront fee to obtain a loan"

Sources: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/prohibited-commercial-practices/ and https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/prohibited-commercial-practices/

## Rule 3: Profanity

Policy (last updated Oct 14, 2025): "Ads must not contain profanity."

- Prohibited: "Profanity and obscene language, even when it's obscure, misspelled or vague" and "Profane gestures, including those that use photo editing or alteration".
- Workarounds are named as non-compliant: misspellings such as "$hit", asterisks, symbol or emoji substitution, special characters, diacritics and letter spacing.
- Allowed: "Fully blurred profanity, or bleeped or covered audio, as long as it's not used excessively" and "Acronyms that stand for profane language, as long as the acronym itself does not spell a profane word".

Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/profanity/

## Rule 4: Health and Wellness (fitness, body, wellness offers)

Policy (last updated July 22, 2026):
- "Ads promoting or marketing dietary, health, or weight loss or weight gain products and services must be targeted to people at least 18 years or older."
- Not allowed: "statements of inferiority about physical appearance", and imagery that pinches fat to highlight a problem area.
- Not allowed: "clickbait tactics in a health, weight loss, or weight gain context, such as sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers."
- Before and after: listed as permitted for 18+ audiences for "General cosmetic products, procedures, surgeries depicting before and after transformation."
- The age restriction does not apply to "products and services marketed for general wellbeing, such as fitness services and equipments, health clubs".

Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/

## Rule 5: Third-party IP, music and likeness

Policy (last updated Aug 26, 2024): "Ads may not contain content that violates the intellectual property rights of any third party, including copyright, trademark or other legal rights."

- Music: "If your ad contains music you are required to secure the necessary licenses (e.g. for the sound recording and/or the musical composition)".
- Meta may reject or remove ads on a rights-holder report, or where there are signs of infringement without a formal complaint.

Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/third-party-infringement/

## Rule 6: Misinformation and deceptive links

- Misinformation (last updated Jul 18, 2024): "Ads cannot contain content debunked by third-party fact checkers." Repeat offenders "may have restrictions placed on their ability to advertise across Meta technologies."
- Spam (last updated Jun 26, 2024): "Ads must not share Deceptive Links or attempt to buy, sell, or exchange platform assets, features, privileges, or engagement."

Sources: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/unacceptable-content/misinformation/ and https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/business-assets/spam/

## How AID applies this (our reading, not Meta's wording)

Run this before any ad copy or creative script is presented. The examples are ours, written to show the pattern. They are not Meta's examples.

1. **Call out the situation or the role, never the reader's condition.** Addressing a role is fine ("Agency owners running paid ads"). Asserting a listed attribute about the viewer is not ("You're broke", "Still drowning in debt?", "Struggling with anxiety?", "Hey Sarah"). Questions count as implying. This sits alongside the Haynes rule to open on circumstances and never name the income qualifier in the hook: describe the circumstance in the third person or as the speaker's own story, not as a fact about the viewer.
2. **Money is the highest-risk attribute for our clients.** "Vulnerable financial status" and "imply knowledge of financial information" are both on the list. Lines that tell the viewer what their bank account, income, debt or trading account looks like are out. Lines about what the client or a named customer did are in.
3. **Results are told as someone's real result, never as a promise to the viewer.** A documented client result with its timeframe is proof. The same number framed as what the viewer will get, as guaranteed, or as fast and effortless, is the pattern Rule 2 targets. No "guaranteed returns", no "turn X into Y", no reward for little or no effort. Offer guarantees (refund terms) are stated as the terms of the offer, not as a guaranteed income.
4. **Real proof only.** No fabricated screenshots, receipts, P&L, testimonials, news-outlet framing or implied endorsement by a famous person or established brand. This is the same rule as "never invent proof" in CLAUDE.md.
5. **No profanity in any form**, including censored spellings, symbols and emojis that stand in for a swear word. Applies to spoken lines, captions and on-screen text. Already a hard copy rule for every client.
6. **Body and health offers:** no line or visual that makes the viewer feel inferior about their body, no specific result in a set timeframe without a disclaimer, 18+ targeting where the offer is weight loss, diet or cosmetic.
7. **Assets:** only licensed music, only logos and likenesses we have the right to use. Competitor names and logos in creative need a second look before they ship.
8. **The landing page is part of the ad.** Any claim cut from the ad for policy reasons must also be cut from the page it points to, and the page must deliver what the ad says it will.
9. **Finance, trading, lending, insurance, housing, employment:** also load `policy-and-special-ad-categories.md` before writing.

When a line is cut or reworded for policy, say so in the handover so Ellis can see what changed and why.
