﻿The Howitzer Video Sales Letter
A howitzer isn’t about precision sniping. It’s about dropping massive, decisive firepower early, then keeping up a relentless bombardment until nothing’s left standing.


That’s exactly how this VSL works:


Presented by: [CREATOR_NAME]
[CREATOR_BRAND]©
  



Purpose: 


Increases conversion rates, booked calls, sales, lead quality and lowers CAC with a properly dialed in video sales letter that conveys your offer in a clear and persuasive manner.


Who is this for?


This structure laid out inside this document is specifically tailored for a book a call funnel with cold traffic (strangers) being sent into it.


The principles and theory stay the same but the actual structure will vary between conversion mechanisms.


What is a VSL?
A video sales letter is essentially an extended version of your ad.
It should reiterate who this is for, the reasons they should buy, and explain exactly what they get in more detail.
There are many structures, tactics, and methods to creating video sales letters (VSLs) that convert.
But the structure in this document is easy and has proven to work for us time and time again.
How to use this SOP
This document gives you a battle-tested structure for scripting a high-converting Video Sales Letter (VSL) - specifically for book-a-call funnels with cold traffic.
It’s not a word-for-word script.
It’s the scaffolding.
Follow the structure.
Use the examples.
And adapt it to your offer and buyer.
Write each part like it’s the only thing they’ll hear - because odds are, they’ll skim, skip, or click around.
That’s why we lead with the CTA, repeat the core promise, and reinforce it in multiple ways.
Objective of this document
Most people write VSL’s completely backwards because they assume a funnel is linear. Someone watches your whole ad → watches your whole vsl → books a call.
This is not the case, and if you write things in your funnel like this, you will be poor.
So don’t build your video like a novel.
Every section should:
* Make sense on its own
* Reinforce the core message
* Have a mini payoff or “ah-ha” moment
* Give them another reason to book
That’s why we pitch early…
…restate/repeat similar points throughout…
…and stack credibility near the end.
You’re not trying to be fancy or intriguing.
You’re trying to catch the viewer wherever they’re at in their buying thought process - and hand them a way to act.
And we’re trying to give them the “one thing” they need to go ahead and book a call.
Which leads us to the question:
What is a VSL? (And its purpose)
A video sales letter is essentially an extended version of your ad.
It should reiterate who this is for, the reasons they should buy, and explain exactly what they get in more detail.
The average engagement rate for a VSL sits between 20% - 30% we have found that having a “mini pitch” in this first 30% of your VSL drastically increases conversion rates as opposed to the classic: “Hook them in to watch the rest” approach
So our first 2-3 minutes will be the absolute most important and we give all of the possible information someone should need to book a call, and the rest of the VSL is just reiterating what was said in the hook in more detail for people who are a bit more skeptical.
The hook does the heavy lifting, it lets them know there’s an offer, the benefits - everything they need. And the rest of the VSL is just prodding until we find the one reason they haven’t taken action yet.
Part 1: The Hook
The hook is by far the most important part of your video sales letter.
We see that on average VSL retention sits at around 20% - 30%.
So why would we wait to give our call to action or offer at the end of the video when only a handful of people make it that far?
It’s stupid.
So here’s what we do in the first 60-180 seconds:
1. Immediately let the ideal prospect know that this is for them
2. Talk about the big benefit your product or service delivers
3. Reinforce the outcome that will happen because of the benefit
4. Explain why this works differently than what they’ve tried
5. Give action takers an opportunity to take action right away
6. Stack proof, relevance, and uniqueness, instantly

All inside 60-180 seconds.
We are not creating intrigue.
We’re not hooking them in.
We’re not trying to bribe them to watch the whole video.
We are giving them all the possible information they would need to take action within the first 1-3 minutes of the video sales letter.
Because the person watching this might:
   * Be on the toilet
   * Be in between calls
   * Be watching on mute
   * Be skeptical as hell
   * (And most likely) ready to buy, but needs the final shove or a recap of what they’re getting
So we frontload everything.
We treat the first 30% of the VSL like a standalone sales pitch - because most people won’t watch more than that anyway.
If they book off the hook, great.
If not, the rest of the video is just deepening the logic, clarity, and trust until the belief gap is closed.
This is contrary to popular belief.
A lot of traditional copy and marketing practices say “wait until the end to reveal you have a product” or “provide value upfront” - that’s how you convert broke, shitty clients.
We’re targeting people who give us a lot of money and who are a delight to work with.
Not the general population - the “in market buyer.”
The in market buyer:
   * Is already problem-aware
   * Has tried other solutions before
   * Is actively looking for a solution to their problem right now
   * Is ready to take action when they hear the right things
   * Wants clarity, not “free value” 
   * Will give you money if you show them it’s worth it
That’s why our hook sells.
We say what this is, who it’s for, what makes it different, what they’ll get, and how to take action - all before they click away.
Example of a Great Hook:


If you’re a Chiropractor, I will help you get 30-60 new patient appointments every single month, through our “attract, retain, and refer” strategy.


Just like we’ve done for 134 other Chiropractors like: John, Mary, & Steven


This way you can focus solely on patient care, while your calendar gets automatically filled with potential patients who are ready to pay for high-ticket care packages, and of course have the money for it…


And if you’ve tried it all before, maybe you’ve even worked with a “marketing guy” who promised the world but just delivered expensive, low-quality leads…


…This is completely different. Because we pay for 100% of your ad spend. And we only get paid when a patient PAYS for a high-ticket package.


Not leads. Not calls. Not even appointments.


High-ticket care packages that put cash in your pocket.


Until we deliver cold hard cash for you, we lose money.


If that sounds good to you, and you know this is something you need, there’s a button below this video to book in a quick call with me where we’ll run through your business and see if this makes sense for you.


If you need a bit more info, stick around and let me tell you about myself, our track record, and how this all works.

Part 2: Early CTA (Mini Checkpoint)
You saw it in the example above, but I’ll go over it in a bit more detail here.
This is where we add a quick checkpoint call-to-action right after the hook - for the people who don’t need any more convincing.
You're going to get a lot of viewers who are already nodding, already bought into the hook, and just need permission to act.
We give them a fast lane.
If you don’t do this, sure some people will book anyway, but you’d miss out on the people who need that extra push (which is a lot)
You’re also giving permission that this is really all they need to know, so there’s no need for them to stick around (and potentially lose attention, even though they were otherwise interested)
Example Mini CTA:
“Now if that sounds like exactly what you’ve been looking for - If you already know this is something your business needs, there's a button below this video where you can hook a call.
We’ll walk through your business, see if you’re a fit, and I’ll show you exactly how we’d roll this out for you - start to finish.
If you’re still on the fence or just want to make sure this isn’t another BS marketing thing - totally cool. Stick around and I’ll show you exactly how this works, why it’s different, and what makes it perform.”
This CTA reinforces action early without being pushy. It gives “yes” buyers the path forward and makes “maybe” buyers feel respected - keeping them engaged without triggering resistance.

Part 3: The Introduction
Here we take a step back and introduce ourselves.
Quick note: every section hereon out has one purpose - give the viewer the “one thing” missing for them to take action.
We already gave them the meat and potatoes in the intro - now it’s a game of prodding until we give that one phrase, piece of proof, or explanation that makes the cost of inaction (clicking off) outweigh the cost of action (booking a call).
It’s that simple.
The point of our introduction is to let the cold viewer (stranger) understand our authority from a high level and why we’re an expert in the field.
It’s also a nice “breathing point” after we’ve just unloaded a shit ton of information in the hook.
________________


What to include in your introduction:
   1. Your name

   2. Your experience

   3. An implied authority sentence or two

   4. Why you’re qualified to talk about this

It should read like:

“Okay, let me take a quick step back and tell you who I am and why you should even listen to me in the first place… My name is Cam Allen, I’m the COO of [CREATOR_BRAND] and I’ve personally written over 1,000 ad scripts, our company has generated over $38M in directly attributable revenue for our clients and we’ve worked with industry leaders such as [x], [y] and [z].
I’m not telling you this to brag, I just want to establish why I’m even qualified to talk about this in the first place”

Part 4: The Core Problem
Next we want to let the viewer know that we understand their problem.
Because if you can clearly articulate their exact situation, the problem they’re facing (the problem that made them want to click your ad), and how it’s manifesting in their life right now - not “pressing the problem” to a cringe and disingenuous level - you will achieve true emotional resonance.
Emotional Resonance (verb): “The deep, often subconscious connection that individuals feel when they encounter emotions, stories, or experiences that align with their own feelings or values.”
And if you just do that… it doesn’t matter what else you say, the prospect will believe and trust you. And they WILL take action.
It’s that simple.
Why this matters:
The other reason we do this is to subtly remind them how this problem is making them feel:
      1. In life
      2. In business
      3. In their own mind
Because pain is a big driving factor in decision making.
Important note on pain-based messaging:
Now we have to be very careful here. Typical “pain based messaging” that you see in 1930s sales letters for dick pills does not work with the “in market buyer” - he is more sophisticated, has less time for BS, and sees through typical marketing shit because he has seen it all.
They also tend to operate from a more “sure of themself” level - who are you to tell him how he feels?
So your pain-based messaging has to be more subtle, more “objective.”
Rather than talking about how “now he’s questioning why he ever started this business to begin with” and how “his wife is doubting him constantly”…
You’re talking about how this problem is frustrating him because his business is stuck - it’s plateaued, and it’s pissing him off.
Frustration is a higher frequency emotion than fear, doubt, and self pity.
And the in-market buyer tends to be a higher frequency individual.
He is aware of his problem, he has the agency to do something about it.
  



(Frustration falls on Pride/Anger)
Talking to emotions below this level tend to attract desperate, nightmare clients (you know the type, the ones who see you as a lifeline, pay you the least, and ask for the most)
Important note too: someone can still be a high frequency (ambitious, amazing client etc) individual, yet still experience a low frequency emotion.
It’s a spectrum for a reason, but if your entire marketing strategy is around low frequency emotions you’ll attract poo.
This stuff is a bit woo-woo but it’s important to understand why different people behave and relate to different types of emotional messaging.
And frequency and consciousness are great thinking models for that.
You might view this chart and ask, “why not only target people moving towards pleasure?” good question, that’s solely because it's easier to convert someone with a problem, and a problem 90% of the time spawns negative emotion in the human brain.
Targeting higher frequency negative emotions is how you get the best of both worlds.

You’ve probably been having a couple beers with friends and someone starts talking about an exact situation you were in and it gets you all excited and you interject and start adding on things to what he's saying, finishing the sentence and finally SOMEONE ELSE GETS IT.
That's what we’re trying to do here.
Again, if we can articulate their situation and their problems clearly and how they would SELF IDENTIFY, pretty much nothing else matters - you’ve got them and they’re ready for an offer.
The problem section needs to be extremely clear and backed by real data and research from your clients problems and you have to articulate it in a way how they identify as.
For example:
If you’re working with roofing contractors who can’t get leads predictably.
You WON’T say: “are you struggling to get qualified leads predictably”
You WOULD say: “are you finding it impossible to get your phone ringing with ready-to-book homeowners?”
This is a real example. It’s all about using the messaging and words they use - once you do this and you speak like your clients and you assimilate into their thinking and brain - you win.

Part 5: Introduce the False Solution
This is one of those things that a lot of people miss, or do but don’t realize it.
And 99.999% of people who try to do this fuck it up so badly you wouldn’t believe it.
Probably one of the most powerful lines in copywriting, psychology, and marketing is:
“[Problem] isn’t because of [x], it’s actually because of [y]”
Where:
         * x = common cause

         * y = uncommon cause

This goes hand-in-hand with:
“Most people think the solution to [problem] is [x], but it’s actually [y]”
Where again:
            * x = common solution

            * y = uncommon solution

Why this works:
Psychologically, this works because of something called cognitive dissonance - the internal discomfort someone feels when their beliefs and actions contradict each other.
  



This is a psychological state where the brain can’t hold two opposing ideas at once without feeling tension - so it’s forced to resolve the conflict.
Your job in this section is to intentionally create that tension, by showing them that what they believe (or what they’ve been doing) is flawed…
…and then immediately offer a new belief that feels clearer, more accurate, and easier to act on.
That’s how you shift behavior and become an authority and trusted expert.
What people get violently wrong:
Now people who don’t understand this, but use this tactic anyway - and they fuck it up.
Because for this to work - you need to already have trust built with the prospect.
They already need to think:
               1. You know what you’re on about
               2. You know THEIR situation
               3. You are the right person to listen to

(This is why we focus heavily on the “problem introduction” section)
If you haven’t proven that you understand their problem...
If you haven’t shown results or established authority...
If you haven’t made them feel seen…
Then you haven’t earned the right to challenge their beliefs.
It just ends up with the prospect thinking “this guy is full of shit”
And their brain will resolve the tension by dismissing you - not listening to you.
And you get no monies.
Here’s a clear example:
“Most IT Companies think the reason they can’t scale is because they need more leads - so they try hiring cold callers, buying lead lists, running ads, but it just ends up with them constantly working with one-off jobs, getting stuck at $10k-$20k mrr but somehow working 12-14 hour days CONSTANTLY”
“But in reality, you’re only stuck because you’re charging $100 per seat, have no real sales process or way to attract actual recurring revenue, and you have no offer that justifies charging $300+ per seat. Because if you did, you can make way more, without increasing your workload, hiring, or adding 100 other services onto your package.”
Here’s how this creates and resolves cognitive dissonance:
                  1. They believe:
                  1. “I need more leads to grow.”

                     2. They’re acting on that belief by:
                     1. Spending on outbound, buying tools, running cold traffic, or waiting on referrals.

                        3. You introduce a contradictory (but believable) idea:
                        1. “You don’t need more leads - you need to fix your offer, pricing, and sales process first. Otherwise, more leads just means more rejection and wasted time.”

                           4. Now they’re in dissonance:
                           1. They thought leads = growth. But their actual results haven’t improved - and now you’ve pointed out why.

                              5. They feel tension.
                              1. Their actions don’t align with your insight. If they trust you, that tension pulls them into your worldview.

                                 6. If they accept your belief, they think:
                                 1. “Shit. Maybe I don’t need more traffic. Maybe I need to charge more and close better.”

                                    7. Now they’re primed for the next section of the VSL
                                    1. You introduce your offer, a sales system that helps them charge $300/seat, close recurring deals, and scale without chasing new leads.
That’s how you break their old mental model… and replace it with one that makes your mechanism the obvious answer.
I want to really drive this point home, so here’s another example of when cognitive dissonance fails due to a lack of authority or proof:
Bryan Johnson vs Grandma
It’s the exact same as if you’re drinking a coke zero and your grandma says:
“You know they’re worse for you than real coke.
You think fuck off mate, and there’s 0 uncomfortable thoughts or doubt in your head.
But if Bryan Johnson (the vampire dude who wants to live forever and spends like $5M a year on anti aging) says:
“Are you always waking up tired, and no amount of caffeine seems to do the trick, and this just makes it so you can’t focus like you used to and you’re just constantly getting brain fog… and it’s annoying you because everyone around you seems to be grinding 12 hour days with no issue?”
“Most people think its the amount of sleep you get, but its actually the QUALITY of the sleep you get… and funnily enough your sleep quality has nothing to do with the amount of sugar you consume, seed oils, or no screens before bedtime… we’ve recently found its artificial sweeteners, more specifically the artificial sweeteners they use in sodas like coke zero, pepsi max, and dr pepper”
“We’ve found that aspartame, because it’s substituting for the naturally sweet sugar, has to be loaded up with this chemical that can't make it past the blood brain barrier…”
Not just because he’s Bryan Johnson, but because he’s given a logical argument and also specifically explained that he understands your situation and pain and problem.
Now you’re more likely to have that inner conflict…
Now you’re sitting there holding a Coke Zero thinking:
“I like drinking Coke Zero. I drink it every day. But it might be fucking up my brain?”
Boom. Dissonance.
-
And now Bryan Johnson is in a position to introduce more information to me, press the pain of not having good focus… ORRR
Introduce a new product.
A soda with 0 calories that tastes like coke zero and doesn’t have any of the artificial sweeteners.
Execution:
In this section you want to talk about what the prospect has tried before, give them a reason as to why it didn’t work.
(Not the “it’s not your fault” BS)
And logically explain why everything else is shit and your solution isn’t.

                                    * You’ll get your time back - and still grow faster

Part 7: Reinstate the Big Promise


This is your “bring it home” moment.


We’ve walked them through the core problem, the lies they’ve believed, and the actual system that works.


Now we remind them - boldly and clearly - that this is exactly what we’re offering.


We restate the big promise we opened with…except this time, they fully understand what it means.


And they want it.


“And yes - this is exactly what we help you install inside 7 Figure MSP


It’s a plug & play sales and marketing system specifically for MSPs, designed to double your MRR within 60-120 days.


A system that doesn’t rely on referrals or word of mouth.


A sales process that lets you charge $300/seat instead of $100.


And a repeatable offer that turns cold traffic into consistent monthly revenue - without chasing, discounting, or praying.”


What to include in this section:


                                    * Restate your core transformation promise (the thing from the Hook)
                                    * Tie it directly to the mechanism you just explained
                                    * Use emotionally resonant language, not just dry “what we do” copy
                                    * Keep it benefit-rich, not deliverables-heavy (save those for Part 9)


This isn’t revealing that you’re selling something (we already established that in the intro) we’re just again giving the viewer permission to either take action or remind them that we’ve been saying all of this stuff for a reason: to help them get a result.

Part 8: Case Studies / Proof
This is the perfect time to give some hard proof.
We’ve let them know this is for them.
We’ve told them why what they’ve tried hasn’t worked.
We’ve told them why our thing works.
We’ve told them how their business and life will look after.
Now the only possible doubt is…
“Have you done this before?”
“Has this worked for someone like me?”
So we have to answer them!
It’s important that you have to make your case studies feel believable, and you’re not rambling on.
I like to do a 3 part formula for case studies.
                                    1. Who you helped + industry they’re in
                                    2. What you helped them do
                                    3. How you helped them do it
But this all has to happen in 1 or 2 sentences at max.
“We helped George drop his cost per call by 72% after identifying one single bottleneck in his funnel”
I keep it to 3 specific case studies.
And then a line or two below it with implied authority, which is something like:
“And over 56+ other clients we’ve helped drop their cost per call and scale predictably with paid advertising that they don’t even have to think about”
Visually too, you absolutely need to show pictures of the clients, and screenshots of results.
Something we like to do as well is throw up their social media handles and tell them to go message them asking how they found working with us.
No one else does this and it gives a nice little boost of trust.
“And yeah all their handles are on the screen and below this video in our case studies section, you can go message them and ask how it was working with us - we’ve not paid them or anything haha, in fact they don’t even know I’m giving out their social media handles but I’m just so confident in the jobs we’ve done for these guys”
Part 9: List What They Get
Now, we’re justifying with hard logic.
We’re making them see exactly what they get.
We’ve talked a lot about theory, benefits, logic, and all that stuff here.
Now it’s time to talk concrete about what they physically get with our product or service.
We’ve gotta make it feel like A LOT here.
For some reason people think it's enough to just “promise an outcome” that's so stupid because the prospect cannot see how you’re going to do it.
They can’t feel it or touch it.
Even if you explain the mechanism, show you’ve got the results.
There’s still that gap of “What am I actually going to get and how are you going to do this”
It’s like buying a pizza oven and on the box it says “you get everything you need to make pizza with this oven”
But there’s no list of contents, it doesn’t say: “you get a pizza slide, a bag of coal, an anti stick grate, oven gloves” etc
Plus this makes our offer just look way juicier and it's a logical step.
You’re doing something called “value stacking” - making the offer seem big and huge and like they’re robbing you blind for everything you’re giving them.
Key here is to be genuine and not lie or big up the offer too much.
And please for the love of god don’t do any of this fucking “valued at $497” bullshit.
It’s a pretty simple section - you’re literally just listing features.
Throw in a couple “so that you can” statements but only on more abstract and less implied-benefit features.
Example:
“30 days of ongoing coaching so that if you run into any roadblocks, you can just message me and we’ll get it solved instantly and you’ll feel 100% confident in running this system yourself.”


I explain the benefits of the coaching because “coaching” itself doesn’t get anyone super excited or immediately understand the implied benefits.


I don’t need to explain the benefits when I say something like:


“Your entire funnel built completely done for you”


In this section I DON’T need to add: “so you don’t have to touch clickfunnels”


Get it?

Part 11: Final CTA


This is where you close the loop, and make them feel like an irresponsible business owner for at least not hearing you out on a call.


We’re not saying “maybe check it out.”


We’re saying:


“This works. You’ve seen how. Here’s how to get it. Do it now, what’s to lose? 30 minutes?”


You're summarizing the entire VSL in about 30–60 seconds - but it shouldn’t feel like a summary. It should feel like a natural next step.


Example:


“So, if you’re still stuck charging $150 per seat, relying on referrals, or constantly chasing one-off break-fix jobs…


And you want to finally install a real sales system that closes $300/seat retainers with ease…


That’s exactly what we build with you inside 7 figure MSP.


We work with you 1-1 to help you reposition your offer, build sales assets, turn your one-off jobs into recurring revenue, and give you everything you could possibly need to double your MRR within 60-120 days and become an exit-ready business.


Just like we’ve done for over 2,000+ MSPs over the last decade.


This system works and if you want in on it so that you can scale your business drastically in 2025 and beyond, hit that button below this video to book in a free MSP audit Zoom call.


On the call we’ll look at your pricing, your offer, your sales process - and I’ll show you what’s broken and how we’d fix it.


If it makes sense, I’ll tell you all about the program including pricing and everything thats included.


If not, no pressure. But either way, you’ll leave with some tips, and some advice on what we’d do if we were in your shoes.


If I’m wrong, you’ll lose 30 minutes of your day.


If I’m right? Well you’ll join the 2,000+ other happy MSPs who are scaling predictably and building the businesses they set out to do when they started.


So hit the button below, pick a time, and let’s talk.”


Use this format for writing high-converting final CTAs across any VSL:


                                    1. Reopen the pain loop (in 1 sentence)
                                    1. “If you're still stuck with [undesirable status quo]…”

                                       2. Re-state the transformation promise
                                       1. “…and you want to [insert main desire/outcome]…”

                                          3. Tie it directly to your mechanism/offer
                                          1. “That’s exactly what we help you do with [product/service]”

                                             4. List what happens on the call (simple, non-threatening)
                                             1. “On the call, we’ll look at [X, Y, Z] and I’ll show you exactly how we’d roll this out for your business.”

                                                5. Add friction-lowering line
                                                1. “If it makes sense, we move forward. If not, no hard feelings. You’ll still walk away with [deliverable].”
                                                6. (Optional) “Stack the deck in your favour”
                                                1. If I’m wrong, you’ll lose 30 minutes of your day. If I’m right? Well you’ll join the 2,000+ other happy MSPs who are scaling predictably and building the businesses they set out to do when they started.

                                                   7. Final CTA
                                                   1. “So hit the button, pick a time, and let’s talk.”
