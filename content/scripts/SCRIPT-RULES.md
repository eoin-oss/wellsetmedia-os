# SCRIPT RULES — the law for every personal-brand script

Standing rules. Added 2026-09-22. These sit **on top of** the locked 5-phase retention framework,
they don't replace it. Framework = the shape. This doc = the language.

> **7 Oct 2026:** every script also runs through `../../brand/COPY-RULES.md` (the copy rules pack): zero em dashes, no foil framing, three-test on every line.

---

## 1 · Before writing — inputs required

Don't start without these four. If any is missing, ask (max 3 questions), then write:

- **The angle** — the point of view, not the topic
- **Who it's for**
- **Length + format** — e.g. 45s talking head, B-roll heavy, vertical/landscape
- **Raw material** — real stories, numbers, names, places, moments from Eoin's own life

Voice reference: `../../brand/POSITIONING.md`, `../../internal/brand-narrative.md`,
`~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`. Read them first and match that voice.

---

## 2 · Banned patterns — never use these

- **Crowd claims** — "everybody is…", "most people don't realise…", "nobody talks about…"
- **Fake contrast** — "it's not about X, it's about Y", "X isn't the problem, Y is"
- **Hype openers** — "here's the thing", "let's dive in", "this changes everything", "let me tell you"
- **Rhetorical questions** — "ever wondered why…?", "what if I told you…?"
- **Abstract filler** — mindset, journey, game-changer, unlock, level up, leverage, landscape,
  navigate, elevate
- **Neat triplets** — "faster, smarter, better"
- **Cliché closers** — "and that's the secret", "trust me", "let that sink in"
- **Em dashes as a crutch**

Plus the standing bans: never "quick 15" in any form. No agency-speak, no fake urgency, no glazing.

---

## 3 · The rules

1. **Specific beats general.** Every claim needs a concrete anchor — a number, a name, a place,
   a date, a first-hand moment.
2. **Never invent specifics.** Missing a real detail → write `[NEED: a real number here]`.
   Fabricating one is worse than leaving the gap.
3. **Write for the ear.** Short sentences. Contractions. How he'd actually say it out loud.
4. **The hook is a specific moment or claim** — never a question on the page, never a crowd
   statement. (This does not contradict the framework: the hook still *opens* a question in the
   viewer's head. It states something specific enough that they need the rest to resolve it.)
5. **One idea per script.** Cut anything that doesn't serve it.
6. **No names from the research** (Eoin, 25 Oct 2026: "it cant be referencing these random guys off of some blog
   post if no one knows who it is. keep the names out and just develop my point"). Don't name or quote the people,
   journalists, academics, execs or outlets behind a fact unless his audience already knows them. Well-known brands
   are fine as examples. Put the evidence in his words ("their own team admitted it cost them"), never "X from Y
   said". The research backs his point; it is never the point. The "name" in rule 1 means names from his own world
   (a client, a place, his own moment), not a source.

## 4 · Self-audit — run this before showing him anything

Go line by line. For each line: **"Could this appear in anyone else's script?"**
If yes → rewrite it with a specific, or cut it. Then scan the whole draft for every banned
pattern in §2. Do the audit silently; show the clean draft.

---

## 5 · Output format

1. The script, with `[VISUAL]` cues on their own lines where useful
2. A short list of every `[NEED:]` placeholder he has to fill
3. Two alternative hooks

No preamble, no explaining the choices unless he asks.

## 6 · Story structure — the layer underneath the framework

Source: Notion → Learning Lab → Notes → **Storytelling** (23/09/26). This is the *why* under the
five phases, not a replacement for them. They map cleanly:

| Retention framework | Story structure |
|---|---|
| Hook | Hook |
| Context | Setup — character, world or question |
| Conflict | Conflict — escalate rapidly |
| Turn + Payoff | Resolution — transformation or revelation |
| Echo | Emotional echo — let the feeling linger |

### The two things this adds

**1 · The midpoint: want → need.** The story turns when the character stops chasing what they
*wanted* and finds what they actually *needed*. Pinpoint that moment before writing a single
scene. It's usually a failure or an awakening — a truth that recontextualises everything before it.

**2 · Define the lingering emotion first.** What should still be in the room after the last frame?
Decide that before any scene gets written, not after.

### The one-sentence test

Write a one-sentence summary of each act. **If it can't be said in one sentence, the structure
isn't finished.** Run this before writing, not after.

### On endings

- External resolution alone leaves the audience empty. The surface conflict closing is not the
  ending — **the internal change is.**
- Return to the opening theme. Circle back to the question or image from act one.
- The strongest endings leave people *changed, not comfortable*. Understanding, not easy answers.

This is also why the loop works: a **circular story** ends where it began, but the perspective has
changed. First watch the hook is a question; on the loop it's the conclusion.

### On hooks

The opening seconds decide whether someone invests or leaves.

- **Create curiosity, not confusion.** Those fail in opposite directions — a confusing hook loses
  them, a curious one holds them.
- Establish the emotional tone immediately.
- Hint at the central question or conflict. Don't answer it.
- The hook is a specific moment or claim (§3.4) — and it must open something the rest closes.

*Reference hook from the notes: "a filmmaker wakes up and deleted every file he ever shot.
Everything."*

**Known weakness, flagged by Eoin 24/09/26:** his hooks are consistently the weak point across his
Instagram. Dedicated work on this is parked, not done.

## 7 · Still true from the framework

- Five phases: **hook → context → conflict → turn → payoff → echo**, and it must loop.
- Conflict is the tension, never the realisation. The realisation is the payoff.
- First person throughout, max one "you" line. Context is one working line, never a bio,
  never his age.
- On-screen text under 8 words a line. No CTA on tape — captions only.
- Shot lists are grouped **by setup**, across scripts, and every shot names the line it serves.
- One video at a time. Never batch-write. Nothing goes to the Notion Content Pipeline until he
  says lock it.
