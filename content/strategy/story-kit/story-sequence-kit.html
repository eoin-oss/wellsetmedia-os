01

Why they tap through

People don't skip your stories because they're bad. They skip because nothing tells them to stay.

Look at most founder stories. Frame one is about you: your news, your launch, your week. Nothing on frame two gives them a reason to see frame three. And every story ends on the same "DM me", so people learn to tap past it.

Stories are the one place you reach the people who already follow you, every day. They're warm. They're watching. They just aren't being given a reason to reply.

The fix isn't posting more. It's the order.

02

The four jobs

Every sequence does four jobs, in this order. A job can take more than one frame. My Monday story below is eight frames. The order never changes.

The rules that keep it working

One thought per box

One to five boxes a frame. If a box needs two lines on a phone, it's two thoughts.

End on "..."

Every frame but the last ends on "..." so the thought isn't finished. That's what makes them tap forward.

Red for the pain

The word that stings goes red. The hook line can sit in a full red box. One bright box per frame, max.

Real photos only

Your own snaps, never stock. Text in the empty space, the sky or the floor, never across a face.

Stay in the safe area

Keep text away from the top and bottom of the screen, where your name and the reply bar sit.

Proof is real or it's out

Every number, result and screenshot is real, dated and OK to show. Keep them in one place so you never have to invent one.

Scarcity only if it's true

"Two spots left" when there are ten is how people stop believing you.

Fight ideas, not people

Be controversial about an idea. Never knock another creator to make your point.

03

Four types, three a week

Every sequence is one type. Two can mix. Rotate them so your stories never feel the same twice.

Documentation

Your week. Loose, few boxes, real moments. This is how people get to know you, and it's the one that needs the least work.

Ends on: nothing. No ask.

Wins

Results. A client's message, a number that moved, a before and after. Their words beat yours every time.

Ends on: your offer keyword, if this is your one ask of the week.

Value

A process, a breakdown, a step by step, or a free thing you actually use. They should feel they got something even if they never reply.

Ends on: a keyword to get the free thing.

Limiting belief

Open on the objection in their words. Agree with it. Then show the reality, the proof, and the way through.

Ends on: your offer keyword.

Offer keyword

Only one story a week asks for the sale. The Wednesday keyword hands out something free, so it reads as a gift, not a pitch. Ask on every story and people stop reading.

Use a keyword, not "DM me". "Reply STORY" is one word, easy to type, and tells you exactly which story worked. Pick a different word for each ask so the same line never shows up two days running.

04

Hooks and amplifiers

The hook stops them on frame one. The amplifier is the reason to keep tapping. It isn't a second hook. It's the bit that makes them think "ok, this is real". Pick one of each.

Six hooks for frame one

Fourteen amplifiers for frame two or three

Never later than frame three. By frame five most people are gone.

What counts as value

A toolA resourceAn insightA step by stepA relatable story with a lessonEntertainment

Whatever it is, it has to make them feel something. Information alone gets a nod. A feeling gets a reply.
