01

Goal and offer

Start with the money, not the content. Content is how people find you. The offer is why any of it matters. If you can't say what you sell and who buys it in one line, every post after this gets harder.

02

Your ICP: the person who pays

Your ideal client profile is the person with the problem and the money to fix it. Not everyone who watches. Write them in their words, not yours. The words you hear on calls and in DMs are the words that go in your hooks.

Assume first. Then check every answer against real conversations.

Your questions bank

Every real question from a call, a DM, a comment or an onboarding goes here. This is where your middle of funnel ideas come from. Add to it every week, forever.

Questions to ask your best clients

Ask your three best clients these, word for word. Write down what they say, not what you hoped they'd say. The first four tell you about your dream follower. The rest tell you about your ICP.

    03

    Your dream follower

    Your dream follower is who you want watching. Your ICP is who pays. They're not the same person. The dream follower relates to you: your story, your taste, what you believe. Your ICP lives inside that crowd.

    Talk only to buyers and you only reach people already shopping. Talk to everyone and nobody buys. So top of funnel posts talk to the dream follower and grow the account. Middle of funnel posts talk to the ICP and sell.

    04

    Your story beats and proof

    Your top of funnel runs on your story. Write the beats down once and you'll never stare at a blank page again. Proof is anything real that backs a claim: a number, a client message, a screenshot, behind the scenes.

    One rule. Nothing goes in a post that isn't in the proof bank.

    Proof bank

    One line per item. Date, what it is, where it came from, and whether you're allowed to show it. If it has someone else's face, name or message in it, get their OK first.

    05

    Pillars

    Pillars are the five or so subjects you keep coming back to. They stop you starting from zero every week, and they teach people what you're about. Every pillar should show up about three times a month.

    TOFTop of funnel · reach

    Talks to the dream follower. Your story, your beliefs, what they want and what they fear. Most of your posts live here. This is how the account grows.

    MOFMiddle of funnel · trust

    Talks to the ICP. Their pains in their words, your proof, how you think, free value. This is where a follower starts to think "I need this".

    BOFBottom of funnel · the sale

    Happens in stories and DMs, not in posts. A keyword, a reply, a real conversation, then the call. Posts load it. Stories and DMs close it.

    TOF pillars: for the dream follower

    Each one should hit something from stage 3: a want, a fear, or their tension.

    MOF pillars: for the ICP

    Each one should name a pain from stage 2, the way they'd say it.

    BOF: how the sale happens

    06

    Hooks and amplifiers

    The hook stops the scroll. The amplifier is the reason to keep watching, or keep tapping. It isn't a second hook. It's the thing that makes them think "ok, this is real". Tap the ones that suit you to build your kit. Your kit shows up in the story builder and on your plan.

    07

    The idea multiplier

    You don't need 30 ideas. You need about five good ones, multiplied. Each seed becomes four rephrases, three reframes and a few formats. That's a month.

    1. Steal like an artistFind outliers: posts doing three times an account's normal views, from big and small accounts in your space and next to it. Take the idea, never the wording.
    2. Rephrase ×4Same idea, said four different ways. Different first line, different example.
    3. Reframe ×3New angle on the same idea. Flip it, zoom in, tell it as a story, argue the other side.
    4. ReformatPut it in a shape that already works: talking head, day in the life, POV, how-to, story time, a real call.

    Seeds come from three places: outliers you found, questions from your bank in stage 2, and your story beats in stage 4. Tick Pick on the versions you'd actually film. Picked ideas fill your calendar in stage 12.

    08

    The weekly rhythm

    Same week, every week. Your followers learn when to expect you and you stop deciding what to post each morning. This is filled in with the rhythm we run. Change anything that doesn't fit your life.

    Content day, in order

    Don't skip a step. Each one feeds the next.

    1. Research30 min. Outliers, new questions for the bank, new proof.
    2. Multiply45 min. Five seeds, multiplied. Pick 7 to 10 for the week.
    3. ScriptsOne at a time. Hook, one line of context, the tension, the payoff.
    4. Shot listBy setup, not by script. Same setup, film it all, then move.
    5. FilmEverything in one go. Snap photos for your stories as you work.
    6. EditThen check it again later with fresh eyes before it goes out.
    7. StoriesPlan the week's three sequences from the day's snaps.
    09

    Stories

    Posts get attention. Stories are where people reply. They're the one place you reach your followers without them having to sit through a video, so this is where the asking happens. Same shape as a good reel: hook, amplifier, value, then an ask only when it's the asking day.

    Documentation

    Your week, loose. Few words. No ask. People buy from people they've watched for a while.

    Wins

    Real results from your proof bank. A client message, a number, a before and after.

    Value

    A breakdown, a process, a free drop. Your weekly free value goes here with its own keyword.

    Limiting belief

    What your buyer tells themselves. Then the reality. Then proof. Then the ask.

    Three a week, rotating. Run the same ask every day and people stop seeing it. Not every sequence ends on an ask. Two types can mix in one sequence.

    Build one sequence, frame by frame

    One line per box. Start a line with ! to make it the bright box: one per frame, for the line that has to land. Every frame but the last ends on "..." automatically.

    10

    The weekly free value drop

    One a week, every week. Give away something real you built and actually use: a template, a checklist, a breakdown, a system. It's the fastest way to prove you know what you're doing without saying it.

    Strip anything private first. Make sure they can actually use it without you. Keep the thing you sell back; the drop shows how you think, the offer is you doing it for them.

    11

    Measurement

    Log the numbers once a week. Don't change the plan when a post flops. One week is noise. Thirty days is a pattern. Change things at the month-end review, not before.
